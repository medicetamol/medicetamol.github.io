# Complete Change in Protein Production

A **frameshift mutation** (insertion or deletion of a number of nucleotides not divisible by 3) shifts the **reading frame** of all downstream codons. An insertion at the **4th position** of a 900-nucleotide mRNA alters nearly every codon after the first, giving an entirely different amino acid sequence, and usually an early **stop codon**.

## Trigger Point

> **Insertion** of a single nucleotide at the **4th** position = frameshift near the start.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. No change in the final protein**
Only silent point mutations leave the protein unchanged.
A frameshift alters the codons.

**B. Partial protein production**
A truncated protein would be expected if the change were near the 3' end.
Here it occurs near the beginning, so almost the whole sequence changes.

**D. No change due to RNA editing**
RNA editing is a specific, limited process (e.g. APOB-48).
It does not correct frameshifts.

## Mind Capsule

> Frameshift near the 5' end = **maximum damage**; in-frame insertion or deletion (multiple of 3) preserves the frame.
