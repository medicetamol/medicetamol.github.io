# Biliary Obstruction

In **obstructive jaundice**, absence of bile in the gut impairs the absorption of **fat-soluble vitamins**, including **vitamin K**. This causes a prolonged PT and bleeding, which **corrects with parenteral vitamin K**. In hepatocellular failure, synthesis of clotting factors is impaired, so there is no response to vitamin K.

## Trigger Point

> Jaundice + bleeding that **normalised after vitamin K injection**.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency**
Causes liver cirrhosis and emphysema.
The coagulopathy is from synthetic failure, not vitamin K malabsorption.

**C. Autoimmune hepatitis**
Hepatocellular injury with impaired factor synthesis.
Vitamin K does not correct the coagulopathy.

**D. Factor XI deficiency**
A haemophilia C-type disorder with mild bleeding.
It is not associated with jaundice or vitamin K response.

## Mind Capsule

> Vitamin K-responsive coagulopathy = **cholestasis / malabsorption**; not corrected by vitamin K = hepatocellular failure.
