# Type IIa

**Type IIa (familial hypercholesterolaemia)** has a raised **LDL** with **normal triglycerides**, tendon and subcutaneous xanthomas, and a positive family history. **Type IIb** also has raised VLDL, so triglycerides are elevated.

## Trigger Point

> **Raised cholesterol + xanthomas + normal triglycerides** (< 140 mg/dL).

## Fredrickson Classification

| Type | Raised lipoprotein | Triglycerides |
|---|---|---|
| I | Chylomicrons | Very high |
| IIa | LDL | Normal |
| IIb | LDL + VLDL | High |
| III | IDL | High |
| IV | VLDL | High |
| V | VLDL + chylomicrons | Very high |

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Type I**
Chylomicron excess with marked hypertriglyceridaemia and pancreatitis.
Cholesterol is only mildly raised.

**C. Type IIb**
Combined LDL and VLDL elevation, so triglycerides are raised.
Here they are normal.

**D. Type II**
It is the umbrella term and does not specify the subtype.
The normal triglyceride level specifically indicates type IIa.

## Mind Capsule

> IIa = ↑ LDL, **normal TG**; IIb = ↑ LDL + ↑ VLDL, **raised TG**.
