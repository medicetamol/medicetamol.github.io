# Defective Collagen Formation

**Vitamin C (ascorbic acid)** is a cofactor for **prolyl and lysyl hydroxylase**, needed for hydroxylation of proline and lysine in procollagen. Without it, collagen cannot form a stable triple helix, leading to **fragile capillaries, bleeding gums, perifollicular haemorrhages, poor wound healing** and bone changes in scurvy.

## Trigger Point

> **Bleeding gums and easy bruising** with a diagnosis of scurvy.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Low calcium**
Causes tetany or rickets; it is not the cause of scurvy.
Scurvy is a vitamin C deficiency.

**C. Inhibition of clotting factors**
Clotting factors are normal in scurvy.
The bleeding is from vessel fragility, not a coagulopathy.

**D. Increased keratinization of epithelium**
It is seen in vitamin A deficiency.
It has no role in scurvy.

## Mind Capsule

> Scurvy = ↓ hydroxylation of proline and lysine = defective collagen; coagulation tests are normal (positive tourniquet test).
