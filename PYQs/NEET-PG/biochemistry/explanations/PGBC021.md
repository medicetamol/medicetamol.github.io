# Liver Glycogen

After a dinner at 8 PM, glucose at **7 AM (about 11 hours)** is maintained mainly by **liver glycogenolysis** during the early fasting (post-absorptive) state, up to about 12-18 hours. After this, **gluconeogenesis** takes over as glycogen runs out. **Muscle glycogen** cannot release glucose because muscle lacks **glucose-6-phosphatase**.

## Trigger Point

> Blood sugar at **7 AM**, about **11 hours** after dinner = overnight fast.

## Fuel Sources During Fasting

| Duration | Main glucose source |
|---|---|
| 0-4 hours | Dietary glucose |
| 4-16 hours | Liver glycogenolysis |
| >16-18 hours | Gluconeogenesis |

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Muscle glycogen**
It supplies the muscle itself and cannot release free glucose.
Muscle lacks glucose-6-phosphatase.

**C. Gluconeogenesis**
It becomes the major source only after liver glycogen is depleted, beyond about 16-18 hours.
It contributes less at 11 hours.

**D. Dietary carbohydrate**
Glucose from the meal is absorbed and used within 4-6 hours.
It is not the source at 7 AM.

## Mind Capsule

> Overnight fast = **liver glycogenolysis**; prolonged fast (> 18 hours) = gluconeogenesis; starvation (days) = ketones for brain.
