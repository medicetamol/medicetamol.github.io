# Glucocerebrosidase

**Gaucher disease** is the most common lysosomal storage disease, due to **glucocerebrosidase (β-glucosidase)** deficiency. **Glucocerebroside** accumulates in macrophages, giving **Gaucher cells** with a **crumpled tissue paper** cytoplasm. Features are **hepatosplenomegaly, bone pain, Erlenmeyer flask deformity and pancytopenia**.

## Key Finding

> Large pale macrophages with fibrillary, crumpled-paper cytoplasm in the marrow.

## Trigger Point

> Bone pain + **hepatosplenomegaly** + marrow macrophages with crumpled-paper cytoplasm.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Hexosaminidase**
Deficient in Tay-Sachs and Sandhoff disease, with GM2 accumulation.
There is neurodegeneration without organomegaly.

**C. Sphingomyelinase**
Deficient in Niemann-Pick disease, with foamy macrophages.
The cells are vacuolated, not crumpled-paper.

**D. Alpha 1,4-glucosidase**
Deficient in Pompe disease (GSD II), causing cardiomegaly and hypotonia.
It is not associated with Gaucher cells.

## Mind Capsule

> Gaucher = **glucocerebrosidase** deficiency = crumpled tissue paper cells; treated with enzyme replacement (imiglucerase).
