# McArdle Disease

**McArdle disease (GSD type V)** is deficiency of **muscle glycogen phosphorylase**. Muscle glycogen cannot be broken down, so exercise leads to **cramps, fatigue and myoglobinuria**, with **no rise in lactate** during ischaemic exercise. The **second-wind phenomenon** appears after rest as fuel shifts to blood glucose and fatty acids.

## Trigger Point

> Cramps on **exertion**, relief with rest, and **no lactate rise** = muscle glycogen phosphorylase deficiency.

## Glycogen Storage Diseases

| Type | Enzyme | Key feature |
|---|---|---|
| I (von Gierke) | Glucose-6-phosphatase | Severe fasting hypoglycaemia, hepatomegaly, lactic acidosis |
| II (Pompe) | Lysosomal α-1,4-glucosidase | Cardiomegaly, hypotonia |
| III (Cori) | Debranching enzyme | Hypoglycaemia, hepatomegaly, limit dextrin |
| IV (Andersen) | Branching enzyme | Cirrhosis |
| V (McArdle) | Muscle phosphorylase | Exercise cramps, no lactate rise |
| VI (Hers) | Liver phosphorylase | Mild hypoglycaemia |

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Hers disease**
Liver phosphorylase deficiency with mild hepatomegaly and fasting hypoglycaemia.
It has no exercise-related muscle symptoms.

**C. Cori's disease**
Debranching enzyme deficiency, affecting liver and muscle.
Fasting hypoglycaemia and hepatomegaly dominate; lactate rises normally.

**D. Andersen disease**
Branching enzyme deficiency causing abnormal glycogen and progressive liver cirrhosis.
It does not present with exercise-induced cramps.

## Mind Capsule

> Exercise cramps + no lactate rise + myoglobinuria + second wind = **McArdle (type V)**.
> (Hypoglycaemia in the stem is atypical, since muscle glycogen cannot release glucose to blood.)
