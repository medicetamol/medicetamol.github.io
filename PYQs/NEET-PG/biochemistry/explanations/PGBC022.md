# Type II Hyperlipoproteinemia

**Type II (familial hypercholesterolaemia)** is caused by **LDL receptor** defects (or apoB-100 or PCSK9 gain-of-function), leading to raised **LDL cholesterol**, **tendon xanthomas (Achilles)**, xanthelasma, corneal arcus and **premature coronary artery disease**.

## Trigger Point

> **Tendon xanthomas + raised LDL and cholesterol**.

## Fredrickson Classification

| Type | Lipoprotein raised | Feature |
|---|---|---|
| I | Chylomicrons | Pancreatitis, eruptive xanthomas |
| IIa | LDL | Tendon xanthomas |
| IIb | LDL + VLDL | Combined hyperlipidaemia |
| III | IDL (remnants) | Palmar (tuberoeruptive) xanthomas |
| IV | VLDL | Hypertriglyceridaemia |
| V | VLDL + chylomicrons | Pancreatitis |

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Type I**
Lipoprotein lipase or apoC-II deficiency with raised chylomicrons.
It causes eruptive xanthomas and pancreatitis, not tendon xanthomas.

**C. Type III**
Dysbetalipoproteinaemia (apoE2 homozygosity) with palmar crease xanthomas.
It features raised IDL, not LDL.

**D. Abetalipoproteinemia**
MTP deficiency with **absent** apoB-containing lipoproteins and low cholesterol.
LDL is low, not high.

## Mind Capsule

> Tendon xanthomas + ↑ LDL = **type IIa (LDL receptor)**; palmar xanthomas = type III; eruptive xanthomas = types I, V.
