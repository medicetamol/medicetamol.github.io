# Alcohol

**Metronidazole** inhibits **aldehyde dehydrogenase**, so alcohol use causes a **disulfiram-like reaction**: flushing, vomiting, palpitations and headache from acetaldehyde accumulation. Alcohol is avoided during therapy and for 24-48 hours after.

## Trigger Point

> Patient on **metronidazole**, what to avoid.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Ciprofloxacin**
A fluoroquinolone often combined with metronidazole for intra-abdominal infections.
No harmful interaction needs avoiding.

**C. Rifampicin**
An enzyme inducer that can lower metronidazole levels slightly.
It is not contraindicated.

**D. Colchicine**
Used for gout; no disulfiram-type reaction with metronidazole.
There is no major interaction.

## Mind Capsule

> Disulfiram-like reaction: **metronidazole, cefoperazone, cefotetan, procarbazine**, griseofulvin, chlorpropamide.
