# Increased Calcium Excretion

**Furosemide** blocks the **Na⁺-K⁺-2Cl⁻ cotransporter (NKCC2)** in the thick ascending limb. This abolishes the lumen-positive potential that drives paracellular **Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺** reabsorption, so **urinary calcium excretion rises**. The resulting **hypercalciuria** predisposes to **calcium stones and nephrocalcinosis**, especially in premature infants on long-term loop diuretics.

## Trigger Point

> **Furosemide** started, then **renal stones** develop.

## Loop vs Thiazide Diuretics

| Feature | Loop diuretics | Thiazides |
|---|---|---|
| Site | Thick ascending limb (NKCC2) | Distal convoluted tubule (NCC) |
| Urinary Ca²⁺ | **Increased** | Decreased |
| Use in Ca disorders | Hypercalcaemia | Calcium stone prevention |

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Decreased calcium excretion**
This is the effect of **thiazides**, which enhance distal Ca²⁺ reabsorption and protect against stones.
Furosemide does the opposite.

**C. Increased oxalate absorption**
Seen in enteric hyperoxaluria, e.g. fat malabsorption or after bariatric surgery.
It is not a mechanism of loop diuretics.

**D. Decreased citrate excretion**
Low urinary citrate occurs with distal RTA, hypokalaemia and chronic diarrhoea, and is a stone risk factor.
It is not the main loop diuretic mechanism.

## Mind Capsule

> **Loops lose calcium** (hypercalciuria, stones, used in hypercalcaemia); **thiazides take calcium back** (prevent stones).
