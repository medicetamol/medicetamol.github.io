# Atropine Toxicity

**Atropine** blocks muscarinic receptors, so excess causes **anticholinergic toxicity**: **hyperthermia (no sweating), dry flushed skin, mydriasis, tachycardia, delirium and urinary retention**. In organophosphate poisoning, atropine is titrated to **drying of secretions**, not to a fixed dose.

## Trigger Point

> Rise in **temperature** after **atropine infusion** in OP poisoning.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Side effect of pralidoxime**
Adverse effects include dizziness, blurred vision, and muscle weakness with rapid infusion.
Hyperthermia is not a typical effect.

**C. Due to organophosphate poisoning**
OP poisoning gives **cholinergic** features (sweating, salivation, bradycardia, miosis).
It does not typically cause fever.

**D. Idiopathic**
There is a clear drug-related explanation.
It should not be labelled idiopathic.

## Mind Capsule

> Atropine overdose = "hot as a hare, dry as a bone, red as a beet, blind as a bat, mad as a hatter".
