# LABA Plus Inhaled Steroid

Persistent **nocturnal symptoms** despite reliever therapy (salbutamol ± ipratropium) indicate **uncontrolled persistent asthma**. The next step is **controller therapy**: **inhaled corticosteroid (ICS) + long-acting β2 agonist (LABA)**, e.g. budesonide-formoterol.

## Trigger Point

> **Nocturnal exacerbations** on **reliever-only** treatment.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Oral corticosteroids**
Reserved for acute severe exacerbations or severe refractory asthma.
They are not the next step for chronic control.

**C. Montelukast**
A leukotriene antagonist, an alternative controller, but less effective than ICS-based therapy.
It is not first choice for step-up.

**D. Increase salbutamol dose**
More reliever does not control inflammation and risks tachyphylaxis and adverse effects.
It does not prevent nocturnal symptoms.

## Mind Capsule

> Asthma step-up: reliever only → **ICS (± LABA)** controller; **LABA never alone**.
