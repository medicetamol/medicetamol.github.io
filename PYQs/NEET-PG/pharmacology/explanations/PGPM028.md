# Verapamil

**Verapamil** (a non-dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker) slows conduction through the **AV node** and is used for **prophylaxis of PSVT** orally, as well as for acute termination in some cases. **IV adenosine** is the drug of choice for **acute termination** of PSVT but is too short-acting (half-life seconds) for prophylaxis.

## Trigger Point

> **Prophylaxis** of PSVT.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. IV adenosine**
Terminates PSVT by transient AV block, with a half-life of about 10 seconds.
It cannot give prevention.

**B. IV esmolol**
An ultra short-acting IV β-blocker for acute rate control and perioperative use.
It is not used for oral prophylaxis.

**C. Oral phenytoin**
An antiepileptic with limited use in digoxin-induced arrhythmias.
It is not used for PSVT prophylaxis.

## Mind Capsule

> PSVT: **acute = adenosine** (or vagal manoeuvres); **prophylaxis = verapamil, diltiazem, β-blockers**.
