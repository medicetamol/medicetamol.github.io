# Enhances Fibrin Degradation

**Tissue plasminogen activator (alteplase, tPA)** converts **plasminogen to plasmin**, which **degrades fibrin** in clots (fibrinolysis). It is fibrin-selective and is used in acute ischaemic stroke, MI and pulmonary embolism. Main risk is bleeding.

## Trigger Point

> **Mechanism of action** of tPA.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Inhibit extrinsic pathway**
This would be the effect of warfarin (reducing factor VII synthesis) or direct inhibitors.
tPA does not inhibit coagulation.

**B. Inhibits platelet aggregation**
This describes aspirin, clopidogrel and GP IIb/IIIa inhibitors.
tPA is not antiplatelet.

**D. Inhibit clot formation**
Anticoagulants such as heparin prevent clot formation.
tPA dissolves already formed clots.

## Mind Capsule

> Thrombolytics (alteplase, streptokinase, tenecteplase) = plasminogen → plasmin = **fibrinolysis**; antidote for bleeding: tranexamic acid.
