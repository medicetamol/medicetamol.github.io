# Tardive Dyskinesia: Valbenazine

**Tardive dyskinesia** follows **chronic dopamine blockade** (e.g. haloperidol for years) and presents with **orofacial dyskinesia**. It is treated with **VMAT2 inhibitors (valbenazine, deutetrabenazine)** and by switching to a drug like clozapine.

## Trigger Point

> **Haloperidol for 2 years** + **orofacial dyskinesia**.

## Antipsychotic Movement Disorders

| Disorder | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Acute dystonia | Anticholinergic (benztropine) or promethazine |
| Akathisia | Propranolol, benzodiazepines |
| Parkinsonism | Anticholinergics, amantadine |
| Tardive dyskinesia | VMAT2 inhibitors |

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Acute dystonia with ropinirole**
Acute dystonia is treated with **anticholinergics**.
Ropinirole, a dopamine agonist, would worsen psychosis.

**C. Akathisia with propranolol**
Propranolol is the right drug for akathisia, but the stem's orofacial dyskinesia after 2 years points to tardive dyskinesia.
It does not match the clinical picture.

**D. Parkinsonism with levodopa**
Levodopa can worsen psychosis and is ineffective against drug-induced parkinsonism.
Anticholinergics are used instead.

## Mind Capsule

> Late, orofacial, chronic = **TD → VMAT2 inhibitor**; early, acute spasm = dystonia → anticholinergic.
