# Immediate Fasciotomy

**Acute compartment syndrome**, commonly after a tibial shaft fracture: raised pressure in a closed fascial compartment compromises perfusion. Pain **out of proportion** and **pain on passive stretch** are the key signs; **pulses are often present** because pulselessness is late. After removing tight casts or dressings, proceed to **urgent fasciotomy**. Loss of sensation in the first web space points to the deep peroneal nerve (anterior compartment).

## Clinical Features

| Feature | Remark |
|---|---|
| Pain out of proportion | Early |
| Pain on passive stretch | Earliest and most reliable |
| Paraesthesia | Early (nerve ischaemia) |
| Paralysis, pallor, pulselessness | Late |

## Trigger Point

> Tibial fracture, **pain out of proportion**, pain on **passive dorsiflexion**, unrelieved by analgesics, with **no sensation over the first web space**.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Elevate the limb and observe**
Delay risks irreversible muscle necrosis. Elevation above the heart also lowers perfusion.

**C. Opioids and observation**
Masks symptoms and delays decompression.

**D. Apply cast and follow up**
A cast adds compression and worsens the syndrome.

## Mind Capsule

> Fracture + pain out of proportion + pain on passive stretch = compartment syndrome → fasciotomy, even if pulses are present.

## Memory Hook

> **5 Ps**: Pain, Paraesthesia, Pallor, Paralysis, Pulselessness; pain comes first, pulselessness last.
