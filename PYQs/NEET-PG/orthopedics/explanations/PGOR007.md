# Posterior Dislocation of Hip

The commonest hip dislocation, from a force along the femur (dashboard injury, fall). The femoral head lies behind the acetabulum, so the limb is **shortened, flexed, adducted and internally rotated**. The **sciatic nerve** is at risk, and early reduction (within about 6 hours) lowers the risk of AVN.

## Trigger Point

> Fall from a bicycle with hip pain, **shortening**, and **flexion, adduction, internal rotation** of the hip.

## Limb Attitude in Hip Injuries

| Injury | Attitude |
|---|---|
| Posterior dislocation | Shortened, flexed, adducted, internally rotated |
| Anterior dislocation | Flexed, abducted, externally rotated |
| Femoral neck or intertrochanteric fracture | Shortened, externally rotated |

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Anterior dislocation**
Abduction with external rotation.

**B. Transcervical fracture**
Shortening with external rotation, not internal rotation.

**D. IT Fracture**
Shortening with marked external rotation.

## Mind Capsule

> Hip: shortened, flexed, adducted, internally rotated = posterior dislocation.

## Memory Hook

> **FAIR**: Flexed, Adducted, Internally Rotated (and shortened) means posterior dislocation.
