# Fat Embolism Syndrome

After **long-bone fractures** (femur, tibia) or intramedullary nailing, marrow fat enters the circulation. At **24-72 hours** it causes **hypoxia and breathlessness, confusion and a petechial rash** over the chest, axilla and conjunctivae. Treatment is supportive, and early fracture fixation helps prevent it.

## Gurd's Criteria

| Type | Features |
|---|---|
| Major | Petechial rash, respiratory distress, cerebral involvement |
| Minor | Tachycardia, fever, retinal changes, jaundice, renal changes, anaemia, thrombocytopenia, ↑ ESR |

## Trigger Point

> Femur and tibia fractures, then **2 days later** breathlessness, tachycardia and a **petechial rash on the chest and conjunctiva**.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Disseminated intravascular coagulation**
Bleeding diathesis with low platelets and prolonged clotting times; it lacks the fracture-linked triad.

**C. Pulmonary embolism**
Sudden dyspnoea and pleuritic pain from a venous thrombus, with no petechial rash or confusion.

**D. Sepsis**
Fever and hypotension from an infective source.

## Mind Capsule

> Long-bone fracture + 24-72 h + hypoxia + confusion + petechiae = Fat embolism syndrome.
