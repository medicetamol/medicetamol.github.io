# Giant Cell Tumor

A tumour of skeletally mature adults (20-40 years) arising in the **epiphysis**, extending from the metaphysis to the subarticular surface. It is **eccentric and lytic** with a "soap-bubble" look and no sclerotic rim. It most often involves the distal femur, proximal tibia and distal radius.

## Trigger Point

> 22-year-old woman with a well-defined **epiphyseal** lesion at the knee.

## Bone Tumours by Site

| Site | Tumours |
|---|---|
| Epiphysis | Chondroblastoma, giant cell tumour, clear cell chondrosarcoma |
| Metaphysis | Osteosarcoma, osteochondroma, NOF, ABC |
| Diaphysis | Ewing's sarcoma, osteoid osteoma, myeloma |

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Chondroblastoma**
Epiphyseal tumour of adolescents with an open physis (under 20 years).

**B. Osteochondroma**
A cartilage-capped metaphyseal outgrowth that points away from the joint.

**D. Aneurysmal bone cyst**
An expansile, eccentric lytic lesion with fluid-fluid levels, usually metaphyseal, in patients under 20.

## Mind Capsule

> Epiphyseal-metaphyseal lesion at the knee in a 20-40 year old = Giant cell tumour.

## Memory Hook

> **CGC**: Chondroblastoma, Giant cell tumour, Clear cell chondrosarcoma are the epiphyseal tumours.
