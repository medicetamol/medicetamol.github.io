# Teriparatide Before Bisphosphonates

This is the **false statement**. In postmenopausal osteoporosis (a Colles' fracture is a fragility-fracture clue), **bisphosphonates** are first-line. **Teriparatide**, an anabolic PTH analogue, is reserved for severe or refractory disease and is followed by an antiresorptive to keep the gains. Sequencing is guideline-dependent.

## Osteoporosis Drugs

| Drug | Role |
|---|---|
| Bisphosphonates | Antiresorptive, first-line |
| Denosumab | RANKL antibody, antiresorptive |
| Teriparatide | Anabolic PTH analogue, severe osteoporosis, up to 2 years |
| Romosozumab | Sclerostin inhibitor (anabolic and antiresorptive) |

## Trigger Point

> 60-year-old woman with a previous **Colles' fracture** and backache, and the question asks for the **wrong** statement.

## Why the Others Are True

**B. Bisphosphonates not given for more than a year**
Treated as acceptable by the key. Therapy is usually reassessed at 3-5 years, so the strict "one year" wording is not standard.

**C. Calcium requirement is 1200 mg per day**
True for postmenopausal women.

**D. Oral vit D3 along with calcium**
True; calcium and vitamin D are standard adjuncts to every osteoporosis drug.

## Mind Capsule

> Osteoporosis = bisphosphonate first (with calcium 1200 mg + vitamin D).
> Teriparatide is only for severe cases and is followed by an antiresorptive.
