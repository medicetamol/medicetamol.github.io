# Bipartite Patella (X-ray the Other Knee)

A **normal variant** where the superolateral secondary ossification centre fails to fuse with the patella. The fragment has smooth, **well-corticated** margins and is often an incidental finding, frequently **bilateral**, so the opposite knee is X-rayed. No fixation is needed.

## Trigger Point

> Chronic knee pain **without trauma**, with a separate rounded fragment of **smooth, well-corticated** margins at the upper pole of the patella.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Patellar avulsion fracture, TBW**
Avulsions follow trauma and have sharp, non-corticated edges. Tension band wiring is for displaced transverse fractures.

**C. Upper pole fracture, cylindrical cast**
A fracture needs a history of trauma and sharp fracture lines.

**D. Avulsion fracture, interfragmentary screw**
The same problem: no trauma, and a corticated fragment indicates a normal variant.

## Mind Capsule

> Smooth corticated superolateral fragment + no trauma = Bipartite patella; image the other knee.
