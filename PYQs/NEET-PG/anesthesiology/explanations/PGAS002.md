# Head Tilt, Chin Lift

Opens the airway by lifting the tongue off the posterior pharyngeal wall: one hand on the forehead **tilts the head back**, the fingers of the other hand **lift the bony chin**. Avoid it if a **cervical spine injury** is suspected; use jaw thrust instead.

## Trigger Point

> Airway manoeuvre with the head and neck positioned to maintain airway patency (forehead tilt with chin elevation).

## Airway Manoeuvres

| Manoeuvre | Use |
|---|---|
| Head tilt-chin lift | Opening the airway when no cervical injury is suspected |
| Jaw thrust | Suspected cervical spine injury (trauma) |
| In-line manual stabilisation | Holds the head neutral during airway interventions in trauma |

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Jaw thrust**
Both hands push the mandible forward at its angles, without head extension. Preferred in trauma.

**C. In line manual stabilization**
Hands hold the head neutral at the sides to protect the cervical spine. There is no head tilt.

**D. Head stabilization**
Holding the head still, not an airway-opening manoeuvre.

## Mind Capsule

> No neck injury = head tilt-chin lift.
> Suspected C-spine injury = jaw thrust.
