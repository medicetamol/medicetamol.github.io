# CPR with 20% Intralipid

Bupivacaine is highly lipophilic and blocks cardiac sodium channels, so toxicity causes seizures and **cardiovascular collapse (LAST)**. Treatment is airway support, **CPR and 20% lipid emulsion**, with reduced-dose adrenaline (avoid vasopressin).

## Drug Facts

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| 20% lipid emulsion | About 1.5 mL/kg bolus, then 0.25 mL/kg/min infusion (repeat bolus if collapse persists) |
| Adrenaline | Small doses only (≤ 1 µg/kg) |
| Avoid | Vasopressin, calcium channel blockers, β-blockers, more local anaesthetic |

## Trigger Point

> Regional block with **0.25% bupivacaine**, then **unresponsive with unrecordable pulse**.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. CPCR with sod. Bicarbonate**
Useful for sodium-channel blocker poisoning in some settings, but it does not reverse local anaesthetic toxicity.

**C. CPCR with dobutamine**
An inotrope with arrhythmia risk. It is not the specific antidote.

**D. CPCR with calcium**
For hyperkalaemia or calcium channel blocker toxicity; no role in LAST.

## Mind Capsule

> Local anaesthetic cardiac arrest = CPR + 20% lipid emulsion.
