# Dantrolene

**Malignant hyperthermia** after a volatile agent and succinylcholine: rigidity, tachycardia, **rising EtCO₂** and later hyperthermia. Stop the triggers, hyperventilate with 100% O₂ and give **IV dantrolene**, a ryanodine receptor (RyR1) blocker that reduces calcium release from the sarcoplasmic reticulum.

## Drug Facts

| Drug | Dose | Key adverse effect |
|---|---|---|
| Dantrolene | 2.5 mg/kg IV bolus, repeated until controlled | Muscle weakness; hepatotoxicity with chronic oral use |

## Trigger Point

> Rigidity, rapid ↑ temperature and tachycardia after **sevoflurane and succinylcholine**, with **rising EtCO₂**.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Diazepam**
A benzodiazepine that may calm the patient but does not stop the hypermetabolic crisis.

**C. Pancuronium**
A non-depolarising blocker acting at the neuromuscular junction. The rigidity here comes from calcium release inside the muscle cell, so it does not treat the crisis.

**D. Vecuronium**
Also a non-depolarising blocker, with the same limitation: it does not stop intracellular calcium release.

## Mind Capsule

> Volatile agent or succinylcholine + rigidity + rising EtCO₂ = malignant hyperthermia → dantrolene.
