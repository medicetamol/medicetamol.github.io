# Squamous Cell Carcinoma

**Squamous cell carcinoma of the lung** is strongly linked to **smoking**, arises centrally, and secretes **PTHrP**, causing **hypercalcaemia**. Histology shows **keratin pearls and intercellular bridges** with large atypical cells and hyperchromasia.

## Trigger Point

> Smoker + cough + weight loss + **raised calcium** + large atypical hyperchromatic cells.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Large cell neuroendocrine tumour**
Large cells with neuroendocrine features; hypercalcaemia is not typical.
It lacks keratinization.

**B. Small cell carcinoma**
Small cells with scant cytoplasm and **nuclear moulding**; produces ACTH or ADH.
It is not the typical cause of hypercalcaemia.

**C. Adenocarcinoma**
The commonest lung cancer, **peripheral**, with gland formation and mucin; linked to non-smokers too.
It is not typically the cause of PTHrP-mediated hypercalcaemia.

## Mind Capsule

> Lung **S**CC = **S**moking, **S**quamous, PTHrP hypercalcaemia (**S**erum calcium up), central, cavitating.
