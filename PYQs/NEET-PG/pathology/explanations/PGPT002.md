# Osmotic Fragility Test: Hereditary Spherocytosis

**Hereditary spherocytosis (HS)** is an autosomal dominant defect of **spectrin, ankyrin or band 3**. RBCs lose membrane and become **spherocytes** with no central pallor, raised **MCHC**, and early splenic destruction. The child has **jaundice, splenomegaly and a positive family history**. Diagnosis is by **osmotic fragility test** (increased fragility) or **EMA binding (flow cytometry)**.

## Key Finding

> Many small round, dense RBCs with loss of central pallor = spherocytes.

## Trigger Point

> Child + intermittent jaundice + splenomegaly + **similar illness in the elder brother**.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Coombs test, AIHA**
Autoimmune haemolytic anaemia also shows spherocytes, but there is no family history.
Coombs would be positive in AIHA and negative in HS.

**C. G6PD deficiency, genetic testing**
X-linked, with **bite cells and Heinz bodies** and haemolysis after drugs or fava beans.
Spherocytes are not the main finding.

**D. PNH, flow cytometry**
Acquired disorder with haemoglobinuria and loss of CD55 and CD59.
It is not familial and shows no spherocytes.

## Mind Capsule

> Spherocytes + ↑ MCHC + family history + splenomegaly = **HS**; osmotic fragility ↑; treated with splenectomy.
