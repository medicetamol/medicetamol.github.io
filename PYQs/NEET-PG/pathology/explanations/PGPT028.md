# Flow Cytometry

**Flow cytometry** uses fluorescent-labelled antibodies to detect **CD markers** on individual cells in suspension, and **quantifies** the percentage of cells carrying each marker. It is the standard test for immunophenotyping leukaemias, lymphomas and **CD4 counts** in HIV.

## Trigger Point

> Technique to **compare and quantify CD markers on cells**.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. ELISA**
Measures soluble antigens or antibodies in serum, not cell surface markers.
It cannot count cells.

**B. Western blot**
Detects specific proteins in a lysate after electrophoresis.
It does not quantify cell surface markers on individual cells.

**D. Immunohistochemistry**
Detects markers on tissue sections and is mostly qualitative or semi-quantitative.
It is not as quantitative as flow cytometry.

## Mind Capsule

> CD markers on cells in suspension = **flow cytometry**; tissue sections = IHC; soluble proteins = ELISA.
