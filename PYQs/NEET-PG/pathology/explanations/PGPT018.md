# Rheumatic Heart Disease

**Aschoff bodies** are the pathognomonic granulomas of **rheumatic carditis**. Each has a **central area of fibrinoid necrosis** surrounded by **Anitschkow cells** (caterpillar nuclei), **Aschoff giant cells** and lymphocytes. They are found in the myocardium, in the interstitium near vessels.

## Key Finding

> Cardiac muscle with a pale central area surrounded by inflammatory cells = Aschoff body.

## Trigger Point

> Central pale necrosis with surrounding inflammatory cells in the myocardium.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Infective endocarditis**
Shows friable vegetations with bacteria, fibrin and neutrophils on the valve.
It does not form Aschoff bodies.

**C. SABE**
Subacute bacterial endocarditis is a type of infective endocarditis on damaged valves.
It shows vegetations, not granulomas in the myocardium.

**D. Cardiac failure**
A clinical syndrome, with changes such as heart failure cells in the lungs.
It does not give a histological Aschoff lesion.

## Mind Capsule

> Aschoff body + Anitschkow (caterpillar) cells = **acute rheumatic carditis**; MacCallum plaques in the left atrium.
