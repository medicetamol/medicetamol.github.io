# Seminoma

**Seminoma** is the commonest testicular germ cell tumour in young men. It is a **well-circumscribed, homogeneous, non-haemorrhagic, pale** mass. Histology shows **uniform round cells with clear cytoplasm, distinct cell borders** and a **lymphocytic infiltrate** (with possible granulomas). It is highly radiosensitive.

## Key Finding

> Sheets of uniform cells with clear cytoplasm, distinct borders and scattered lymphocytes.

## Trigger Point

> 30-year-old with painless testicular mass, **homogeneous, non-haemorrhagic** on ultrasound.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Yolk sac tumour**
Shows **Schiller-Duval bodies** and raised AFP, mostly in infants and children.
It is not a homogeneous clear cell tumour.

**B. Choriocarcinoma**
**Haemorrhagic** tumour with syncytiotrophoblast and cytotrophoblast, with raised β-hCG.
The tumour here is non-haemorrhagic.

**C. Teratoma**
Heterogeneous mass with cysts and tissues from all three germ layers.
It would not appear homogeneous.

## Mind Capsule

> Seminoma = **homogeneous, clear cells, lymphocytes, radiosensitive**; choriocarcinoma = haemorrhagic.
