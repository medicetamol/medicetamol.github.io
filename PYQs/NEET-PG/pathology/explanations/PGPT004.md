# Subepithelial Deposits

**Post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis (PSGN)** presents in a child with **cola-coloured urine, hypertension and periorbital puffiness** (nephritic syndrome). Electron microscopy shows **subepithelial "humps"**, and immunofluorescence shows a **granular "lumpy-bumpy"** pattern of IgG and C3. The question key follows this classic EM answer. Note that rapid worsening of creatinine over weeks points to a crescentic transformation, which is rare in children with PSGN.

## Trigger Point

> Child with cola urine, hypertension, puffy eyes = **nephritic syndrome of PSGN**.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Subendothelial deposits**
Characteristic of **type I MPGN** and lupus nephritis (class III/IV).
They are not seen in PSGN.

**C. Crescent formation**
Seen on **light microscopy**, not EM, and reflects RPGN.
The question asks for an EM finding.

**D. Mesangial deposits**
Seen in **IgA nephropathy** and mesangial proliferative GN.
They are not the hallmark of PSGN.

## Mind Capsule

> PSGN = **subepithelial humps** (EM) + lumpy-bumpy IF; low C3 that normalizes in 8 weeks.
