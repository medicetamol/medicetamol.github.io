# Fluid Overload (TACO)

**Transfusion-associated circulatory overload (TACO)** occurs within about 6 hours of transfusion, particularly with rapid or large volumes, with **dyspnoea, chest discomfort, hypertension, raised JVP and pulmonary oedema**. Radiology shows **fissural thickening and bilateral infiltrates**. It responds to **diuretics** and slowing the transfusion.

## Trigger Point

> **4 units** transfused, **2 hours ago**, with chest discomfort and **bilateral fissural thickening**.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. ABO incompatibility**
Causes fever, hypotension, haemoglobinuria and DIC, usually within minutes.
Chest radiograph changes of oedema are not the feature.

**C. Hypocalcaemia**
Occurs with massive transfusion due to citrate, causing tetany and arrhythmias.
It does not give pulmonary fissural thickening.

**D. Graft-versus-host disease**
Occurs **days to weeks** after transfusion in immunocompromised patients, with rash, diarrhoea and pancytopenia.
It does not occur in 2 hours.

## Mind Capsule

> TACO = hypertension, ↑ JVP, responds to diuretics; TRALI = hypotension, non-cardiogenic oedema, no diuretics.
