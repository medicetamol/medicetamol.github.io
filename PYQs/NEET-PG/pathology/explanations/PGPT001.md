# 2/3rd of Mendelian Disorders Are Autosomal Dominant

Among **Mendelian (single-gene) disorders**, the commonest pattern is **autosomal dominant**, accounting for about **two-thirds**. Autosomal recessive disorders form about one-third, and **X-linked** and **Y-linked** disorders are much rarer (Y-linked are the rarest). Percentages are approximate, taken from standard pathology texts.

## Trigger Point

> Which statement is true about the **proportion** of Mendelian modes of inheritance.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Every 10th patient has a Y-linked disorder**
Y-linked disorders are extremely rare (e.g. hairy ears, male infertility genes).
They do not account for 10% of patients.

**B. 1/3rd of Mendelian disorders is autosomal recessive**
This is a rounded figure that is close, but the standard teaching quotes dominant disorders as the major group (2/3).
The option statement is not the standard reference answer.

**C. Every 5th patient has an X-linked recessive disorder**
X-linked recessive disorders are uncommon, well below 20% of cases.
The figure is overestimated.

## Mind Capsule

> Mendelian disorders: **AD about 2/3**, AR about 1/3 (in the standard teaching), X-linked and Y-linked small; Y-linked the rarest.
