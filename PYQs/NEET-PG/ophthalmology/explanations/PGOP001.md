# Left Optic Tract Lesion

Loss of the **right half of the visual field in both eyes** is a **right homonymous hemianopia**. It is caused by a lesion **behind the chiasma on the opposite (left) side**, here the **left optic tract**.

## Trigger Point

> Cannot see the **right side from both eyes**.

## Visual Field Defects by Lesion Site

| Site of lesion | Field defect |
|---|---|
| Optic nerve | Total loss of vision in that eye |
| Optic chiasma | Bitemporal hemianopia |
| Optic tract | Contralateral homonymous hemianopia (incongruous) |
| Occipital cortex | Contralateral homonymous hemianopia, macula spared |

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Right occipital lobe lesion**
Would cause loss of the **left** field in both eyes.

**C. Optic chiasma lesion**
Causes bitemporal hemianopia (each eye loses its outer half).

**D. Right optic nerve lesion**
Causes blindness in the right eye alone, not a half-field loss in both.

## Mind Capsule

> Right homonymous hemianopia = **left** retrochiasmal lesion (tract, radiation or cortex).
