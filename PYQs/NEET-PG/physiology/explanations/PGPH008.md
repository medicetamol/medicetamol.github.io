# Absence of Ipsilateral Arm Proprioception

The **dorsal column** is laminated: **fasciculus gracilis (medial)** carries fibres from the lower limb and **fasciculus cuneatus (lateral)** carries fibres from the upper limb above T6. Fibres remain **uncrossed** until the medulla, so a lateral dorsal column lesion in the cervical cord causes loss of **ipsilateral upper limb** proprioception, vibration and fine touch.

## Trigger Point

> Cervical injury piercing the **lateral** aspect of the dorsal column.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Ipsilateral lower limb proprioception**
Lower limb fibres run in the medial fasciculus gracilis.
They are spared in a lateral lesion.

**B. Loss of fine motor movement of fingers**
Fine motor movement is a corticospinal (motor) function.
The dorsal column is sensory; its loss gives sensory ataxia, not weakness.

**D. Contralateral lower limb proprioception**
Dorsal column fibres do not cross until the medulla.
A cord lesion therefore produces ipsilateral deficits.

## Mind Capsule

> Dorsal column: **medial = gracilis = leg**, **lateral = cuneatus = arm**; deficits are ipsilateral below the lesion.
