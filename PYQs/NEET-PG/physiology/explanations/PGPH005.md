# Facilitated Diffusion

In **facilitated diffusion**, a substance crosses the membrane through a **carrier or channel protein**, **down its concentration gradient**, without ATP. It is **saturable** and shows specificity and competitive inhibition, e.g. **GLUT transporters** for glucose.

## Key Finding

> Substance moves through a membrane protein, down its gradient, with no direct ATP use.

## Trigger Point

> Through a membrane protein, **down the concentration gradient**, no ATP.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Simple diffusion**
Movement directly through the lipid bilayer without a carrier.
It is not saturable and needs no protein.

**C. Primary active transport**
Movement **against a gradient** using ATP directly, e.g. Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase.
The diagram shows downhill movement without ATP.

**D. Secondary active transport**
Uphill movement coupled to the downhill flow of another solute (usually Na⁺), e.g. SGLT.
It uses an ion gradient made by ATP indirectly, and is not purely downhill.

## Mind Capsule

> Protein + downhill + no ATP = facilitated diffusion (GLUT); protein + uphill + ATP = primary active.
