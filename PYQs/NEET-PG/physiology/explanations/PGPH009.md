# Slow Prolonged Opening of Ca²⁺ Channels

The **ventricular myocardial action potential** has a **plateau (phase 2)** produced by **slow, prolonged opening of L-type Ca²⁺ channels** (inward Ca²⁺) balanced by outward K⁺ current. This prolongs the refractory period, preventing tetanus of the heart.

## Key Finding

> Red point marked on the plateau (phase 2) of the myocardial action potential.

## Trigger Point

> Point marked on the plateau phase.

## Myocardial Action Potential Phases

| Phase | Event |
|---|---|
| 0 | Rapid depolarization (Na⁺ influx) |
| 1 | Early repolarization (K⁺ efflux, Na⁺ channel closure) |
| 2 | **Plateau (Ca²⁺ influx vs K⁺ efflux)** |
| 3 | Repolarization (K⁺ efflux) |
| 4 | Resting potential |

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Na⁺ opening and fast K⁺ closing**
This describes phase 0 depolarization.
The plateau is not due to Na⁺ entry.

**C. Na⁺ closure and slow K⁺ opening**
This describes repolarization (phase 3).
At the plateau Ca²⁺ influx still balances K⁺ efflux.

**D. Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase**
It maintains ionic gradients, mainly in phase 4.
It does not shape the plateau.

## Mind Capsule

> Plateau = slow Ca²⁺ in (L-type) = long refractory period; this is why cardiac muscle cannot be tetanized.
