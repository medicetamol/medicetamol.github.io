# Sweating

**Heat stroke** is a core temperature above **40 °C (104 °F)** with CNS dysfunction. Thermoregulation fails, with **hot, dry, flushed skin**, tachypnea, tachycardia and **hypotension** from vasodilatation and dehydration. Decreased skin turgor reflects fluid loss. Sweating is classically absent (**anhidrosis**), although exertional heat stroke may still show some sweating.

## Trigger Point

> Sudden collapse while working in the field, temperature **105 °F**, reduced skin turgor.

## Why the Others Are True

**A. Tachypnea**
Hyperventilation occurs from the high temperature and acidosis.
It is a recognized feature of heat stroke.

**B. Hypotension**
Peripheral vasodilatation and volume depletion lower blood pressure.
This is a common finding.

**D. Red hot skin**
Cutaneous vasodilatation gives flushed skin, and failure of sweating leaves it hot and dry.
This is the classical sign.

## Mind Capsule

> Heat stroke = **hot, dry, red skin + temp > 40 °C + CNS signs**; heat exhaustion has profuse sweating with normal mentation.
