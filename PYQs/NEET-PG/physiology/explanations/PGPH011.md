# SGLT 1

**SGLT1** is the Na⁺-glucose cotransporter on the **brush border of small intestinal enterocytes**. It absorbs glucose (and galactose) by **secondary active transport**, driven by the Na⁺ gradient. In **ORS**, glucose enhances Na⁺ and water absorption, helping rehydration even in diarrhoea.

## Trigger Point

> ORS in dehydration; receptor/transporter that absorbs glucose from the gut.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. SGLT 2**
Located in the **early proximal convoluted tubule**, reabsorbing about 90% of filtered glucose.
It is the target of gliflozins, not an intestinal transporter.

**C. GLUT 1**
An insulin-independent transporter in RBCs, brain and the blood-brain barrier.
It does not mediate intestinal absorption.

**D. GLUT 2**
It moves glucose across the **basolateral membrane** of enterocytes and renal tubules, and is found in liver and beta cells.
It exports glucose to blood, not the luminal uptake.

## Mind Capsule

> Intestinal uptake = **SGLT1** (apical), exit = **GLUT2** (basolateral); fructose = GLUT5.
