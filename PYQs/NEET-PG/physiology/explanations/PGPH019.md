# Respiratory Alkalosis

At high altitude, **hypoxia** stimulates the **peripheral chemoreceptors** and causes **hyperventilation**. The fall in **PaCO₂** produces a primary **respiratory alkalosis**. Renal compensation (bicarbonate excretion) develops over days, and the HIF-mediated increase in erythropoietin supports acclimatization.

## Trigger Point

> Trekking at **high altitude** with a physiological response to hypoxia.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Metabolic acidosis**
Primary fall in HCO₃⁻, as in diabetic ketoacidosis or renal failure.
Altitude does not cause it as the primary change.

**B. Metabolic alkalosis**
Primary rise in HCO₃⁻, as in vomiting.
It is not related to altitude.

**C. Respiratory acidosis**
CO₂ retention from hypoventilation, as in COPD.
Altitude causes hyperventilation instead.

## Mind Capsule

> High altitude = hypoxia = hyperventilation = **respiratory alkalosis**; compensation by renal HCO₃⁻ loss (acetazolamide helps).
