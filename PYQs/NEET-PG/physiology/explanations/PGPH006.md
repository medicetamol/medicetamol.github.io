# Surface Tension Increased; Compliance Decreased

**Neonatal RDS** is due to **surfactant deficiency** (type II pneumocytes mature around 34-36 weeks). Without surfactant, alveolar **surface tension rises**, alveoli collapse at end-expiration (atelectasis), and the lungs become stiff, so **compliance falls**. Surfactant (mainly **DPPC**) reduces surface tension and prevents collapse as per Laplace's law (P = 2T/r).

## Trigger Point

> Preterm baby at 28 weeks with respiratory distress syndrome.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Surface tension decreased; compliance increased**
This is the normal effect of surfactant.
It is the opposite of what happens in RDS.

**C. Both decreased**
Compliance falls, but surface tension does not fall; surfactant lack raises it.
Only half of this pair is correct.

**D. Both increased**
Surface tension increases, but a stiff lung with high tension has reduced compliance.
Compliance cannot rise in RDS.

## Mind Capsule

> No surfactant = **↑ surface tension, ↓ compliance**, atelectasis; the L/S ratio above 2 indicates fetal lung maturity.
