# Becomes More Positive

The **resting membrane potential** is mainly set by the **K⁺ gradient** across the membrane. Raising extracellular K⁺ (**hyperkalaemia**, here from muscle injury and rhabdomyolysis) reduces the gradient for K⁺ efflux, so the RMP becomes **less negative (partially depolarized)**. This brings the membrane closer to threshold; prolonged depolarization inactivates Na⁺ channels.

## Trigger Point

> Muscle injury with serum K⁺ **5.5 mEq/L** (raised) = hyperkalaemia.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. No change**
The RMP is sensitive to extracellular K⁺ because the membrane is most permeable to K⁺ at rest.
Even small rises in K⁺ shift the potential.

**B. Becomes more negative**
This occurs in **hypokalaemia**, where the K⁺ gradient increases (hyperpolarization).
Hyperkalaemia does the opposite.

**D. First more positive then negative**
No such biphasic change is seen in the RMP.
The shift is sustained depolarization.

## Mind Capsule

> ↑ ECF K⁺ = RMP less negative (depolarized); ↓ ECF K⁺ = hyperpolarized (more negative).
