# Respiratory Alkalosis

**Hyperventilation** causes respiratory alkalosis. Alkalosis increases **albumin binding of Ca²⁺**, lowering **ionized Ca²⁺** and increasing neuromuscular excitability. This produces **perioral and distal paraesthesia, carpopedal spasm, tetany, seizures and laryngospasm**, as in hypocalcaemia.

## Trigger Point

> Muscle spasm, numbness of hands and feet, seizures and laryngospasm.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Respiratory acidosis**
Raised CO₂ causes drowsiness, flushing and headache (CO₂ narcosis).
Acidosis increases ionized Ca²⁺ and does not cause tetany.

**C. Metabolic acidosis**
Presents with deep, rapid breathing (Kussmaul) and lethargy.
Ionized Ca²⁺ is raised, so tetany does not occur.

**D. Metabolic alkalosis**
It can also lower ionized Ca²⁺, but the clinical clue here is the acute presentation after hyperventilation.
Here the classical neurological picture of **alkalosis from hyperventilation** fits best.

## Mind Capsule

> Alkalosis = ↓ ionized Ca²⁺ = **tetany** (spasm, perioral numbness, laryngospasm).
