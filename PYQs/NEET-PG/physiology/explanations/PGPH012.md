# Tricuspid Stenosis

A **giant 'a' wave** in the JVP results from the right atrium contracting forcefully against a **raised resistance**, such as **tricuspid stenosis**, pulmonary hypertension or pulmonary stenosis. Right heart congestion explains the **hepatomegaly** and fatigue with exertional dyspnoea.

## Trigger Point

> JVP with **higher amplitude of 'a' wave** and hepatomegaly.

## JVP Waveform Abnormalities

| Finding | Cause |
|---|---|
| Giant 'a' wave | Tricuspid stenosis, pulmonary hypertension, pulmonary stenosis |
| Cannon 'a' wave | AV dissociation |
| Absent 'a' wave | Atrial fibrillation |
| Giant 'c-v' wave | Tricuspid regurgitation |

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Tricuspid regurgitation**
Regurgitant flow into the right atrium gives a large **c-v (systolic) wave**.
The 'a' wave is not typically prominent.

**C. Mitral regurgitation**
It affects the left atrium and does not change the JVP directly in the early stages.
It does not produce a giant 'a' wave.

**D. Mitral stenosis**
The left atrium is affected, and later pulmonary hypertension causes right heart changes.
A prominent 'a' wave is not the typical finding.

## Mind Capsule

> Giant **a** wave = atrium contracting against resistance (TS, pulmonary hypertension); giant **c-v** wave = TR.
