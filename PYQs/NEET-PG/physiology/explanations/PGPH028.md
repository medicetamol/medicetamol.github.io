# −2 mmHg

**Starling forces** determine net fluid movement across a capillary: **Net filtration = (Pc − Pi) − (πc − πi)**. With no net movement, the net filtration pressure is zero, so the interstitial hydrostatic pressure can be solved. Subcutaneous interstitial pressure is slightly **negative** at rest, consistent with this answer.

## Working

> Net filtration = (Pc − Pi) − (πc − πi) = 0
> (18 − Pi) − (27 − 7) = 0
> 18 − Pi − 20 = 0
> **Pi = −2 mmHg**

## Trigger Point

> No net movement of fluid across the capillary wall = net filtration pressure is zero.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. 0 mmHg**
Substituting gives a net force of −2 mmHg, i.e. net absorption.
The fluid would not be in balance.

**B. +1 mmHg**
It gives net filtration pressure of −3 mmHg.
That favours reabsorption.

**C. +2 mmHg**
It gives a net pressure of −4 mmHg, also favouring reabsorption.
Only −2 mmHg gives zero.

## Mind Capsule

> Net filtration = (Pc − Pi) − (πc − πi); equilibrium when the sum is zero.
