# 14 mEq/L

The **anion gap** is calculated as **Na⁺ − (Cl⁻ + HCO₃⁻)**. Here it is 142 − (100 + 28) = **14 mEq/L**, at the upper end of normal (8-12 mEq/L by older methods; up to about 12-16 by some labs).

## Working

> AG = Na⁺ − (Cl⁻ + HCO₃⁻)
> AG = 142 − (100 + 28)
> AG = 142 − 128
> **AG = 14 mEq/L**

## Trigger Point

> Calculate anion gap from Na⁺ 142, Cl⁻ 100, HCO₃⁻ 28.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. 10 mEq/L**
It results from arithmetic error (e.g. using 132 for Na⁺).
The correct subtraction gives 14.

**C. 18 mEq/L**
It would need a lower sum of Cl⁻ and HCO₃⁻ (about 124).
The values given do not support it.

**D. 24 mEq/L**
It is a high anion gap, typical of metabolic acidosis, and does not fit these values.
The patient has respiratory acidosis with renal compensation.

## Mind Capsule

> AG = Na⁺ − (Cl⁻ + HCO₃⁻); normal about 8-12 (lab-dependent). COPD with ↑ PaCO₂ and ↑ HCO₃⁻ = chronic respiratory acidosis with normal gap.
