# Expiratory Reserve Volume (ERV)

**ERV** is the extra volume that can be forcibly expired after a **normal (tidal) expiration**, i.e. from the end-tidal level down to maximal expiration. Normal value is about **1100 mL**.

## Key Finding

> Marked region lies between the end of a normal expiration and the maximal expiration level.

## Trigger Point

> Region between end of normal expiration and maximal expiration.

## Lung Volumes and Capacities

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Tidal volume (TV) | Air moved in a normal breath (~500 mL) |
| ERV | Forced expiration after a normal expiration (~1100 mL) |
| RV | Air left after maximal expiration (~1200 mL) |
| FRC | ERV + RV (~2300 mL) |
| Expiratory capacity | TV + ERV |

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Functional residual capacity**
It is ERV + RV, the lung volume at the end of a normal expiration.
It includes the residual volume, which cannot be seen on spirometry.

**B. Tidal volume**
It is the normal breathing excursion between inspiration and expiration.
It does not extend down to maximal expiration.

**D. Expiratory capacity**
It is TV + ERV, measured from the end of normal inspiration to maximal expiration.
The marked region is only the ERV part.

## Mind Capsule

> FRC = ERV + RV; it cannot be measured by simple spirometry (needs helium dilution or body plethysmography).
