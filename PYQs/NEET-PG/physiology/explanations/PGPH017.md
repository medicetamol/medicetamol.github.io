# Moderate, Optimal Overlap with Cross-Bridges

Tension in a sarcomere depends on the **number of cross-bridges formed**. At **optimal length (about 2.2 µm)**, thick and thin filaments overlap maximally, so **active tension is maximum**. Stretching beyond this reduces overlap; shortening too much causes thin filaments to collide and overlap, reducing tension.

## Key Finding

> Point C is at the top of the ascending limb near the plateau, with near-maximal tension.

## Trigger Point

> Point C = region of maximal tension in the length-tension curve.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Widely separated filaments, minimal overlap**
It matches an over-stretched sarcomere on the descending limb.
Few cross-bridges form, so tension is low.

**C. Increased overlap, thinner zone**
It matches a shortened sarcomere on the left side of the curve.
Overlap beyond the optimum reduces tension.

**D. Thin filaments overlapping at the centre**
It matches maximal shortening, where thin filaments collide.
Tension is very low.

## Mind Capsule

> Max tension = optimal overlap (~2.2 µm), max cross-bridges; too stretched or too short = less tension.
