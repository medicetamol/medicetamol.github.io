# A: Glucose, B: Bicarbonate, C: Inulin, D: PAH

Renal handling differs for each substance. **Inulin** is freely filtered and neither reabsorbed nor secreted, so its clearance equals **GFR** and stays constant at all plasma levels. **PAH** is filtered and also secreted, so its clearance approximates **renal plasma flow** and falls as the secretory Tm is saturated. **Glucose and bicarbonate** are completely reabsorbed up to their renal threshold, so their clearance is zero until it is exceeded.

## Key Finding

> Curves show a flat line (inulin), a falling line (PAH) and two substances with zero clearance until a threshold (glucose, bicarbonate).

## Trigger Point

> Match each curve with the substance based on its renal handling.

## Renal Handling

| Substance | Handling | Clearance |
|---|---|---|
| Inulin | Filtered only | = GFR, constant |
| PAH | Filtered + secreted | ≈ RPF, falls at high plasma levels |
| Glucose | Filtered, fully reabsorbed to Tm | Zero below threshold |
| Bicarbonate | Filtered, reabsorbed | Zero below threshold |

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Glucose, PAH, Bicarbonate, Inulin**
It assigns the PAH and inulin curves to the wrong lines.
Inulin cannot show the falling pattern, which belongs to PAH.

**C. PAH, Inulin, Glucose, Bicarbonate**
It places PAH on a flat or zero-clearance curve.
PAH clearance is the highest of all and decreases as plasma levels rise.

**D. Inulin, Glucose, Bicarbonate, PAH**
It matches the substances to the curves in an order that conflicts with their handling.
Inulin must be the flat constant-clearance curve.

## Mind Capsule

> Inulin clearance = GFR (constant); PAH clearance = RPF (falls once Tm saturates); glucose = 0 until threshold (~180 mg/dL).
