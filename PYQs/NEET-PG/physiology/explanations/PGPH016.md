# Increases Ca²⁺ Reabsorption in the DCT

**Thiazides** inhibit the Na⁺-Cl⁻ cotransporter in the **distal convoluted tubule**, which increases the Na⁺ gradient and enhances **Ca²⁺ reabsorption** through the Na⁺/Ca²⁺ exchanger. The resulting **fall in urinary calcium** reduces calcium stone formation (hypercalciuria).

## Trigger Point

> Hydrochlorothiazide to prevent **calcium stones**.

## Drug Facts

| Drug | Effect on urinary Ca²⁺ | Key adverse effect |
|---|---|---|
| Thiazides | Decreased | Hypokalaemia, hyperuricaemia, hyperglycaemia |
| Loop diuretics | Increased | Ototoxicity, hypokalaemia |

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Increases urinary citrate**
Citrate is a stone inhibitor, but it is increased by potassium citrate, not thiazides.
Thiazides can lower citrate through hypokalaemia.

**C. Increases glomerular filtration of Ca²⁺**
Thiazides do not increase the filtered load of calcium.
Their effect is on tubular reabsorption.

**D. Directly dissolves stones by altering urinary pH**
Thiazides do not dissolve existing stones.
Alkalinization with citrate dissolves uric acid stones.

## Mind Capsule

> **T**hiazides = **T**ake calcium back (↓ urinary Ca²⁺, useful in hypercalciuria and osteoporosis); loops = lose calcium.
