# B to D is the Refractory Period

The **refractory period** of a nerve extends from the **start of depolarization (threshold)** to the **end of repolarization**. During the **absolute** refractory period (Na⁺ channel inactivation) no stimulus can fire another action potential; during the **relative** refractory period only a stronger stimulus can.

## Key Finding

> Action potential with threshold, depolarization (up-stroke), repolarization and after-hyperpolarization marked from A to E.

## Trigger Point

> Which statement is true about the nerve action potential curve.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. The threshold point is at A**
A marks the resting membrane potential, before the stimulus.
Threshold is the point where the rapid rise begins.

**B. At point E, the nerve is more excitable**
E lies in the after-hyperpolarization phase, where the membrane is further from threshold.
A stronger stimulus is needed, so excitability is lower.

**C. Point C to D is due to Na⁺ opening and K⁺ closing**
C to D is repolarization.
It is due to **Na⁺ channel inactivation and K⁺ channel opening**, the reverse of what is stated.

## Mind Capsule

> Refractory period = from threshold to end of repolarization; absolute RP (Na⁺ inactivation) then relative RP.
