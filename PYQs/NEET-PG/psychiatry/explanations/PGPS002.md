# Delirium Tremens

The most severe form of alcohol withdrawal, appearing **48–72 hours** after the last drink. It presents as delirium (clouded sensorium, agitation), vivid hallucinations (often visual), coarse tremor and autonomic hyperactivity. Treat with **benzodiazepines** plus **thiamine**.

## Trigger Point

> Alcohol stopped abruptly **3 days** ago **+** agitation, altered sensorium, paranoid delusions and hallucinations.

## Alcohol Withdrawal Timeline

| Time after last drink | Syndrome |
|---|---|
| 6–12 h | Tremor, anxiety, insomnia |
| 12–48 h | Withdrawal seizures ("rum fits"), alcoholic hallucinosis |
| 48–96 h | Delirium tremens |

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Wernicke's Encephalopathy**
Thiamine-deficiency triad of confusion, ataxia and ophthalmoplegia. It is not tied to abrupt withdrawal and has no hallucinations.

**B. Korsakoff's psychosis**
A chronic amnestic syndrome (amnesia with confabulation) with a clear sensorium, not an acute agitated delirium.

**C. Hangover**
A self-limiting post-intoxication state (headache, nausea) without delirium or hallucinations.

## Mind Capsule

> Alcohol stopped 48–72 h ago + delirium + hallucinations + autonomic hyperactivity = Delirium Tremens.
