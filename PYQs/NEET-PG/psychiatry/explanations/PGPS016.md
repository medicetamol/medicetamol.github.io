# Stop Antipsychotic and Give Tetrabenazine

**Tardive dyskinesia** is a late-onset, involuntary orofacial and choreoathetoid movement disorder after prolonged dopamine-blocker use. Management is to **stop or switch the offending drug** (e.g. to clozapine) and add a **VMAT-2 inhibitor** (tetrabenazine, valbenazine, deutetrabenazine). Stopping is not always feasible in active psychosis.

## Drug Facts

| Drug | Class | Key adverse effect |
|---|---|---|
| Tetrabenazine | VMAT-2 inhibitor | Depression, suicidality, parkinsonism |
| Valbenazine | VMAT-2 inhibitor | Somnolence |

## Trigger Point

> Long-term antipsychotics with involuntary **mouth and lip (perioral) movements**.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Increase the antipsychotic dose**
Masks the movements temporarily but worsens tardive dyskinesia over time.

**B. Start benzodiazepines**
Not a first-line treatment; their role is minor and symptomatic at best.

**D. Give anticholinergics**
Treat acute EPS (dystonia, parkinsonism) but can worsen tardive dyskinesia.

## Mind Capsule

> Acute EPS = anticholinergics.
> Tardive dyskinesia = stop / switch the offender + VMAT-2 inhibitor; anticholinergics worsen it.
