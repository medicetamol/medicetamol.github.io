# Hollow Viscus Perforation

**Free gas under the diaphragm** (a crescent beneath the hemidiaphragm on an erect chest X-ray) is **pneumoperitoneum**, most often from perforation of a hollow viscus such as a duodenal ulcer. The patient should stay upright for 5-10 minutes before the film. It can also be seen after recent laparotomy.

## Trigger Point

> Erect chest radiograph with **crescentic free gas beneath the diaphragm**.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Empyema Thorax**
A pleural collection with fluid, not gas under the diaphragm.

**B. Liver abscess**
Gas, if present, lies within the liver parenchyma, often with an air-fluid level.

**C. Gastric volvulus**
A distended stomach with double air-fluid levels, with no free subdiaphragmatic crescent.

## Mind Capsule

> Crescent of free gas under the diaphragm on an erect film = pneumoperitoneum → hollow viscus perforation.
