# Temporal Lobe Abscess

A **ring-enhancing lesion** with a hypodense centre (pus), a thin smooth capsule and perilesional oedema; restricted diffusion in the centre favours abscess over necrotic tumour. **Temporal lobe** and cerebellar abscesses commonly arise from **otogenic** spread (middle ear and mastoid infection).

## Trigger Point

> CT brain showing a **ring-enhancing lesion in the temporal lobe**.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Cerebellar abscess**
A ring lesion in the posterior fossa. The lesion here is temporal.

**C. Extradural abscess**
A biconvex extra-axial collection beneath the skull, not an intraparenchymal ring.

**D. Meningitis**
Leptomeningeal enhancement without a discrete ring lesion.

## Mind Capsule

> Ring-enhancing lesion + restricted diffusion = abscess; temporal lobe site suggests otogenic origin.
