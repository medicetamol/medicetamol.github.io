# Gallstone Ileus

A large gallstone (> 2.5 cm) erodes through a cholecysto-enteric fistula and impacts in the small bowel, usually at the terminal ileum. The abdominal radiograph shows **Rigler's triad**: small bowel obstruction, **pneumobilia** and an ectopic gallstone.

## Trigger Point

> Dilated bowel loops, air-fluid levels and **branching gas within the biliary tree**.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Small bowel obstruction**
Non-specific and does not explain air in the biliary tree.

**C. Large bowel obstruction**
Dilated colon with haustra at the periphery, without pneumobilia.

**D. Paralytic ileus**
Generalised dilatation of small and large bowel without a transition point or pneumobilia.

## Mind Capsule

> Small bowel obstruction + pneumobilia + ectopic gallstone = Gallstone ileus (Rigler's triad).
