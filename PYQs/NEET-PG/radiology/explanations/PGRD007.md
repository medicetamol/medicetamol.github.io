# FAST

**eFAST** (extended Focused Assessment with Sonography in Trauma) is a rapid, repeatable bedside ultrasound that looks for free fluid in the pericardium and abdomen, and for **haemothorax and pneumothorax** on the extended chest views. Absent air entry with chest wall tenderness after trauma suggests thoracic or splenic injury.

## eFAST Windows

| Window | Looks for |
|---|---|
| Pericardial | Haemopericardium |
| Right and left upper quadrants | Free fluid in Morison's pouch and splenorenal space |
| Pelvis | Free fluid |
| Thorax | Pneumothorax (absent lung sliding), haemothorax |

## Trigger Point

> RTA with **absent air entry on the left** and **left lower chest wall tenderness**; asks for the **next step in emergency**.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. X-ray**
Chest X-ray is also an ATLS adjunct and is done alongside; the key prefers the faster bedside eFAST as the first step.

**B. CT**
Needs a haemodynamically stable patient and transport.

**D. DPL**
Invasive and now largely replaced by FAST.

## Mind Capsule

> Blunt trauma, emergency first imaging = eFAST.
