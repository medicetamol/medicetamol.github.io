# Ankylosing Spondylitis

**Bilateral sacroiliitis** (erosions, sclerosis, fusion) with **marginal syndesmophytes** bridging the vertebral bodies gives the **bamboo spine**. It is an HLA-B27 spondyloarthropathy of young men.

## Radiological Signs in AS

| Sign | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Bilateral sacroiliitis | Earliest and most consistent finding |
| Shiny corner / squaring | Romanus lesion and vertebral squaring |
| Syndesmophytes | Thin marginal vertical bridges, giving bamboo spine |
| Dagger and trolley-track signs | Ossified interspinous and supraspinous ligaments |

## Trigger Point

> Spine radiograph with **bilateral sacroiliac joint changes** and **marginal vertebral bridging**.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Psoriatic arthritis**
Asymmetric sacroiliitis with non-marginal (chunky) syndesmophytes and DIP changes.

**C. Rheumatoid arthritis**
Peripheral small-joint erosions and cervical involvement, sparing the sacroiliac joints.

**D. Sjogeren Syndrome**
An autoimmune sicca syndrome without bamboo spine.

## Mind Capsule

> Bilateral sacroiliitis + marginal syndesmophytes = Ankylosing spondylitis.
