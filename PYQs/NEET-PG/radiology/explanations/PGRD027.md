# MRI Sacroiliac Joint

**MRI of the SI joints** (STIR / fat-suppressed) shows active sacroiliitis as **bone marrow oedema**, years before X-ray changes appear. It is therefore the earliest imaging modality for ankylosing spondylitis.

## Imaging in Axial Spondyloarthritis

| Modality | Role |
|---|---|
| X-ray pelvis | First-line, but shows established changes late |
| MRI SI joints | Earliest: marrow oedema (active inflammation) |
| CT | Best for erosions and structural change; radiation |
| Bone scan | Low specificity |

## Trigger Point

> "**Earliest** imaging modality to detect ankylosing spondylitis."

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. CT sacroiliac joint**
Shows structural damage that appears late, with radiation.

**C. X ray**
First-line but changes appear only after years.

**D. Bone scan**
Non-specific with low sensitivity.

## Mind Capsule

> Earliest imaging for sacroiliitis = MRI SI joints (STIR).
