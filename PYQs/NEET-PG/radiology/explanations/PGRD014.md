# TEE

A hypertensive patient with sudden severe chest pain, syncope and **unequal pulses** has **aortic dissection** until proven otherwise. In an **unstable** patient, bedside **transoesophageal echocardiography** is rapid, needs no contrast or transport and is highly accurate. CT angiography is preferred if stable.

## Investigations in Aortic Dissection

| Modality | Role |
|---|---|
| CT angiography | First choice in stable patients |
| TEE | Unstable patients, bedside |
| MRA | Most accurate, but slow; stable patients and follow-up |
| Chest X-ray | Widened mediastinum; non-specific |

## Trigger Point

> Hypertension with **acute severe chest pain**, loss of consciousness and **unequal pulses**, in an **emergency**.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Cardiac enzymes**
Used for myocardial infarction and cannot diagnose dissection.

**B. MRI**
Very accurate, but slow and impractical in an unstable patient.

**C. X-ray**
A widened mediastinum is only suggestive, and the film can be normal.

## Mind Capsule

> Suspected aortic dissection: unstable → TEE; stable → CT angiography.
