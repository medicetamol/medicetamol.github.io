# Cephalhematoma

A **subperiosteal** blood collection from birth trauma. The swelling is firm, appears within hours to days of birth and does **not cross suture lines** because periosteum is attached at the sutures. It resolves over weeks to months and may calcify. It can cause neonatal jaundice.

## Neonatal Scalp Swellings

| Swelling | Layer | Crosses sutures? |
|---|---|---|
| Caput succedaneum | Subcutaneous oedema | Yes |
| Cephalhematoma | Subperiosteal | No |
| Subgaleal hematoma | Under the galea aponeurotica | Yes |

## Trigger Point

> Parietal swelling present since birth, with the X-ray showing swelling **confined within suture margins**.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Subgaleal hematoma**
Crosses sutures, is boggy and expanding, and can cause shock.

**C. Caput succedaneum**
Soft, pitting oedema that crosses sutures and settles within days.

**D. Encephalocele**
A midline skull defect with herniation of brain tissue.

## Mind Capsule

> Swelling that does not cross sutures = cephalhematoma; crossing sutures = caput or subgaleal.
