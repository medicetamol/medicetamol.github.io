# Giant Cell Tumor

An **eccentric lytic epiphyseal-metaphyseal** lesion reaching the subarticular surface in a skeletally mature adult (20-40 years), with a "soap-bubble" look and sometimes an adjacent soft-tissue mass. Common sites are the distal femur, proximal tibia and **distal radius**.

## Bone Tumours by Site

| Site | Tumours |
|---|---|
| Epiphysis | Chondroblastoma, giant cell tumour, clear cell chondrosarcoma |
| Metaphysis | Osteosarcoma, osteochondroma, NOF, ABC |
| Diaphysis | Ewing's sarcoma, osteoid osteoma, myeloma |

## Trigger Point

> Localised **soft-tissue swelling around the wrist** with an adjacent soft-tissue mass.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Osteochondroma**
A cartilage-capped metaphyseal outgrowth pointing away from the joint.

**C. Osteoid Osteoma**
A small lucent nidus with surrounding sclerosis and night pain relieved by NSAIDs.

**D. Ewings Sarcoma**
A diaphyseal permeative lesion with onion-peel periosteal reaction in children.

## Mind Capsule

> Eccentric lytic epiphyseal lesion at the distal radius or knee in an adult = Giant cell tumour.

## Memory Hook

> **CGC**: Chondroblastoma, Giant cell tumour, Clear cell chondrosarcoma are the epiphyseal tumours.
