# Syphilis

**Primary syphilis** (Treponema pallidum) produces a single, **painless, indurated ulcer (chancre)** with **non-tender, rubbery** bilateral inguinal lymphadenopathy, appearing about 3 weeks after exposure.

## Trigger Point

> **Painless** genital ulcer with **non-tender** inguinal lymphadenopathy.

## Genital Ulcer Diseases

| Disease | Ulcer | Lymph nodes |
|---|---|---|
| Primary syphilis | Single, painless, indurated | Non-tender, rubbery |
| Chancroid | Multiple, painful, ragged, soft | Tender, suppurative bubo |
| Genital herpes | Grouped vesicles, painful erosions | Tender |
| Donovanosis | Painless, beefy-red, bleeds easily | No true nodes (pseudobubo) |

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Chancroid**
Painful, soft ulcer with a tender suppurative bubo.

**C. Herpes genitalis**
Painful grouped vesicles that break into shallow erosions.

**D. Granuloma inguinale**
Painless beefy-red, bleeding ulcers with pseudobuboes rather than true lymphadenopathy.

## Mind Capsule

> Painless indurated ulcer + non-tender nodes = Syphilis; painful ulcer + tender bubo = Chancroid.
