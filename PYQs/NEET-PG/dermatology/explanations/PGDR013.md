# Nevus Depigmentosus

A congenital or early-onset, **stable, localised hypomelanosis** with normal melanocyte number but reduced melanin transfer. The patch does not progress or change in distribution, and Wood's lamp shows accentuation. **Diascopy is negative** because the defect is pigmentary, not vascular. It is usually left untreated.

## Trigger Point

> **Non-progressive** hypopigmented trunk lesion, **white accentuation on Wood's lamp**, **diascopy negative**.

## Hypopigmented Macules

| Condition | Wood's lamp and clues |
|---|---|
| Vitiligo | Chalky-white accentuation; acquired, progressive |
| Nevus depigmentosus | Accentuated; stable; diascopy negative |
| Nevus anemicus | No accentuation; vanishes on diascopy |
| Indeterminate leprosy | No accentuation; impaired sensation |

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Vitiligo**
Acquired, progressive loss of melanocytes. A stable, non-progressive patch favours nevus depigmentosus.

**C. Nevus anemicus**
A vascular naevus (local vasoconstriction); diascopy makes it disappear.

**D. Indeterminate leprosy**
Ill-defined hypopigmented patch with impaired sensation and no Wood's lamp accentuation.

## Mind Capsule

> Stable hypopigmented patch + Wood's accentuation + diascopy negative = Nevus depigmentosus.
> Nevus anemicus = diascopy positive.
