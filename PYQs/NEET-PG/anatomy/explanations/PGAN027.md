# Marginal Mandibular Nerve

The **marginal mandibular branch of the facial nerve** runs along the lower border of the mandible, superficial to the **submandibular gland** and its fascial capsule. It crosses superficial to the gland and the facial vein, at the level of the posterior belly of digastric, and is at risk during resection of the gland. A skin incision is placed about **2.5 cm below the mandible** and the platysma and deep fascia are lifted to protect it. Injury causes drooping of the **lower lip on the affected side**.

## Trigger Point

> Structure injured during submandibular gland resection that runs **superior** to the posterior belly of digastric = facial nerve branch.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Lingual nerve**
It lies deep to the gland, hanging from it by the submandibular ganglion.
It is at risk from deep dissection near the mylohyoid and hyoglossus, not superficial to the gland.

**C. Trigeminal nerve**
The main trunk lies at the skull base and is not near the submandibular region.
Only its branches (lingual, mylohyoid) approach the gland.

**D. Hypoglossal nerve**
It loops deep to the posterior belly of digastric and runs on hyoglossus to reach the tongue.
It can be damaged in deep dissection, but it is not the superficial structure in the clue.

## Mind Capsule

> Superficial structures at risk in submandibular gland excision = **marginal mandibular nerve**; deep structures = lingual and hypoglossal nerves.
