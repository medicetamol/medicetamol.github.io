# Deltoid Ligament

The **deltoid (medial) ligament** is a strong, triangular ligament connecting the **medial malleolus to the talus, calcaneus and navicular**. It resists **eversion**, so forceful eversion tears it, often with an avulsion of the medial malleolus or a fibular fracture.

## Trigger Point

> Ankle injury after **forceful eversion**.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Anterior talofibular ligament**
The weakest and most commonly injured lateral ligament.
It tears with **inversion** and plantar flexion.

**B. Calcaneofibular ligament**
A lateral ligament injured with severe inversion, after the ATFL.
It does not resist eversion.

**D. Posterior talofibular ligament**
The strongest of the lateral ligaments, torn only in severe dislocations.
It is rarely injured in isolation and not by eversion.

## Mind Capsule

> **E**version = **D**eltoid (medial); **I**nversion = **A**TFL (lateral), the most commonly sprained.
