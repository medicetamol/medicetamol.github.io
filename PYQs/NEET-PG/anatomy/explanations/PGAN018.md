# Lateral Femoral Cutaneous Nerve (C)

**Meralgia paresthetica** is entrapment of the **lateral femoral cutaneous nerve of thigh (L2, L3)** as it passes under or through the inguinal ligament near the ASIS. It causes burning, tingling and numbness over the **anterolateral thigh** without motor weakness. Obesity, tight belts and pregnancy are classic causes.

## Key Finding

> Lumbar plexus diagram with four branches; the nerve arising from L2-L3 and running to the lateral thigh is marked C.

## Trigger Point

> Meralgia paresthetica = lateral femoral cutaneous nerve.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. A**
This marks a different branch from the upper plexus (e.g. iliohypogastric or ilioinguinal), supplying suprapubic or groin skin.
It does not supply the lateral thigh.

**B. B**
This is another branch of the plexus that does not supply the anterolateral thigh.
It would not produce the sensory pattern of meralgia paresthetica.

**D. D**
This marks a lower branch of the plexus, such as the femoral or obturator nerve, with significant motor roles.
Meralgia paresthetica is purely sensory.

## Mind Capsule

> Meralgia paresthetica = **L2-L3 lateral femoral cutaneous nerve**, pure sensory, anterolateral thigh.
