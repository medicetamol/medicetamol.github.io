# Median Nerve

The **pen test** assesses **abductor pollicis brevis (APB)**: with the palm facing up, the patient abducts the thumb at right angles to the palm to touch a pen held above it. APB is supplied by the **recurrent (thenar) branch of the median nerve**, so failure indicates median nerve injury (e.g. carpal tunnel syndrome).

## Trigger Point

> Hand held palm-up with a pen test for thumb movement = **thumb abduction (APB)**.

## Common Clinical Tests of Hand Nerves

| Nerve | Test |
|---|---|
| Median | Pen test (APB), ape-hand, Benediction sign |
| Ulnar | Card test (palmar interossei), Froment's sign (adductor pollicis) |
| Radial | Wrist drop, finger extension |
| PIN | Inability to extend thumb and fingers at MCP joints, no wrist drop |

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Ulnar nerve**
Tested by the **card test** and **Froment's sign** (adductor pollicis and interossei).
These test adduction, not thumb abduction.

**C. PIN**
A purely motor branch of the radial nerve to the extensors of the forearm.
Its loss gives finger drop, not loss of thumb abduction.

**D. Musculocutaneous**
Supplies biceps, brachialis and coracobrachialis in the arm.
It has no role in the muscles of the hand.

## Mind Capsule

> Pen test = **P**en = **P**ollicis brevis (abductor) = median nerve; Card test = ulnar nerve.
