# Umbilical Artery

The fetus has **two umbilical arteries** (branches of the internal iliac arteries) that carry **deoxygenated blood from the fetus to the placenta**. The single **umbilical vein** carries oxygenated blood back to the fetus.

## Trigger Point

> Vessel carrying **deoxygenated** blood **back to the placenta**.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Umbilical vein**
One vein carries the most oxygenated blood **from the placenta to the fetus**.
It is the opposite direction to the one asked.

**C. Uterine artery**
It is a maternal vessel from the internal iliac artery that supplies the uterus and placental bed.
It belongs to the mother's circulation, not the fetal one.

**D. Descending aorta**
In the fetus it gives rise to the internal iliac arteries and then the umbilical arteries.
It is a feeder of the umbilical arteries, not the vessel that returns blood to the placenta.

## Mind Capsule

> Umbilical **A**rteries = **A**way from the fetus (deoxygenated); umbilical **V**ein = **V**ery oxygenated, toward fetus.
> Two arteries and one vein in the cord.
