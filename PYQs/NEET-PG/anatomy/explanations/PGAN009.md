# Musculocutaneous Nerve

The **musculocutaneous nerve (C5-C7)** supplies **coracobrachialis, biceps and brachialis** in the anterior compartment of the arm, then continues as the **lateral cutaneous nerve of the forearm**. Its injury weakens elbow flexion and forearm supination and causes sensory loss over the lateral forearm.

## Trigger Point

> Humerus fractures + **lateral forearm sensory loss** + weak elbow flexion and supination.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Median nerve**
It supplies the flexor compartment of the forearm and thenar muscles, with palmar sensation of the lateral 3.5 digits.
It does not give lateral forearm skin sensation or flexion at the elbow.

**C. Axillary nerve**
It supplies deltoid and teres minor, with skin over the **regimental badge area**.
It is injured in surgical neck fractures and does not affect elbow flexion.

**D. Radial nerve**
It is injured in mid-shaft humerus fractures, producing **wrist drop** and loss of elbow extension.
Though it supplies brachioradialis, the clue of lateral forearm sensory loss with weak biceps points elsewhere.

## Mind Capsule

> Weak elbow flexion + weak supination + lateral forearm numbness = **musculocutaneous**.
> Humeral shaft fracture with wrist drop = radial nerve.
