# Left Anterior Horn Cells

Muscle wasting, reduced tone and weakness of the hand indicate a **lower motor neuron (LMN) lesion**. The cell bodies of lower motor neurons lie in the **anterior horn of the spinal cord**, so their loss (e.g. in motor neuron disease or syringomyelia) produces wasting, hypotonia and fasciculations of the muscles supplied, here the left hand.

## Trigger Point

> **Muscle wasting + reduced tone** with impaired fine movements of one hand = LMN pattern.

## Quick Comparison

| Feature | LMN lesion | UMN lesion |
|---|---|---|
| Tone | Decreased | Increased (spastic) |
| Wasting | Marked | Minimal (disuse only) |
| Reflexes | Decreased | Increased |
| Fasciculations | Present | Absent |

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Pyramidal lesion**
A UMN lesion that causes spasticity, hyperreflexia and a Babinski sign.
Wasting and reduced tone are not features.

**C. Internal capsule**
A UMN lesion producing contralateral hemiplegia with spasticity, usually with face involvement.
It does not cause isolated hand wasting.

**D. Left corticospinal tract in the cervical cord**
It carries UMN fibres, so its damage gives spasticity and hyperreflexia below the level.
It would not produce hypotonia and wasting.

## Mind Capsule

> Wasting + hypotonia + fasciculations = **LMN** (anterior horn cell or nerve); spasticity + hyperreflexia = UMN.
