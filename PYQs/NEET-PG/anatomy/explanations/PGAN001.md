# Fascia Transversalis

The **deep inguinal ring** is not a true hole but an **oval opening (defect) in the fascia transversalis**, situated about **1.25 cm above the midpoint of the inguinal ligament**. The fascia transversalis is evaginated through it as the **internal spermatic fascia**, which covers the spermatic cord.

## Trigger Point

> Opening in the posterior wall of inguinal canal, through which the spermatic cord passes = **deep inguinal ring**.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Internal oblique**
Contributes the **cremasteric fascia** and the **conjoint tendon**, and forms the roof of the canal.
It is not the source of the deep ring, and it does not form the posterior wall.

**B. External oblique**
Its aponeurosis forms the **anterior wall**, and the **superficial inguinal ring** is a defect in this aponeurosis.
That is the opening at the other end of the canal, not the deep ring.

**C. Parietal peritoneum**
It lies deep to the fascia transversalis and only lines the ring from inside, without forming it.
It takes part in forming a hernial sac, but it is not the layer with the defect.

## Mind Capsule

> Deep = Deep fascia layer: deep ring is a defect in **fascia transversalis**.
> Superficial ring is a defect in **external oblique aponeurosis**.
