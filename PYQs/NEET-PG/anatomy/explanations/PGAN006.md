# Neck of the Fibula (Site 3)

The **common fibular (peroneal) nerve** winds around the **neck of the fibula**, where it is subcutaneous and lies directly on bone. Injury here (fracture, tight cast, trauma) paralyses the dorsiflexors supplied by the **deep fibular nerve** and the evertors supplied by the superficial fibular nerve, causing **foot drop**.

## Key Finding

> Radiograph marks four sites around the knee; the site over the neck of the fibula is the vulnerable point of the common fibular nerve.

## Trigger Point

> Injury at a marked site causes failure of **dorsiflexion** = common fibular nerve injury.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Site 2**
This marks another region around the distal femur/proximal tibia, away from the fibular neck.
Injury here would not selectively damage the nerve to the dorsiflexors.

**C. Site 1**
This lies away from the lateral side of the proximal fibula.
Nothing at this site is in direct contact with the common fibular nerve.

**D. Site 4**
This is not the point where the nerve winds around bone.
The common fibular nerve is not at risk here.

## Mind Capsule

> Fibular neck fracture = common fibular nerve injury = **foot drop** (loss of dorsiflexion and eversion) with a high-stepping gait.
