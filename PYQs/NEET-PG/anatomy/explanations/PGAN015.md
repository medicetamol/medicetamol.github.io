# Left 6th Aortic Arch (PDA)

A **patent ductus arteriosus (PDA)** is a persistent channel between the **pulmonary trunk (left pulmonary artery) and the descending aorta**. The ductus arteriosus is derived from the **distal part of the left 6th aortic arch**, and it normally closes functionally within days of birth.

## Key Finding

> Persistent channel connecting the pulmonary artery to the aorta = **PDA**.

## Trigger Point

> Abnormal development of which aortic arch branch leads to this defect.

## Derivatives of the Aortic Arches

| Arch | Derivative |
|---|---|
| 1st | Maxillary artery |
| 2nd | Hyoid and stapedial arteries |
| 3rd | Common carotid and proximal internal carotid |
| 4th | **Left:** arch of aorta <br>**Right:** proximal right subclavian artery |
| 6th | **Left:** proximal pulmonary artery + ductus arteriosus <br>**Right:** proximal right pulmonary artery only |

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Right 4th aortic arch**
It forms the proximal part of the right subclavian artery.
Its anomalies cause aberrant right subclavian artery, not PDA.

**C. Right 6th aortic arch**
It contributes to the proximal right pulmonary artery; its distal portion regresses.
It does not form a ductus.

**D. Left 4th aortic arch**
It forms the definitive arch of the aorta.
Its defects cause coarctation or interruption of the aortic arch, not PDA.

## Mind Capsule

> PDA = failure of closure of the ductus arteriosus (left 6th arch).
> Ligamentum arteriosum is its adult remnant.
