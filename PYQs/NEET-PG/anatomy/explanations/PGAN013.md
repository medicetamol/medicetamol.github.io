# Saphenous Nerve

The **great saphenous vein** accompanies the **saphenous nerve** (the longest branch of the femoral nerve) along the medial side of the leg below the knee. During vein harvest, the nerve can be injured, causing **neuralgia or numbness on the medial leg and medial side of the foot**.

## Trigger Point

> Great saphenous vein graft harvested + neuralgia on the **medial leg and foot**.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Femoral vein**
It is a deep vein and not a nerve; it cannot produce neuralgia.
It is not used for harvesting or injured in a saphenous graft.

**C. Profunda femoris vein**
A deep vein of the thigh draining the posterior and lateral thigh muscles.
It is a vein, not a nerve, and is not near the harvest site.

**D. Popliteal vein**
A deep vein behind the knee that continues as the femoral vein.
It does not cause neuralgia and is not related to the harvest.

## Mind Capsule

> Great saphenous vein = saphenous **nerve** (medial leg and foot); small saphenous vein = **sural** nerve (lateral foot).
