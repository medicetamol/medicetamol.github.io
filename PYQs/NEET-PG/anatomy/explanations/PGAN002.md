# Lateral Pterygoid

The **lateral pterygoid** (superior head) inserts into the **articular disc and capsule** of the temporomandibular joint, along with the pterygoid fovea of the mandibular neck. It pulls the disc and condyle forward during mouth opening. **Overactivity or incoordination** during a wide yawn can pull the condyle too far anterior to the articular tubercle, causing **anterior dislocation (locked jaw)**.

## Trigger Point

> Jaw locked while yawning + muscle attached to the **articular disc** of the TMJ.

## Muscles of Mastication

All four are supplied by the **mandibular nerve (V3)**.

| Muscle | Insertion | Main action |
|---|---|---|
| **Masseter** | Lateral surface of ramus and angle of mandible | Elevates mandible (closes mouth) |
| **Temporalis** | Coronoid process and anterior border of ramus | Elevates (anterior fibres) and retracts (posterior fibres) mandible |
| **Medial pterygoid** | Medial surface of angle and ramus | Elevates mandible, assists in protrusion and side-to-side grinding |
| **Lateral pterygoid** | Superior head: **articular disc and capsule of TMJ**; inferior head: pterygoid fovea of mandibular neck | **Opens mouth** (depresses), protrudes mandible, side-to-side grinding |

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Temporalis**
Inserts on the **coronoid process** of the mandible. It elevates and retracts the mandible, with no attachment to the disc.

**C. Medial pterygoid**
Inserts on the **medial surface of the angle and ramus** of the mandible. It elevates the mandible and does not attach to the disc.

**D. Masseter**
Inserts on the **lateral surface of the ramus and angle** of the mandible. It is a powerful elevator with no attachment to the disc.

## Mind Capsule

> Only the **lateral pterygoid** attaches to the TMJ disc.
> It is the only muscle of mastication that **opens** the mouth, and its overactivity can cause **anterior dislocation**.
