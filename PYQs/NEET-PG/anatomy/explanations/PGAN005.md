# Median Umbilical Ligament

The **urachus** (allantois remnant) connects the apex of the fetal bladder to the umbilicus. After birth it obliterates to form the **median umbilical ligament**, which runs on the deep surface of the anterior abdominal wall along the midline.

## Trigger Point

> Structure marked 4 lies in the midline, in the connecting-stalk/umbilical region = **urachus (allantois)**.

## Fate of Fetal Umbilical Structures

| Fetal structure | Adult remnant |
|---|---|
| Urachus (allantois) | Median umbilical ligament |
| Umbilical artery (distal part) | Medial umbilical ligament |
| Umbilical vein | Ligamentum teres hepatis |
| Ductus venosus | Ligamentum venosum |
| Ductus arteriosus | Ligamentum arteriosum |

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Medial umbilical ligament**
It is the obliterated distal part of the **umbilical artery**, one on each side.
It is paired and lateral to the midline, so it is not the urachus.

**C. Meckel's diverticulum**
It is a remnant of the **vitellointestinal (vitelline) duct**, seen on the ileum.
The vitelline duct is a different structure from the allantois/urachus.

**D. Ligamentum teres**
It is the remnant of the **left umbilical vein**, running in the free margin of the falciform ligament.
It carries oxygenated blood in fetal life and has no relation to the urachus.

## Mind Capsule

> Urachus = **median** umbilical ligament (midline, single); umbilical arteries = **medial** umbilical ligaments (paired).
> Patent urachus leaks urine at the umbilicus.
