# Enterobius vermicularis

***Enterobius vermicularis*** (pinworm) causes **nocturnal perianal itching**, mainly in children. Eggs are **D-shaped (planoconvex)** with a thin shell and a developing larva, and are best detected by the **cellophane (adhesive tape) swab** in the morning.

## Key Finding

> Oval egg with one flattened side, thin shell and developing larva = *Enterobius* egg.

## Trigger Point

> **Nocturnal perianal itching** + adhesive tape test.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Ancylostoma duodenale**
Eggs are ovoid with a thin shell and **4-8 cell stage blastomeres**.
Hookworm causes anaemia and is diagnosed in stool.

**C. Hymenolepis nana**
Eggs are round with a **hexacanth embryo and polar filaments**.
It is found in stool and does not cause perianal itching.

**D. Trichuris trichiura**
Eggs are **barrel-shaped (lemon-shaped) with bipolar plugs**.
They appear in stool, not on tape.

## Mind Capsule

> Perianal itching + D-shaped egg + tape test = **pinworm**; treat with albendazole or mebendazole for family.
