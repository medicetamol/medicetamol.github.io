# Detection of Microfilariae in Blood Smear

After a positive **filarial antigen test** (e.g. ICT card for *W. bancrofti* antigen), the standard confirmation is demonstrating **microfilariae in a peripheral blood smear**, which also identifies the species. For *W. bancrofti* the blood must be collected at **night (10 PM-2 AM)** because of nocturnal periodicity.

## Trigger Point

> Fever + positive filarial antigen test, next step to confirm and identify the parasite.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Bone marrow biopsy**
Not used in filariasis, as microfilariae are in peripheral blood.
It is invasive and adds nothing.

**B. DEC provocation test**
Diethylcarbamazine can bring microfilariae into the blood during the day, but it is not preferred.
It can provoke reactions and is not routine.

**D. Ultrasound of the scrotum**
Shows the **filarial dance sign** in adult worms in the lymphatics.
It is not the next step to identify the organism.

## Mind Capsule

> Filariasis = nocturnal blood smear for microfilariae (gold standard); antigen test (ICT) is a screening tool.
