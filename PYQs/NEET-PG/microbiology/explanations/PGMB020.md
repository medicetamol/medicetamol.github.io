# Streptococcus pneumoniae

**Streptococcus pneumoniae** is the **commonest cause of bacterial (lobar) pneumonia**, including in patients with HIV, in whom it is more frequent and severe. This picture of a **right lower lobe consolidation with productive cough and fever** fits bacterial lobar pneumonia, even with CD4 55/µL.

## Trigger Point

> Productive cough, fever, **lobar consolidation** on X-ray.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Staphylococcus aureus**
Causes pneumonia after influenza or in IV drug users, with cavitation or pneumatoceles.
It is less common than the pneumococcus.

**C. Mycoplasma**
Causes atypical pneumonia with dry cough and patchy infiltrates.
It is not typically lobar.

**D. Pneumocystis jirovecii**
A common AIDS opportunist at CD4 < 200, but it gives **bilateral diffuse interstitial infiltrates** with dry cough.
It does not typically produce lobar consolidation.

## Mind Capsule

> Lobar consolidation + productive cough = pneumococcus (even in HIV); diffuse bilateral ground-glass with dry cough = PCP.
