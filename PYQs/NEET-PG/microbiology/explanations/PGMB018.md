# Dark-field Microscopy

A **painless, demarcated genital ulcer (chancre) with inguinal lymphadenopathy** points to **primary syphilis**. ***Treponema pallidum*** is too thin (0.2 µm) to be seen by ordinary light microscopy, so **dark-field microscopy** is used to see its **corkscrew motility** in a fresh chancre exudate.

## Trigger Point

> Painless indurated ulcer on penis + best method to see **motility**.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Light microscopy**
Spirochaetes are too thin to resolve unless they are stained with silver.
Motility cannot be seen.

**C. Fluorescent microscopy**
Direct fluorescent antibody (DFA-TP) can detect the organism but not its motility.
It uses fixed smears.

**D. Electron microscopy**
It can show the structure but needs fixed specimens.
It cannot demonstrate motility in routine practice.

## Mind Capsule

> Chancre = **dark-field microscopy** for motile *T. pallidum*; serology (VDRL, TPHA) in later stages.
