# Antigen Detection in Stool

**Rotavirus** infection is diagnosed by detecting **viral antigen in the stool**, commonly by **ELISA/EIA** or rapid immunochromatographic tests. PCR and electron microscopy are also used, but antigen detection is the standard routine method.

## Trigger Point

> Laboratory test to diagnose **rotavirus diarrhoea**.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Antigen detection in blood**
Rotavirus is a gut-restricted infection, and viral antigen is not routinely measured in blood.
It is not the usual test.

**C. Antibody detection in serum**
Antibodies appear late and do not distinguish current from past infection.
They are not used for diagnosis.

**D. Light microscopy of stool**
The virus is far too small to see with light microscopy.
Electron microscopy shows the **wheel-shaped** virions.

## Mind Capsule

> Rotavirus = stool **antigen (ELISA)**; EM shows wheel-like particles; vaccine is oral live attenuated.
