# Prozone Phenomenon

In the **prozone phenomenon**, **excess antibody** relative to antigen prevents lattice formation, giving a **false-negative agglutination** result. **Diluting the serum** brings antibody and antigen into an optimum ratio, so agglutination appears.

## Trigger Point

> Standard agglutination test **negative initially, positive after dilution**.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Postzone**
Due to **antigen excess**, which would be corrected by diluting the antigen or adding antibody.
Diluting the serum here corrects antibody excess.

**C. Complement inactivation**
Not relevant to agglutination; it matters in complement fixation tests.
Dilution would not change the outcome.

**D. Antigen-antibody complexes**
Soluble complexes form in antigen excess or equivalence zones.
They do not explain the effect of diluting serum.

## Mind Capsule

> **Pro**zone = **Pro**tein (antibody) excess = false negative, fixed by dilution; postzone = antigen excess.
