# Lymphatic Obstruction

The smear shows a **microfilaria**, so the diagnosis is **lymphatic filariasis** (*W. bancrofti*). Adult worms live in lymphatic vessels and cause **inflammation and fibrosis**, leading to **lymphatic obstruction, lymphoedema and elephantiasis**, and hydrocele.

## Key Finding

> Thread-like coiled organism with tapering ends on a blood smear = **microfilaria**.

## Trigger Point

> Progressive limb swelling in an endemic region + microfilariae.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Hypoalbuminaemia**
Causes generalized pitting oedema (e.g. nephrotic syndrome, liver failure).
It does not explain progressive unilateral limb swelling with microfilariae.

**C. Hypoproteinaemia**
Gives low oncotic pressure with generalized oedema.
It is not the cause in filariasis.

**D. Increased hydrostatic pressure**
Seen in heart failure and venous obstruction.
In filariasis, the lymphatics are blocked rather than veins.

## Mind Capsule

> Filariasis = adult worms block lymphatics = **lymphoedema / elephantiasis / hydrocele**.
