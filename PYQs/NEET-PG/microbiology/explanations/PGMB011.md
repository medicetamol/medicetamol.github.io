# Ceftriaxone

Purulent urethral discharge with **intracellular gram-negative diplococci** indicates **gonorrhoea** (*Neisseria gonorrhoeae*). Due to widespread resistance, the recommended treatment is **ceftriaxone** (usually with attention to local guidelines; azithromycin is often added in some regimens to cover chlamydia).

## Trigger Point

> Urethral discharge after unprotected sex + Gram stain = **gonococcus**.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Erythromycin**
A macrolide that is not recommended for gonorrhoea because of resistance.
It is used for other infections such as chlamydia in pregnancy.

**B. Azithromycin**
Used for chlamydia and sometimes as an add-on in gonorrhoea.
It is not the single drug of choice.

**D. Penicillin G**
Widespread penicillinase production and chromosomal resistance make it ineffective.
It is not used empirically.

## Mind Capsule

> Gonorrhoea = **ceftriaxone** (± azithromycin or doxycycline for chlamydia co-infection).
