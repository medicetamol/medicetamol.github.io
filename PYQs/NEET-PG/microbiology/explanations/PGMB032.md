# Aspergillosis

***Aspergillus*** shows **septate hyphae with acute-angle (45°) dichotomous branching**. Invasive pulmonary aspergillosis in immunocompromised patients gives fever, cough, **haemoptysis** and a halo sign on CT.

## Trigger Point

> **Septate hyphae with acute-angle dichotomous branching** in BAL.

## Mucor vs Aspergillus

| Feature | *Aspergillus* | *Mucor* |
|---|---|---|
| Hyphae | Septate | Aseptate (ribbon-like) |
| Branching | Acute angle (45°) | Wide angle (90°) |

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Mucormycosis**
Shows **broad, aseptate** hyphae with right-angle branching.
It is associated with diabetic ketoacidosis and rhinocerebral disease.

**B. Histoplasmosis**
Seen as small intracellular yeasts, not hyphae in tissue.
It does not form septate branching hyphae in BAL.

**D. Candidiasis**
Appears as budding yeasts and **pseudohyphae**.
It is not acute-angle dichotomous true hyphae.

## Mind Capsule

> Acute angle + septate = **Aspergillus**; wide angle + aseptate = Mucor.
