# Malignant Hyperthermia

**Malignant hyperthermia** is a life-threatening pharmacogenetic disorder (mutations in the **ryanodine receptor, RYR1**) triggered by **volatile anaesthetics and succinylcholine**, causing uncontrolled calcium release in skeletal muscle. This produces a hypermetabolic crisis: **rising EtCO2** (the earliest and most sensitive sign), **tachycardia, hyperthermia, and hypertension**, along with muscle rigidity. It is a surgical anaesthesia emergency treated with **dantrolene**.

## Trigger Point

> **Rising EtCO2 + high fever + tachycardia + hypertension**, appearing during general anaesthesia: the classic hypermetabolic picture of malignant hyperthermia.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Heat Stroke**
Results from environmental/exertional heat exposure with failure of thermoregulation, not from anaesthetic drug exposure in an operating theatre.
Does not explain a sudden intraoperative rise in EtCO2 tied to anaesthesia.

**C. Heat Cramps**
A mild, self-limited condition from salt/water depletion during exertion in heat, with no systemic hypermetabolic crisis.
Far too mild and unrelated to the anaesthesia setting described.

**D. Heat Pyrexia**
Not a standard, distinct clinical entity in this context, and does not account for the combination of rising EtCO2, tachycardia and hypertension under anaesthesia.
Does not match the described intraoperative hypermetabolic picture.

## Mind Capsule

> Rising EtCO2 + fever + tachycardia + hypertension under general anaesthesia = malignant hyperthermia = give dantrolene.
