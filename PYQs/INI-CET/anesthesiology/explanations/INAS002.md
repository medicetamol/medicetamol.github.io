# 1, 2, and 4

**Halothane** causes direct **myocardial depression**, reducing cardiac output (statement 1, true). After induction, blood pressure typically **falls initially and then plateaus** at a lower level as anaesthetic depth stabilises, rather than falling indefinitely (statement 2, true; statement 3, false). Halothane also **sensitises the myocardium to circulating catecholamines**, increasing the risk of arrhythmias, especially with exogenous adrenaline (statement 4, true).

## Statement Check

| Statement | True/False | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Direct myocardial depression, reduced cardiac output | True | Halothane's principal cardiodepressant action |
| 2. BP falls initially, then plateaus at a lower level | True | Reflects stabilisation once anaesthetic depth is constant |
| 3. BP progressively falls throughout surgery regardless of depth | **False** | BP plateaus rather than falling indefinitely |
| 4. Increases catecholamine sensitivity, raising arrhythmia risk | True | The basis for avoiding/limiting adrenaline with halothane |

## Trigger Point

> Identify the **one false** statement (progressive, depth-independent BP fall) to select the correct combination: 1, 2 and 4.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. 1, 3, 4**
Includes the false statement 3 and omits the true statement 2 (initial fall with a subsequent plateau).
Both an incorrect inclusion and a missing correct statement.

**B. 1, 2, 3**
Includes the false statement 3 in place of the true statement 4 (catecholamine sensitisation).
Swaps a true statement for a false one.

**D. 2, 3, 4**
Includes the false statement 3 and omits the true statement 1 (direct myocardial depression), a foundational effect of halothane.
Both an incorrect inclusion and a missing correct statement.

## Mind Capsule

> Halothane: myocardial depression + BP falls then plateaus + catecholamine sensitisation — BP does NOT fall indefinitely regardless of depth.
