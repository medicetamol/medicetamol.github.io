# Ketamine

**Ketamine** is relatively unique among anaesthetic/sedative agents in that it **preserves spontaneous respiratory drive and airway reflexes** even at anaesthetic doses, making significant respiratory depression uncommon even in overdose (though rapid IV administration or combination with other depressants can still impair breathing). It also maintains blood pressure via sympathetic stimulation, unlike classical CNS depressants.

## Trigger Point

> **Drug that does NOT typically cause respiratory depression in overdose**: the exception among sedative-hypnotics/opioids is ketamine.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Phenobarbitone**
A barbiturate that potentiates GABA-A receptors; overdose causes classic dose-dependent **respiratory depression** via medullary suppression.
A well-recognised cause of respiratory failure in overdose.

**B. Opioids**
Act on mu-opioid receptors in the brainstem respiratory centre, causing direct, dose-dependent **respiratory depression** — the classic mechanism of opioid overdose death.
One of the most common causes of fatal respiratory depression.

**C. Thiopentone**
An ultra-short-acting barbiturate; like other barbiturates, overdose causes significant **respiratory depression** and apnoea, especially with rapid IV bolus.
A recognised cause of respiratory compromise in overdose.

## Mind Capsule

> Ketamine uniquely preserves airway reflexes and respiratory drive, unlike barbiturates and opioids, which classically cause respiratory depression in overdose.
