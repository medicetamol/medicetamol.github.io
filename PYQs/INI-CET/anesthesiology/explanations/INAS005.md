# Dose-Dependent Fall in Cardiac Output and Blood Pressure

**Halothane** causes direct **myocardial depression**, producing a **dose-dependent fall in cardiac output and blood pressure** (often around a 20 to 30 mmHg drop at surgical anaesthetic depth), without the compensatory tachycardia seen with some other volatile agents; it also **sensitises the myocardium to catecholamines**, predisposing to arrhythmias.

## Drug Facts

| Drug | Key cardiovascular effect |
|---|---|
| **Halothane** | **Dose-dependent fall in CO and BP (myocardial depression); catecholamine sensitisation/arrhythmia risk** |

## Trigger Point

> **Most characteristic cardiovascular effect of halothane**: direct myocardial depression lowering CO and BP.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Increase in Cardiac Output with Reflex Tachycardia**
This pattern is more typical of agents like **isoflurane**, which causes vasodilation with reflex tachycardia.
Halothane does not typically produce reflex tachycardia; it may even cause bradycardia.

**C. Rise in Systemic Vascular Resistance with Hypertension**
Halothane generally causes **vasodilation and hypotension**, not a rise in SVR or hypertension.
The opposite of halothane's known haemodynamic profile.

**D. No Measurable Effect on CO or BP**
Halothane has one of the more pronounced cardiodepressant profiles among volatile anaesthetics, so this statement is inaccurate.
A dose-dependent fall is well documented and characteristic.

## Mind Capsule

> Halothane = myocardial depressant (falls in CO/BP) + catecholamine sensitisation (arrhythmia risk), unlike isoflurane's vasodilation/reflex tachycardia.
