# +14 D

The **amplitude of accommodation** is highest in childhood and falls steadily with age as the lens loses elasticity, eventually causing **presbyopia**. By **Duane's table**, a child of about 7 to 10 years has an amplitude of roughly **14 D**; in middle age it falls to the range of about 4 to 6 D. Exact values differ slightly between tables.

## Trigger Point

> **Amplitude of accommodation in a 7-year-old child**: the childhood value on Duane's table.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. +10 D**
A value typical of young adulthood (around the twenties by Duane's table).
Too low for a young child.

**C. +5 D**
A value seen in the mid-forties, at the onset of presbyopia.
Far too low for a child.

**D. +2 D**
A value seen around 50 years of age or beyond.
Not seen in a child.

## Mind Capsule

> Accommodation is maximal in childhood (about 14 D or more) and falls with age until presbyopia sets in at around 40.
