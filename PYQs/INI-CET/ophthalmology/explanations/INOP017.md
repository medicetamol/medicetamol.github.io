# Endophthalmitis with Propionibacterium

**Delayed-onset (chronic, low-grade) postoperative endophthalmitis** is caused by organisms of low virulence such as **Propionibacterium acnes**, which lodge in the capsular bag. It presents weeks after surgery with reduced vision, anterior chamber cells and flare, and vitreous haze.

## Trigger Point

> Reduced vision 2 weeks after cataract surgery, with AC cells, flare and a hazy vitreous.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Endophthalmitis with Staphylococcus**
Typically acute, appearing within days of surgery with marked pain and hypopyon.

**C. Sympathetic ophthalmia**
Bilateral granulomatous uveitis after penetrating injury or surgery to one eye, and it is not an infection.

**D. Toxic anterior segment syndrome**
Sterile inflammation, appearing within 12–48 hours of surgery, with no vitreous involvement.

## Mind Capsule

> Weeks after cataract surgery + vitreous haze = delayed endophthalmitis (*P. acnes*).
