# Neonatal Lupus Erythematosus

**Transplacental transfer of maternal anti-Ro (SS-A) and anti-La (SS-B) antibodies** causes **neonatal lupus erythematosus**, with cutaneous annular rash, cytopenias, and — most seriously — **congenital complete heart block** from antibody-mediated damage to the fetal conducting system.

## Trigger Point

> **Maternal anti-Ro/anti-La antibodies crossing the placenta**: the classic cause of neonatal lupus with heart block.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Neonatal Myasthenia Gravis**
Caused by transplacental transfer of maternal **anti-acetylcholine receptor antibodies**, causing transient neonatal hypotonia and weakness, not heart block.
A different antibody and a different clinical picture.

**C. Haemolytic Disease of the Newborn**
Caused by maternal **anti-Rh (or other blood group) antibodies** against fetal red cells, causing haemolysis and jaundice.
Unrelated to anti-Ro/La antibodies.

**D. Neonatal Graves' (Thyrotoxicosis)**
Caused by transplacental **TSH-receptor stimulating antibodies** from a mother with Graves' disease.
A distinct antibody and distinct clinical syndrome from neonatal lupus.

## Mind Capsule

> Anti-Ro/La antibodies = neonatal lupus + congenital complete heart block.
