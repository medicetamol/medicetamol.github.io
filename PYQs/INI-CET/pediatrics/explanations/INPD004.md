# PDA with Reversed (Eisenmenger) Shunt

In a **PDA with pulmonary hypertension causing shunt reversal (right to left)**, deoxygenated blood enters the descending aorta **distal to the origin of the arch vessels**, so the **upper limbs stay pink** (supplied by the aortic arch before the ductus) while the **lower limbs become cyanosed** — classic **differential cyanosis**.

## Trigger Point

> **Pink upper limbs, cyanosed lower limbs**: differential cyanosis, specific to reversed-shunt PDA.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Osler-Weber-Rendu Syndrome**
A disorder of arteriovenous malformations causing recurrent bleeding (epistaxis, GI bleeds), unrelated to differential cyanosis.
Not associated with this cardiac shunting pattern.

**C. Peripheral Arterial Disease**
Causes localized limb ischaemia or pallor from vascular occlusion, not a proximal-versus-distal cyanosis pattern from a cardiac shunt.
A vascular, not a cardiac shunting, mechanism.

**D. Transposition of Great Arteries (Simple)**
Causes generalised cyanosis from birth, not a differential pattern between upper and lower limbs.
Uniform, not segmental, cyanosis is seen here.

## Mind Capsule

> Differential cyanosis (pink arms, blue legs) = PDA with reversed (right-to-left) shunt, i.e., Eisenmenger PDA.
