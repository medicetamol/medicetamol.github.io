# Listeria monocytogenes

In **neonates**, particularly those around **1 to 3 months**, common causes of bacterial meningitis are **Group B Streptococcus, E. coli, and Listeria monocytogenes**. Among the given options, **Listeria** is the classic neonatal-period organism, transmitted transplacentally or through contaminated food consumed by the mother, and requires **ampicillin** (not cephalosporins alone) for coverage.

## Trigger Point

> **6-week-old infant with bacterial meningitis**: think of neonatal-specific organisms, notably Listeria.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Staphylococcus aureus**
More associated with meningitis after neurosurgical procedures, shunts, or skin/soft-tissue source, not typical primary neonatal meningitis.
Not the classic organism for this age group.

**B. Streptococcus pneumoniae**
A leading cause of meningitis in **older infants and children** (post-neonatal), not the classic first weeks of life pathogen highlighted here.
More typical beyond the neonatal period.

**C. Haemophilus influenzae**
Historically important in children **older than 2 months to 5 years**, now much reduced due to Hib vaccination.
Not the classic neonatal (under 1 month to early weeks) pathogen.

## Mind Capsule

> Neonatal meningitis = think GBS, E. coli, and Listeria; add ampicillin to empirical cover for Listeria.
