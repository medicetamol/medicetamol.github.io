# Transient Tachypnea of Newborn

**TTN** results from delayed clearance of fetal lung fluid, usually in **late preterm or term neonates**, and settles within 24–72 hours. Chest X-ray typically shows prominent perihilar streaking and fluid in the fissures.

## Quick Comparison

| Condition | Typical setting | X-ray features |
|---|---|---|
| TTN | Late preterm or term, often caesarean | Perihilar streaking, fluid in fissures |
| RDS | Preterm (usually < 34 weeks) | Ground-glass opacity, air bronchograms |
| Meconium aspiration | Term or post-term, meconium-stained liquor | Patchy opacities, hyperinflation |
| Neonatal pneumonia | Infective risk factors | Asymmetric infiltrates |

## Trigger Point

> 36-week neonate with respiratory distress shortly after birth and a chest X-ray.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Respiratory distress syndrome**
Due to surfactant deficiency, usually in more premature infants, with ground-glass appearance.

**C. Neonatal pneumonia**
Needs risk factors for sepsis and shows asymmetric infiltrates.

**D. Meconium aspiration syndrome**
Occurs in term or post-term babies with meconium-stained liquor.

## Mind Capsule

> Late preterm or term + early respiratory distress that settles in 1–3 days = TTN.
