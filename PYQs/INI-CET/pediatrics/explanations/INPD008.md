# The Neonate is Asymptomatic

In a term neonate with **blood glucose 36 mg/dL who is asymptomatic and feeding well**, current guidance favours **continued breastfeeding with close monitoring** rather than immediate IV dextrose, since transient, mild, asymptomatic hypoglycaemia in an otherwise well feeding neonate is common and usually self-limited. **Symptomatic** hypoglycaemia (jitteriness, lethargy, seizures) mandates urgent IV dextrose.

## Trigger Point

> **Feeding well, no clinical signs**: asymptomatic status favours conservative management with breastfeeding.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Symptomatic with Jitteriness**
Jitteriness is a **sign of symptomatic** hypoglycaemia, which would instead favour prompt IV dextrose, not observation.
This finding argues against continued breastfeeding alone.

**C. Having Seizures**
Seizures indicate a **neurological emergency**, requiring immediate IV dextrose correction.
This is an indication for urgent treatment, not conservative feeding.

**D. Lethargic and Not Feeding**
An infant who is lethargic and feeding poorly is symptomatic and cannot be safely managed with oral feeds alone.
This favours IV dextrose, not observation.

## Mind Capsule

> Asymptomatic neonatal hypoglycaemia with good feeding = breastfeed and monitor; symptomatic = IV dextrose.
