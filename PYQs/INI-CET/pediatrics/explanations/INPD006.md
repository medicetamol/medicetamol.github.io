# Café-au-Lait Macules

**Neurofibromatosis type 1** presents earliest with **café-au-lait macules** (flat, light-brown patches), often the first visible sign in infancy, well before Lisch nodules (iris hamartomas, seen on slit-lamp), neurofibromas, or axillary/inguinal freckling appear.

## Key Finding

> Slit-lamp view shows Lisch nodules (iris hamartomas); skin photograph shows café-au-lait macules, in a child with NF1.

## Trigger Point

> **Earliest and most characteristic cutaneous lesion** in NF1: café-au-lait macules.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Ash-Leaf (Hypopigmented) Macules**
Characteristic of **tuberous sclerosis**, not NF1.
A different phakomatosis altogether.

**C. Port-Wine Stain**
Characteristic of **Sturge-Weber syndrome**.
Not a feature of NF1.

**D. Shagreen Patch**
Also a feature of **tuberous sclerosis** (connective tissue nevus over the lumbosacral area).
Not associated with NF1.

## Mind Capsule

> NF1 skin sign, earliest = café-au-lait macules; tuberous sclerosis = ash-leaf macules and shagreen patch; Sturge-Weber = port-wine stain.
