# Staphylococcus aureus

In an infant with **prolonged NICU stay**, meningitis is **late-onset and hospital-acquired**. **Nosocomial pathogens** such as *S. aureus*, **coagulase-negative staphylococci** and **Gram-negative bacilli** are the likely causes, often related to catheters and invasive procedures.

## Trigger Point

> **6-week-old**, hospitalised **since birth** (prematurity, prolonged NICU stay) with meningitis.

## Neonatal Meningitis: Common Organisms

| Setting | Organisms |
|---|---|
| Early onset and community late onset | Group B *Streptococcus*, *E. coli*, *Listeria* |
| Nosocomial (NICU) | *S. aureus*, coagulase-negative staphylococci, Gram-negative bacilli |

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Haemophilus influenzae**
A cause in older unvaccinated infants and children, now uncommon after Hib vaccination.

**B. Streptococcus pneumoniae**
Typical in community-acquired meningitis of older infants and children.

**D. Listeria monocytogenes**
Usually acquired from the mother, causing early-onset or community late-onset disease.

## Mind Capsule

> Long NICU stay + late-onset meningitis = **hospital-acquired organisms** (*S. aureus*, coagulase-negative staphylococci, Gram-negatives).
