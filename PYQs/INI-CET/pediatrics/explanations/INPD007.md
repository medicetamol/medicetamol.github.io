# Purulent Conjunctivitis

**Kawasaki disease** classically causes **bilateral, non-exudative (non-purulent) conjunctival injection**. The presence of **purulent** discharge argues against Kawasaki disease and should prompt consideration of an infective conjunctivitis instead.

## Trigger Point

> **NOT a feature of Kawasaki disease**: the option describing purulent, rather than non-exudative, conjunctivitis.

## Why the Others Are True

**B. Bilateral Non-Exudative Conjunctival Injection**
This is one of the principal diagnostic criteria of Kawasaki disease.
A genuine, classic feature.

**C. Strawberry Tongue and Cracked Lips**
Part of the oral mucosal changes included in the diagnostic criteria.
A well-recognised true feature.

**D. Cervical Lymphadenopathy**
Usually unilateral, greater than 1.5 cm, and one of the principal criteria (though the least consistently present).
A recognised diagnostic feature.

## Mind Capsule

> Kawasaki conjunctivitis is bilateral and non-exudative; purulent discharge is NOT typical.
