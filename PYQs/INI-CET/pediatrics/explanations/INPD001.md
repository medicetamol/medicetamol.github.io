# Speech and Hearing Evaluation

A **20-month-old speaking only one word**, with otherwise normal motor and social development, has an **isolated language delay** (normal expressive language expects around 20 to 50 words with early two-word phrases forming by 18 to 24 months). The correct next step is **speech and hearing evaluation** to rule out hearing loss and characterise the delay, not blanket reassurance.

## Trigger Point

> **Only one word at 20 months** with normal motor/social domains: isolated speech delay needing evaluation.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Reassure the Parents**
Reassurance without evaluation risks missing a treatable cause, such as hearing impairment.
An isolated delay this marked warrants assessment, not dismissal.

**C. Diagnose as Autism**
Autism requires impairment across **social communication** domains, not isolated language delay with normal social skills.
A single delayed domain does not meet the criteria for autism.

**D. Reassess at 5 Years of Age**
Waiting years would miss the critical early window for intervention in speech/hearing disorders.
Early evaluation and intervention give the best outcomes.

## Mind Capsule

> Isolated language delay at any age of concern = speech and hearing evaluation first, not reassurance or a label.
