# Rett Syndrome

**Rett syndrome** (an **MECP2** mutation, almost exclusively in **girls**) shows normal early development followed by **regression between about 6 and 18 months**, with **deceleration of head growth (acquired microcephaly)**, loss of purposeful hand use, and characteristic **stereotyped hand-wringing movements**.

## Trigger Point

> **Girl + regression + decelerating head growth + hand-wringing stereotypies**: the classic Rett syndrome picture.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Childhood Disintegrative Disorder (Heller Syndrome)**
Regression occurs **later** (usually after 2 years of normal development) and affects **both sexes**, without the characteristic hand-wringing or head growth deceleration.
The age and sex pattern, and the specific hand stereotypy, don't match.

**C. Classic Autism Spectrum Disorder**
Typically shows early (often before 3 years) impairment without a period of clearly normal development followed by regression, and without deceleration of head growth.
The regression pattern and hand-wringing are specific to Rett, not typical autism.

**D. Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder**
A disorder of inattention and/or hyperactivity, without developmental regression or hand stereotypies.
Does not fit this clinical picture at all.

## Mind Capsule

> Girl + normal development then regression + decelerating head growth + hand-wringing = Rett syndrome (MECP2).
