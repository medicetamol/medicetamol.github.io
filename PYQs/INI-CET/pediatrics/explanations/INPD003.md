# Congenital Hypothyroidism

**Congenital hypothyroidism** classically presents in the neonatal period with **prolonged (unconjugated) jaundice, umbilical hernia**, along with other subtle signs such as lethargy, poor feeding, macroglossia and constipation. Early detection (often via newborn screening) and treatment with levothyroxine prevent irreversible neurodevelopmental delay.

## Trigger Point

> **Prolonged neonatal jaundice + umbilical hernia**: the classic combination pointing to congenital hypothyroidism.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Down Syndrome**
Presents with characteristic dysmorphic facial features and hypotonia; while umbilical hernia can occur, prolonged jaundice is not a defining feature.
The combination given is more specific to hypothyroidism.

**C. Cerebral Palsy**
A motor disorder from a static brain insult, not typically presenting with neonatal jaundice or umbilical hernia.
Does not explain this combination of findings.

**D. Biliary Atresia**
Causes **conjugated** (direct) hyperbilirubinemia with pale stools and dark urine, not the mechanism implied here, and is not linked to umbilical hernia.
The clinical picture does not match biliary atresia.

## Mind Capsule

> Prolonged jaundice + umbilical hernia + macroglossia/lethargy in a neonate = screen for congenital hypothyroidism.
