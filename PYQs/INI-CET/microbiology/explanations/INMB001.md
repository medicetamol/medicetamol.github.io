# Carbapenems Can Be Used for Treatment

**ESBL-producing organisms** hydrolyse penicillins, cephalosporins (including third-generation) and aztreonam, but their beta-lactamases **do not affect carbapenems**, making **carbapenems (imipenem, meropenem) the treatment of choice** for serious ESBL infections. Resistance is chromosomally encoded (not classically plasmid-borne, unlike many other resistance mechanisms), and piperacillin-tazobactam is unreliable for severe infection despite in-vitro susceptibility.

## Trigger Point

> **True statement about ESBL organisms**: which option correctly reflects a therapeutic or mechanistic fact.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Resistance is Plasmid-Transmitted**
ESBL genes are classically described as **chromosomally** located in many texts, distinguishing them from some other resistance mechanisms; this statement does not hold as the keyed correct fact here.
It is commonly a source of confusion but is not the answer selected.

**C. Piperacillin-Tazobactam is the Reliable Drug of Choice**
Despite apparent in-vitro activity, clinical outcome data show it is **less reliable** than carbapenems for serious ESBL infections (the inoculum effect).
It should not be relied upon for severe disease.

**D. Third-Generation Cephalosporins Can Be Used**
ESBLs specifically hydrolyse third-generation cephalosporins, making them **ineffective**, even if in-vitro testing occasionally looks susceptible.
They should be avoided for confirmed ESBL producers.

## Mind Capsule

> ESBL = resistant to penicillins/cephalosporins/aztreonam, but sensitive to carbapenems = drug of choice.
