# Streptococcus pyogenes

**Group A Streptococcus** (*S. pyogenes*) causes **acute rheumatic fever** after pharyngitis, through **molecular mimicry**: antibodies to the streptococcal **M protein** cross-react with cardiac myosin and valve tissue. Repeated attacks scar the valve, most often the **mitral valve**, giving **chronic rheumatic heart disease** with a **fish-mouth (buttonhole) stenotic orifice**.

## Trigger Point

> **Chronic rheumatic heart disease** with a **"fish-mouth" mitral valve**; the scleroderma-like skin findings do not change the organism implicated.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Staphylococcus aureus**
Causes acute, destructive infective endocarditis, not a chronic scarred rheumatic valve.

**C. Streptococcus pneumoniae**
Causes pneumonia, meningitis and otitis media; it is not linked to rheumatic fever.

**D. Corynebacterium diphtheriae**
Its toxin can cause myocarditis, but it does not produce chronic valvular scarring.

## Mind Capsule

> Rheumatic heart disease = **group A Streptococcus** (molecular mimicry); mitral valve most affected.
