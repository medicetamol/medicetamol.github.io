# Monitor Response to Treatment

**Non-treponemal tests (VDRL, RPR)** detect antibodies against cardiolipin and become **non-reactive (or show a falling titre) with successful treatment**, making them useful to **monitor treatment response and detect reinfection/relapse**. They are quantitative but not perfectly standardized, and titres can wane over time even without treatment (unlike treponemal tests, which usually remain positive for life).

## Statement Check

| Statement | True/False | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Monitor treatment response** | **True** | Titres fall with successful therapy |
| B. Standard dilution used | False | Non-treponemal tests are not run at one fixed dilution; they are titrated |
| C. Remain positive for life | False | This describes treponemal tests (TPHA/FTA-ABS), not non-treponemal tests |
| D. Quantitative | True (but not the keyed distinguishing advantage here) | They are reported as titres, which supports monitoring, not a separate benefit |

## Trigger Point

> **Advantage of non-treponemal tests** in follow-up of syphilis: falling titres on therapy.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. A Standard Dilution is Used**
Non-treponemal tests are read as a **titre** across serial dilutions, not a single standard dilution.
This does not describe how they are actually performed.

**C. They Remain Positive for Life**
This is true of **treponemal** tests (TPHA, FTA-ABS), which stay positive even after cure.
Non-treponemal tests typically revert to negative with treatment.

**D. They are Quantitative**
True as a technical feature, but the specific advantage being tested is their use in **monitoring treatment response**, which follows from being quantitative and falling with therapy.
It supports, rather than replaces, option A as the keyed answer.

## Mind Capsule

> Non-treponemal (VDRL/RPR) = quantitative, used to follow treatment; treponemal (TPHA/FTA-ABS) = qualitative, stays positive for life.
