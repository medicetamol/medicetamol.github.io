# Vibrio parahaemolyticus

The **Kanagawa phenomenon** is **β-haemolysis** on Wagatsuma agar by *V. parahaemolyticus*, caused by **thermostable direct haemolysin (TDH)**. It correlates with virulence, as Kanagawa-positive strains cause gastroenteritis (often after raw seafood).

## Trigger Point

> Kanagawa phenomenon on Wagatsuma agar.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Burkholderia mallei**
Causes glanders; it is non-motile, and not associated with Wagatsuma agar.

**B. Vibrio cholerae**
Grown on TCBS or alkaline peptone water, and does not show this phenomenon.

**D. Staphylococcus aureus**
Shows haemolysis on blood agar but not the Kanagawa phenomenon.

## Mind Capsule

> Kanagawa phenomenon + Wagatsuma agar = *V. parahaemolyticus* (TDH, virulent strains).
