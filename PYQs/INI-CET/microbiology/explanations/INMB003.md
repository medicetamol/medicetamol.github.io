# Histoplasmosis

**Histoplasma capsulatum** causes a chronic pulmonary illness that mimics tuberculosis: cough, fever, weight loss and cavitary lesions, with granulomatous inflammation showing caseous necrosis on histology. In India it is classically linked to endemic pockets such as the **Gangetic plains and Northeast (including Assam)**, often near bat or bird droppings.

## Trigger Point

> **Assam + chronic cavitary lung disease with caseating granulomas**: an endemic clue pointing beyond ordinary TB.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Sarcoidosis**
Causes non-caseating granulomas, typically with bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy rather than cavitation.
The caseous necrosis described argues against sarcoidosis.

**B. Aspergillosis**
Typically causes an aspergilloma within a pre-existing cavity or invasive disease in immunosuppression, with septate hyphae rather than granulomatous caseating inflammation.
The pathology described here fits a dimorphic fungal or mycobacterial pattern, not Aspergillus.

**D. Granulomatosis with Polyangiitis**
An ANCA-associated vasculitis causing necrotising granulomas, often with renal and sinus involvement.
The regional/endemic clue and isolated pulmonary cavitary picture favour an infectious cause.

## Mind Capsule

> Chronic cavitary lung disease + caseating granulomas in an endemic area, once TB is excluded = think histoplasmosis.
