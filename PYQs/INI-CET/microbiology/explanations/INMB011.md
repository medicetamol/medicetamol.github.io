# NaOH with NALC

Sputum samples for TB culture are treated with **N-acetyl-L-cysteine (NALC) combined with sodium hydroxide (NaOH)**: NALC liquefies the mucus (breaking disulfide bonds), while NaOH decontaminates the sample by killing faster-growing commensal/contaminant organisms, sparing the slower-growing, more resistant mycobacteria.

## Trigger Point

> **Digestion and decontamination of sputum for TB culture**: the standard reagent combination is NaOH with NALC.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. HCl with NAC**
Hydrochloric acid is not the standard decontaminating alkali used for sputum processing; the accepted alkali is sodium hydroxide.
Does not match standard mycobacteriology protocol.

**B. H2SO4 with NAC**
Sulfuric acid is used in some historical or alternative digestion methods, but it is not the standard combined with NALC in routine practice.
Not the accepted reagent pairing.

**C. HNO3 with NALC**
Nitric acid is not used for sputum decontamination in standard protocols.
The correct alkali paired with NALC is sodium hydroxide, not an acid.

## Mind Capsule

> TB sputum processing = NALC (liquefies mucus) + NaOH (decontaminates), preserving mycobacteria for culture.
