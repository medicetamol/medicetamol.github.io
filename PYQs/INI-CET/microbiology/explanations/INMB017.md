# Cutaneous Larva Migrans (Hookworm)

**Hookworms** (*Ancylostoma duodenale*, *Necator americanus*) release **thin-shelled, oval, colourless eggs** in stool. Infection is by **filariform larvae penetrating the skin**. Larvae of **animal hookworms** (*Ancylostoma braziliense*, *A. caninum*) wander in the skin to cause **cutaneous larva migrans (creeping eruption)**.

## Trigger Point

> Stool microscopy showing **hookworm ova**; "which statement is true".

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Transmission by contaminated food and water**
The usual route is **skin penetration** of filariform larvae from soil, through bare feet.

**B. Eggs of Strongyloides stercoralis**
Strongyloides is diagnosed by **rhabditiform larvae** in stool; its eggs hatch in the gut and are rarely seen.

**C. Eggs of Hymenolepis nana**
These are round and show a **hexacanth embryo with polar filaments**, which is different from hookworm ova.

## Mind Capsule

> Hookworm: **soil + bare feet + skin penetration**; larvae of animal hookworms cause **cutaneous larva migrans**.
