# Cloxacillin

A **catalase-positive, Gram-positive coccus** causing septic arthritis is **Staphylococcus aureus**. **Cefoxitin sensitivity** on the disc test predicts susceptibility to methicillin, marking this as **MSSA (methicillin-sensitive S. aureus)**, for which an **anti-staphylococcal penicillin such as cloxacillin** is the drug of choice.

## Trigger Point

> **Catalase-positive coccus, cefoxitin sensitive**: MSSA, treated with cloxacillin.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Penicillin**
Most *S. aureus* strains produce penicillinase, making plain penicillin unreliable even when the strain is methicillin-sensitive.
Cloxacillin (penicillinase-resistant) is preferred instead.

**C. Vancomycin**
Reserved for **MRSA** (cefoxitin-resistant) or penicillin-allergic patients.
Not needed for a cefoxitin-sensitive (MSSA) isolate.

**D. Linezolid**
An alternative for resistant or complicated Gram-positive infections, including some MRSA cases.
Not first-line for straightforward MSSA.

## Mind Capsule

> Cefoxitin-sensitive Staph = MSSA = cloxacillin (or other anti-staphylococcal penicillin); cefoxitin-resistant = MRSA = vancomycin.
