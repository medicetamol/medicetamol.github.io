# Cryptococcus neoformans

**Cryptococcus neoformans**, an encapsulated yeast (especially significant in immunocompromised/HIV patients), is classically identified in CSF using **Nigrosin or India ink staining**, which outlines the thick polysaccharide capsule as a clear halo around the budding yeast cells against the dark background.

## Trigger Point

> **Nigrosin/India ink stain showing encapsulated budding yeast** in CSF: the classic finding for cryptococcal meningitis.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Aspergillus fumigatus**
A **septate, acute-angle branching mould**, not a budding encapsulated yeast, and not typically visualised this way in CSF.
Different morphology and staining pattern entirely.

**B. Pneumocystis jirovecii**
Causes pneumonia in immunocompromised patients, visualised with methenamine silver stain in lung tissue/BAL fluid, not as an encapsulated yeast in CSF.
Different organism, different site and stain.

**C. Histoplasma capsulatum**
A dimorphic fungus seen as small intracellular yeast forms within macrophages (classically on Giemsa/Wright stain of blood or bone marrow), without a prominent capsule on India ink.
Does not match the encapsulated CSF yeast picture described.

## Mind Capsule

> India ink/Nigrosin stain + encapsulated budding yeast in CSF = Cryptococcus neoformans meningitis.
