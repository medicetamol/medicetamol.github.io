# Rhizopus Species

**Rhino-orbito-cerebral mucormycosis**, most often from **Rhizopus** species, classically affects **poorly controlled diabetics (especially with ketoacidosis)** and other immunocompromised patients. It presents with facial pain, black necrotic eschar on the nasal septum or palate, and rapidly invades blood vessels, causing thrombosis and tissue infarction.

## Trigger Point

> **Poorly controlled diabetic + facial pain + black eschar** on the nose/palate: classic mucormycosis.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Aspergillus Species**
Causes invasive aspergillosis mainly in neutropenic or transplant patients, with septate hyphae branching at acute angles.
Diabetes/ketoacidosis is the classic setting for mucormycosis, not aspergillosis.

**C. Candida Species**
Causes mucocutaneous or systemic candidiasis, typically without the black necrotic eschar described.
Does not produce this angioinvasive necrotic picture.

**D. Cryptococcus neoformans**
Primarily causes meningitis in immunocompromised (especially HIV) patients.
Not associated with facial eschar or rhino-orbital disease.

## Mind Capsule

> Diabetic ketoacidosis + facial black eschar = mucormycosis (Rhizopus), an angioinvasive emergency.
