# Prions Are NOT Reliably Destroyed at 121°C for 15 Minutes

**Standard autoclaving (121°C, 15 minutes, 15 psi moist heat)** reliably kills bacteria, spores and viruses, but **prions are exceptionally resistant to routine autoclaving** — they require special prion-inactivation cycles (commonly 134°C for at least 18 minutes) or combined chemical treatment (e.g., sodium hydroxide) for reliable decontamination.

## Trigger Point

> **INCORRECT statement about the autoclave**: the claim that prions are destroyed at standard 121°C/15 min cycles.

## Why the Others Are True

**A. Moist Heat Sterilisation Above 100°C**
The autoclave achieves temperatures above 100°C precisely because steam is generated under increased pressure.
A correct, defining feature of autoclaving.

**B. Bowie-Dick Test Positive**
The Bowie-Dick test checks for adequate steam penetration into the load (used mainly for pre-vacuum autoclaves); a valid test result being positive/passed confirms correct functioning.
A genuine quality-control feature of autoclave use.

**D. Commonly Used for Sterilising Culture Media**
Autoclaving is indeed the standard method for sterilising culture media, most surgical instruments, and heat-stable solutions.
A correct, standard application of the autoclave.

## Mind Capsule

> Standard autoclave (121°C/15 min) kills bacteria, spores and viruses — but NOT prions, which need special high-temperature cycles or chemical treatment.
