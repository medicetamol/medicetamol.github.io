# Hyperkalemia

The classic **"lethal triad"** of trauma/massive transfusion is **hypothermia, coagulopathy, and metabolic acidosis** — each worsening the others in a vicious cycle. **Hyperkalemia** can occur with massive transfusion (from stored blood) but is **not** one of the three named components of this specific triad.

## Trigger Point

> **NOT a component** of the classic lethal triad: the option outside hypothermia, coagulopathy, acidosis.

## Why the Others Are True

**B. Coagulopathy**
Dilution of clotting factors and platelets, combined with acidosis and hypothermia, impairs haemostasis.
A defined component of the triad.

**C. Hypothermia**
From cold fluid/blood administration and exposure, impairing enzymatic clotting function.
A defined component of the triad.

**D. Hypocalcemia**
Citrate in stored blood chelates calcium; while an important consequence of massive transfusion, it is not one of the three classically named components of the "lethal triad" itself (that is hypothermia, coagulopathy, acidosis).
A real complication of massive transfusion, but not part of the named triad.

## Mind Capsule

> Lethal triad = hypothermia + coagulopathy + acidosis; hypocalcemia and hyperkalemia are separate transfusion complications.
