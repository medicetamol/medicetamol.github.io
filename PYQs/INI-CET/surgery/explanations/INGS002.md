# Colicky Pain

**Mechanical small bowel obstruction** presents with **intermittent, cramping (colicky) abdominal pain** from peristaltic waves pushing against the obstruction. **Paralytic (adynamic) ileus** lacks organized peristalsis, so pain, if present, tends to be a constant, dull discomfort rather than colicky in nature, and bowel sounds are typically absent rather than hyperactive/tinkling.

## Trigger Point

> **Best differentiates mechanical obstruction from paralytic ileus**: the character of the pain.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Tachycardia**
A non-specific sign of dehydration, pain, or sepsis that can occur in **both** mechanical obstruction and ileus.
Does not distinguish between the two.

**C. Abdominal Distension**
Seen in **both** mechanical obstruction and paralytic ileus, since both cause proximal bowel dilation.
A shared, non-discriminating feature.

**D. Vomiting**
Occurs in **both** conditions, though its timing/frequency may vary with the level of obstruction.
Not a specific discriminator between mechanical and paralytic causes.

## Mind Capsule

> Colicky pain + hyperactive/tinkling bowel sounds = mechanical obstruction; ileus = distension with quiet, absent bowel sounds.
