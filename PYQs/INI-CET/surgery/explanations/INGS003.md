# Bilateral Cleft Lip

**Bilateral cleft lip** results from failure of fusion of both maxillary prominences with the medial nasal prominence, leaving **clefts on both sides of the midline** with an isolated central (prolabial) segment and characteristic flattening/widening of the nasal tip, exactly as described.

## Key Finding

> Bilateral defect of the upper lip with nasal deformity, clefts on both sides of the midline, and an isolated central segment.

## Trigger Point

> **Clefts on BOTH sides of the midline with an isolated central segment**: defines bilateral, not unilateral or midline, cleft lip.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Midline Cleft Lip**
An extremely rare anomaly with a **single central** cleft, not two separate lateral clefts flanking an isolated segment.
Does not match the bilateral pattern described.

**C. Cleft Palate**
Involves a defect in the **hard and/or soft palate**, a separate embryological process from the lip.
The description here is specifically of the lip and nose, not the palate.

**D. Oblique Facial Cleft**
A rare craniofacial cleft running obliquely from the lip toward the eye, a different pattern altogether.
Does not match the bilateral, midline-symmetric description given.

## Mind Capsule

> Bilateral cleft lip = two lateral clefts + a free-floating central (prolabial) segment + nasal deformity.
