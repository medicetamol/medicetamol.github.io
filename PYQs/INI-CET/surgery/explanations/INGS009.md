# Surgery (Appendicectomy)

Ultrasound showing a **non-compressible, blind-ending tubular structure over 6 mm** with a **target sign** and increased Doppler flow, plus peri-appendiceal fluid, confirms **acute appendicitis**. With 48 hours of symptoms and no evidence of perforation/abscess on imaging, **appendicectomy** remains the standard management.

## Key Finding

> Ultrasound with colour Doppler shows a non-compressible, blind-ending tubular structure >6 mm, target pattern, peri-appendiceal fluid, and increased wall Doppler flow, consistent with acute appendicitis.

## Trigger Point

> **RIF pain 48 hours + ultrasound confirming acute appendicitis** (no abscess): standard management is surgery.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. CECT Abdomen**
Reserved for equivocal ultrasound findings or suspicion of complications (perforation, abscess); here the ultrasound is already diagnostic.
Additional imaging is not required when the diagnosis is already clear.

**C. USG-Guided Drainage**
Appropriate for a **periappendiceal abscess**, not simple uncomplicated appendicitis without a defined collection.
No abscess is described here.

**D. Conservative Management with Antibiotics Only**
Considered in select, mild, uncomplicated cases as an alternative in some protocols, but the standard, most widely accepted management, especially in an exam context, remains surgery.
Antibiotics-only is not the conventional first-line answer here.

## Mind Capsule

> Ultrasound target sign + non-compressible >6 mm appendix + peri-appendiceal fluid = acute appendicitis = appendicectomy.
