# Grade III

Per the **AAST Colon Injury Scale**, a laceration involving **more than 50% of the bowel circumference without transection** is classified as **Grade III**. Lesser lacerations (less than 50%) are Grade II, and full transection is Grade IV.

## Classification

| AAST Grade | Colon injury |
|---|---|
| I | Contusion/hematoma without devascularization; partial-thickness laceration |
| II | Laceration <50% of circumference |
| **III** | **Laceration >50% of circumference, without transection** |
| IV | Transection of the colon |
| V | Transection with segmental tissue loss / devascularized segment |

## Trigger Point

> **Laceration >50% circumference, without transection**: AAST Grade III.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Grade I**
Reserved for a contusion or hematoma without devascularisation, or a minor partial-thickness laceration, far less severe than described.
Does not match a >50% circumferential laceration.

**B. Grade II**
Defined by a laceration involving **less than 50%** of the circumference.
The injury described exceeds this threshold.

**D. Grade IV**
Requires **transection** of the bowel, which is explicitly excluded ("without transection") in this scenario.
The injury described has not progressed to transection.

## Mind Capsule

> Colon injury: <50% circumference = Grade II; >50% without transection = Grade III; transection = Grade IV.
