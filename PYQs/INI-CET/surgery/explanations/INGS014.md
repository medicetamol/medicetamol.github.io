# Streptococcus pneumoniae

The **spleen filters encapsulated bacteria** and produces opsonizing antibodies; after **splenectomy**, patients are at highest risk of **overwhelming post-splenectomy infection (OPSI)**, most commonly from **encapsulated organisms**, with **Streptococcus pneumoniae** the leading cause, followed by *Haemophilus influenzae* and *Neisseria meningitidis*. This is why pneumococcal (and other) vaccination is essential before or after splenectomy.

## Trigger Point

> **Post-splenectomy, greatest risk of overwhelming sepsis**: the classic encapsulated organism, S. pneumoniae.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Staphylococcus aureus**
A non-encapsulated organism typically cleared effectively by neutrophils and other immune mechanisms independent of the spleen.
Not the classic OPSI pathogen.

**C. Escherichia coli**
While it can cause sepsis in various settings, it is not the leading, characteristic cause of overwhelming post-splenectomy sepsis.
Not the encapsulated organism most linked to OPSI.

**D. Pseudomonas aeruginosa**
More typically associated with hospital-acquired or immunocompromised (e.g., burns, ICU) infections, not the classic OPSI presentation.
Not the primary organism implicated in post-splenectomy sepsis.

## Mind Capsule

> Post-splenectomy = risk of OPSI from encapsulated organisms; S. pneumoniae is the leading cause = vaccinate.
