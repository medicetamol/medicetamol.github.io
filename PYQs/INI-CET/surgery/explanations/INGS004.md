# Sharp Dissection of the Entire Mesorectum as a Single Intact Unit

**Total mesorectal excision (TME)**, the oncologic standard for rectal cancer, removes the rectum along with its **entire enveloping mesorectal fascia and fat (containing the draining lymphatics) in one intact, unbreached package**, using **sharp dissection along the avascular "holy plane."** This minimizes local recurrence by avoiding fragmentation of tumour-bearing tissue.

## Trigger Point

> **TME, standard operation for rectal cancer**: complete sharp dissection of an intact mesorectal envelope.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Excision of Only the Peritumoral Fat**
TME removes the **entire** mesorectum, not just a limited margin of fat around the tumour.
This would leave behind potentially involved lymphatic tissue.

**C. Blunt Dissection of the Mesorectum**
Blunt dissection risks **tearing** the mesorectal fascia, fragmenting the specimen and increasing local recurrence.
Sharp, not blunt, dissection is the standard technique.

**D. Ligation of the Internal Iliac Artery**
Not part of the definition of TME, which is about the plane and completeness of mesorectal excision, not vascular ligation.
Unrelated to the actual surgical principle being tested.

## Mind Capsule

> TME = sharp dissection of the intact mesorectal envelope along the avascular plane, lowering local recurrence.
