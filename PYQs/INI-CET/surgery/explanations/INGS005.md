# Catastrophic Hemorrhage - Airway - Breathing - Circulation - Disability - Exposure

Modern trauma resuscitation for **exsanguinating external haemorrhage** modifies the classic ABCDE sequence to **C-ABCDE**, addressing **catastrophic, life-threatening external bleeding first** (with direct pressure or a tourniquet) before airway, since uncontrolled massive external haemorrhage can kill faster than an airway problem.

## Correct Order

1. **Catastrophic haemorrhage control**
2. Airway (with C-spine control)
3. Breathing
4. Circulation
5. Disability
6. Exposure

## Trigger Point

> **Exsanguinating external hemorrhage**: the modern protocol places bleeding control ahead of even the airway.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Standard ABCDE**
This is the classic sequence for general trauma, but it does **not** prioritise catastrophic external bleeding control first, which can be fatal within minutes.
Modern guidelines add the "C" for catastrophic haemorrhage at the very start for this specific scenario.

**C. Circulation-Airway-Breathing-Disability-Exposure**
Places circulation ahead of airway in general, which is not the standard modified sequence used specifically for catastrophic external bleeding (C-ABCDE keeps airway second, right after bleeding control).
Not the recognised protocol order.

**D. Disability-Airway-Breathing-Circulation-Exposure**
Placing disability (neurological assessment) first is inappropriate; immediate threats to life (bleeding, airway, breathing) must be addressed before neurological assessment.
Does not reflect correct trauma priority.

## Mind Capsule

> Exsanguinating external bleeding = C-ABCDE: stop the catastrophic bleed before anything else, even airway.
