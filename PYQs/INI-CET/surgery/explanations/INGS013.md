# Raised Serum Lactate (and LDH)

**Acute mesenteric ischemia** classically presents with **pain out of proportion to physical examination findings**, especially in a patient with **atrial fibrillation** (embolic source). As bowel ischemia progresses, anaerobic metabolism raises **serum lactate (and LDH)**, a key, though non-specific, laboratory clue supporting the diagnosis and prompting urgent imaging/intervention.

## Trigger Point

> **Atrial fibrillation + pain out of proportion to findings**: think mesenteric ischemia; the supportive lab finding is raised lactate.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Raised Serum Amylase Only**
Amylase can be mildly elevated in bowel ischemia, but this is non-specific and far less characteristic/sensitive than a raised lactate for supporting the diagnosis.
Amylase elevation is classically linked more with pancreatitis.

**C. Hypocalcaemia**
Not a characteristic laboratory finding of mesenteric ischemia.
Not part of the classic biochemical picture here.

**D. Raised Serum Bilirubin**
Not a typical finding of mesenteric ischemia; raised bilirubin points more toward hepatobiliary pathology.
Does not fit this clinical scenario.

## Mind Capsule

> Pain out of proportion + AF (embolic source) + raised lactate = suspect acute mesenteric ischemia.
