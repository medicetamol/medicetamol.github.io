# Within 1 Hour Before Incision

**Surgical antibiotic prophylaxis** should be administered **within 60 minutes before skin incision** (some agents like vancomycin/fluoroquinolones need a longer 120-minute window) to ensure adequate tissue drug levels at the time of the first incision, minimizing surgical site infection.

## Trigger Point

> **Timing of prophylactic antibiotic** before elective GI surgery: within 1 hour of incision.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. 4 Hours Before Incision**
Far too early; tissue antibiotic levels would have declined significantly by the time of incision.
Would not provide adequate coverage at the critical moment.

**C. 6 Hours Before Incision**
Even further from the ideal window, essentially eliminating any meaningful prophylactic effect at the time of surgery.
Not an effective timing for prophylaxis.

**D. At the Time of Skin Closure**
Far too late; the antibiotic needs to be present in tissues **before** the incision is made, not after the procedure is essentially complete.
Defeats the purpose of prophylaxis against contamination during surgery.

## Mind Capsule

> Surgical prophylaxis = within 60 minutes before incision (longer for vancomycin/fluoroquinolones).
