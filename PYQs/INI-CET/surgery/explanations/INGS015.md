# Hemithyroidectomy

**FNAC cannot reliably distinguish follicular adenoma from follicular carcinoma**, because the diagnosis of malignancy depends on **capsular and/or vascular invasion**, which can only be assessed on **histopathology of the excised gland**. The standard next step for an FNAC read as "follicular neoplasm"/suggestive of follicular carcinoma is a **diagnostic hemithyroidectomy (lobectomy)**, with completion thyroidectomy performed later if malignancy is confirmed.

## Trigger Point

> **FNAC suggestive of follicular carcinoma**: since FNAC alone cannot confirm invasion, the next step is diagnostic hemithyroidectomy.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Total Thyroidectomy**
Performing a total thyroidectomy upfront, before histological confirmation of malignancy, risks unnecessary bilateral surgery and its complications (hypoparathyroidism, bilateral recurrent laryngeal nerve risk) if the lesion turns out to be a benign adenoma.
Hemithyroidectomy first is the more conservative, standard approach.

**C. Radioactive Iodine Therapy**
RAI is a **postoperative adjuvant treatment** for confirmed differentiated thyroid carcinoma, not an initial diagnostic or surgical step.
Premature before tissue diagnosis and primary surgery.

**D. Repeat FNAC**
FNAC has an inherent, well-recognised inability to distinguish follicular adenoma from carcinoma regardless of how many times it is repeated, since the distinguishing feature (invasion) cannot be assessed on cytology.
Repeating the same test will not resolve the diagnostic limitation.

## Mind Capsule

> FNAC "follicular neoplasm" = FNAC can't call malignancy (needs invasion on histology) = diagnostic hemithyroidectomy first.
