# Abduction of the Vocal Cords

The **posterior cricoarytenoid (PCA)** muscle rotates the arytenoid cartilage outwards and is the **only abductor of the vocal cords**, opening the glottis for breathing. It is supplied by the recurrent laryngeal nerve. Bilateral paralysis leaves the cords in the midline and causes stridor.

## Trigger Point

> **Posterior cricoarytenoid, the only muscle** acting on the cords in this way: the sole abductor.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Adduction of the Vocal Cords**
Performed by the lateral cricoarytenoid, interarytenoid and thyroarytenoid muscles.
The PCA does the opposite.

**C. Tensing (Lengthening) of the Vocal Cords**
Done by the cricothyroid, the only tensor, supplied by the external laryngeal nerve.
Not a PCA action.

**D. Relaxation (Shortening) of the Vocal Cords**
Done by the thyroarytenoid (vocalis) muscle.
Not a PCA action.

## Mind Capsule

> PCA = only abductor (opens the cords); cricothyroid = only tensor.
