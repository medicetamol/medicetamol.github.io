# Internal Branch of Superior Laryngeal Nerve

The **internal branch of the superior laryngeal nerve** pierces the thyrohyoid membrane and runs under the mucosa of the **pyriform fossa**, supplying sensation to the supraglottic larynx. A fish bone here injures or irritates this branch, causing foreign body sensation.

## Trigger Point

> Foreign body sensation after eating fish, with a fish bone lodged in the pyriform fossa.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Recurrent laryngeal nerve**
Lies in the tracheo-oesophageal groove and supplies the intrinsic laryngeal muscles; not the sensory nerve of the pyriform fossa.

**C. External branch of superior laryngeal nerve**
Motor to cricothyroid and lies outside the pharyngeal mucosa.

**D. Glossopharyngeal nerve**
Supplies the oropharynx and posterior tongue, not the pyriform fossa.

## Mind Capsule

> Pyriform fossa = internal laryngeal nerve (sensory); fish bone here risks injury to this nerve.
