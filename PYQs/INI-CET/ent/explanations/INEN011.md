# BPPV

**Benign paroxysmal positional vertigo** gives brief episodes of vertigo triggered by **head position changes** (turning in bed, looking up), often with nausea or vomiting, with **no hearing loss** or neurological signs. It is caused by displaced otoconia in a semicircular canal (usually the posterior canal), confirmed by the **Dix-Hallpike test** and treated with the **Epley manoeuvre**.

## Trigger Point

> Sudden vertigo with vomiting brought on by head position change in the morning, with no hearing loss or other cause.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Meniere disease**
Vertigo lasting minutes to hours, with fluctuating hearing loss, tinnitus and aural fullness; not positional.

**C. Vestibular neuritis**
Continuous vertigo lasting days after a viral illness, with no positional trigger.

**D. Acoustic neuroma**
Gradual unilateral sensorineural hearing loss and tinnitus, not positional vertigo.

## Mind Capsule

> Brief positional vertigo + no hearing loss = BPPV (Dix-Hallpike, Epley).
