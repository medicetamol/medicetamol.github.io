# Positive Thompson (Simmonds) Test

**Achilles tendon rupture** presents with sudden pain and weak plantarflexion after a push-off. The **Thompson (Simmonds) test** is diagnostic: squeezing the calf normally causes passive plantarflexion via an intact tendon; **absence of this movement is a positive test**, confirming complete rupture.

## Key Finding

> Sagittal fat-saturated ankle MRI shows discontinuity of the Achilles tendon.

## Trigger Point

> **Sudden pain, weak plantarflexion after push-off**, with imaging confirming rupture: the clinical confirmatory test is Thompson's.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Positive McMurray Test**
Used to detect a **meniscal tear** at the knee, not an Achilles injury.
Anatomically unrelated to this presentation.

**C. Steroid Injection**
Actually a **risk factor** for tendon rupture (weakens tendon collagen), not a diagnostic test.
It is a cause, not a finding, and is contraindicated in the acute setting.

**D. Plaster Cast Immobilisation**
A treatment option for select cases, not a clinical sign or diagnostic test.
Does not answer what the question asks for.

## Mind Capsule

> Achilles rupture: weak plantarflexion + positive Thompson (Simmonds) test (no passive plantarflexion on calf squeeze).
