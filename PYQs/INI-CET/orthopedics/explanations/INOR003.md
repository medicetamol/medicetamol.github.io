# Malunion of a Previous Fracture

Given a **history of previous injury** to the limb, **unilateral genu varum** most likely results from **malunion of a prior fracture** near the knee, healing with a varus angulation. This is a mechanical, post-traumatic cause rather than a metabolic or tumour-related one.

## Key Finding

> X-ray of both lower limbs shows bowing of one leg compared with the other.

## Trigger Point

> **History of previous injury** to the affected limb: points to a post-traumatic, not metabolic, cause.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Rickets**
A metabolic bone disease causing **bilateral, symmetric** bowing from defective mineralisation, not a unilateral deformity tied to a specific prior injury.
The history of unilateral trauma does not fit rickets.

**C. Non-Ossifying Fibroma**
A benign fibrous cortical lesion, usually an incidental finding, not typically a cause of gross angular deformity.
Does not explain a bowing deformity following trauma.

**D. Osteofibrous Dysplasia**
A rare fibro-osseous lesion of the tibia in children, usually presenting with anterior bowing without a preceding fracture.
The history given points to trauma, not this developmental lesion.

## Mind Capsule

> Unilateral bowing + past limb injury = malunion; bilateral, symmetric bowing = metabolic (rickets).
