# Ultrasound of the Abdomen Followed by CT if Inconclusive

For suspected **renal colic** (acute flank pain radiating to the groin), current practice favours starting with **ultrasound** — radiation-free, quick, and able to detect hydronephrosis and many stones — reserving **CT (typically non-contrast CT KUB)** for cases where ultrasound is inconclusive or a stone needs precise localisation/sizing. This stepwise approach is especially emphasised in patients where radiation exposure should be minimised (e.g., young or pregnant patients), though practice can vary with local protocols and patient factors.

## Trigger Point

> **Acute flank pain radiating to the groin**: the recommended initial imaging step is ultrasound, escalating to CT only if needed.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Direct MRI of the Abdomen and Pelvis**
MRI is not the standard first-line or even routine second-line modality for suspected renal colic; it is reserved for special situations (e.g., pregnancy when ultrasound is inconclusive) due to cost and access.
Not the appropriate initial step here.

**C. Plain X-Ray Followed by CT Urogram**
A plain KUB X-ray misses many stones (especially radiolucent ones) and is not the preferred initial test; ultrasound offers better initial sensitivity without radiation.
Skips the radiation-free first step that is now preferred.

**D. NCCT Followed by CECT**
Jumping straight to CT exposes the patient to radiation before a radiation-free option (ultrasound) has been tried, and contrast CT is not the standard second step for simple stone evaluation.
Bypasses the recommended stepwise, radiation-conscious approach.

## Mind Capsule

> Suspected renal colic: start with ultrasound (radiation-free); escalate to CT only if ultrasound is inconclusive.
