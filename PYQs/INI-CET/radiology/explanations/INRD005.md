# Biopsy

In a patient with a **past history of TB on ATT** who now develops a **new vertebral lesion on MRI**, the differential includes **relapse/resistant TB, a new infection, or malignancy (including metastasis)**. Imaging alone cannot distinguish these, so **biopsy** (often image-guided) is required for a **definitive tissue diagnosis** before altering management.

## Trigger Point

> **Vertebral lesion on MRI, past history of TB on ATT**: next step for a DEFINITIVE diagnosis is biopsy.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Mantoux Test**
Reflects past or present TB exposure/infection broadly, but cannot confirm the specific cause of a new vertebral lesion, nor exclude malignancy.
It does not give a definitive tissue diagnosis.

**C. Serum Protein Electrophoresis**
Useful to screen for **multiple myeloma** (M-band), a differential for vertebral lesions, but it is not a definitive, universally applicable diagnostic step for this presentation.
It does not directly sample the lesion in question.

**D. CT Chest**
May help assess the extent of pulmonary disease or find another primary site for malignancy, but it does not sample the vertebral lesion itself to give a tissue diagnosis.
It is complementary staging, not a definitive diagnostic step for the spinal lesion.

## Mind Capsule

> New lesion despite ongoing ATT = don't assume relapse; biopsy for definitive tissue diagnosis (rule out resistant TB vs malignancy).
