# No Radiological Investigation

An **uncomplicated upper respiratory tract infection** in a child is a **clinical diagnosis** not requiring imaging. Following the "as low as reasonably achievable" (ALARA) principle, unnecessary radiation exposure (chest X-ray, CT) should be avoided when it will not change management.

## Trigger Point

> **Uncomplicated URTI in a 12-year-old**: the next appropriate radiological step is none at all.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. HRCT Chest**
A high-radiation, detailed imaging test reserved for suspected interstitial lung disease or complex chronic pathology.
Grossly excessive for a simple URTI.

**C. CECT Chest**
Contrast-enhanced CT is reserved for suspected mass lesions, abscess, or complicated infection.
Not indicated for an uncomplicated, self-limited viral illness.

**D. Chest X-Ray**
Reserved for suspicion of complications such as pneumonia (focal signs, hypoxia, persistent fever).
Unnecessary imaging in an uncomplicated case adds radiation without clinical benefit.

## Mind Capsule

> Uncomplicated paediatric URTI = clinical diagnosis, no imaging needed (ALARA principle).
