# GFR <50 mL/min/1.73m²

**Group II gadolinium-based contrast agents** (the more stable macrocyclic agents) carry the **lowest risk of nephrogenic systemic fibrosis (NSF)** and, per ACR/RCR guidance, can be used **even in patients with reduced renal function (including GFR <50 mL/min/1.73m2 or even on dialysis)**, unlike the higher-risk Group I agents, which require caution or avoidance in significant renal impairment.

## Trigger Point

> **Group II GBCA, lowest NSF risk**: safe even with reduced renal function.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. A 30-Year-Old With No Renal Function Test Done**
Current guidance for Group II agents generally does **not mandate** routine renal function testing before use in patients without risk factors, since the risk of NSF is already minimal with these agents; this option is not what the question is highlighting as the key permitted-use scenario.
It does not represent the specific reduced-renal-function indication being tested.

**C. A 60-Year-Old With No Renal Function Test Done**
Similarly, age alone does not necessitate pre-testing for Group II agents, and this is not the scenario that defines their key safety advantage.
The question is testing knowledge of safety in renal impairment specifically.

**D. A Patient/Child With Normal Renal Function**
While obviously also safe, this is not the distinguishing feature of Group II agents, since **any** gadolinium agent is essentially safe in normal renal function; the real point of Group II agents is their safety **despite reduced GFR**.
This option does not test the specific, distinguishing point about renal impairment.
