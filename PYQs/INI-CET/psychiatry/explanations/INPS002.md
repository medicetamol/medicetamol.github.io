# Conditioned Stimulus Producing a Conditioned Response

**Classical (Pavlovian) conditioning** is the process by which a previously neutral stimulus, after repeated pairing with an unconditioned stimulus, becomes a **conditioned stimulus (CS)** capable of producing a learned **conditioned response (CR)** on its own (for example, a bell alone causing salivation).

## Trigger Point

> **Best describes classical conditioning**: focus on what happens once learning has occurred, i.e., a CS eliciting a CR.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Unconditioned Stimulus Producing an Unconditioned Response**
This describes an **innate, reflexive** response (for example, food causing salivation) that exists **before** any conditioning takes place.
It describes the baseline reflex, not the learned association.

**C. Association Between a Conditioned Stimulus and a Conditioned Response**
This restates part of the definition but is less precise than describing the CS actively **producing** the CR, which is the operational core of classical conditioning.
It is close, but the keyed answer emphasizes the causal CS-to-CR relationship.

**D. Association Between an Unconditioned Stimulus and an Unconditioned Response**
This is simply the innate reflex pairing (like food and salivation), present from the start and not the result of learning.
No new association is being learned here.

## Mind Capsule

> Classical conditioning = neutral stimulus becomes a conditioned stimulus that, by itself, now produces the (conditioned) response.
