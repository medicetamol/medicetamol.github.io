# Hospitalize

A patient with **major depression plus active suicidal ideation** ("what is the point in living?"), compounded by risk factors such as **alcoholism and being divorced (social isolation)**, requires an urgent **safety assessment and hospitalisation** if risk is significant, before any outpatient therapy or medication is considered.

## Trigger Point

> **"What is the point in living?"** in a depressed patient with added risk factors: an expression of suicidal ideation requiring immediate safety action.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Cognitive Behavioural Therapy**
An appropriate longer-term treatment for depression, but not the correct **immediate** step when active suicidal ideation with risk factors is present.
Safety must be secured first.

**C. Antidepressant**
Also part of definitive management, but starting a medication does not address the acute risk, and effects take weeks to appear.
It does not substitute for ensuring immediate safety.

**D. Reassure That It Is Normal**
Minimising or normalising suicidal ideation is dangerous and inappropriate.
This response fails to address a genuine safety concern.

## Mind Capsule

> Active suicidal ideation with risk factors = assess safety and hospitalize first, before starting therapy or medication.
