# Anterograde Amnesia

**Korsakoff syndrome**, from **thiamine (vitamin B1) deficiency** (classically in chronic alcoholics, following untreated Wernicke encephalopathy), is characterised by a striking **inability to form new memories (anterograde amnesia)**, often with confabulation, while **older, previously established memories are relatively preserved**.

## Trigger Point

> **Unable to form new memories, older memories preserved**: the hallmark of anterograde amnesia in Korsakoff syndrome.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Retrograde Amnesia**
Involves loss of memories formed **before** the onset of the causative event, not an inability to form new ones.
The described deficit is about new memory formation, not old memory loss.

**C. Transient Global Amnesia**
A sudden, temporary episode of memory loss, typically resolving within 24 hours, unrelated to chronic thiamine deficiency.
Korsakoff syndrome is chronic and persistent, not transient.

**D. Dissociative Amnesia**
A psychological, stress-related memory loss with no organic brain pathology.
Korsakoff has a clear organic (thiamine deficiency) basis.

## Mind Capsule

> Korsakoff syndrome = thiamine deficiency = anterograde amnesia + confabulation, with retained old memories.
