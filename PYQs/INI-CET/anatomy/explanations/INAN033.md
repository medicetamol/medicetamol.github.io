# Scaphoid

The **scaphoid** is the most commonly fractured carpal bone, typically after a fall on an outstretched hand. It forms part of the floor of the **anatomical snuffbox**, so tenderness there is the classic sign. The **proximal pole** has a retrograde blood supply, so these fractures risk **avascular necrosis** and non-union.

## Trigger Point

> Fall with wrist pain and tenderness in the anatomical snuffbox.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Trapezoid**
A small, deep carpal bone, not palpable in the snuffbox and rarely fractured.

**C. Capitate**
The largest carpal bone, centrally placed and well protected; fractures are uncommon.

**D. Trapezium**
Also lies near the snuffbox floor, but fractures are far less common than scaphoid fractures.

## Mind Capsule

> Snuffbox tenderness after a fall = scaphoid fracture (watch for avascular necrosis of the proximal pole).
