# Internuclear Ophthalmoplegia

A lesion of the **medial longitudinal fasciculus (MLF)** disconnects the abducens nucleus from the contralateral oculomotor nucleus. On lateral gaze, the eye on the side of the lesion fails to **adduct**, while the opposite (abducting) eye shows **nystagmus**. Convergence is usually preserved.

## Key Finding

> Impaired adduction of one eye on lateral gaze with nystagmus of the abducting eye.

## Trigger Point

> Paired eyes in different gaze positions with a label for nystagmus.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. 3rd nerve palsy**
Eye is down and out with ptosis and often a dilated pupil. There is no abducting nystagmus.

**C. 6th nerve palsy**
Affected eye cannot abduct, with an esotropia. Adduction is intact.

**D. Horizontal gaze palsy**
Both eyes fail to look to one side (PPRF or abducens nucleus). It does not give a dissociated adduction deficit with nystagmus.

## Mind Capsule

> Adduction failure + contralateral abducting nystagmus + normal convergence = INO (MLF lesion). Bilateral INO is common in multiple sclerosis.
