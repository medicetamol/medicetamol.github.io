# Neurapraxia

The mildest injury in **Seddon's classification (Sunderland grade I)**: a **reversible physiological conduction block** from focal ischaemia or segmental demyelination. The axon and all connective tissue sheaths are intact, there is no Wallerian degeneration, and full recovery occurs in days to weeks.

## Classification

| Seddon | Sunderland | Structure damaged | Recovery |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Neurapraxia** | I | Myelin only; axon intact | Full, days to weeks |
| Axonotmesis | II to III | Axon (and endoneurium in III) | Slow, about 1 mm/day |
| Neurotmesis | IV to V | Sheath disrupted or nerve transected | Needs surgery |

## Trigger Point

> Neurapraxia is the **mildest nerve injury**, a physiological block with an intact axon.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Endoneurium and Axon Cut**
This is **axonotmesis** (Sunderland III), where the axon and endoneurium are disrupted and Wallerian degeneration follows.
It is structural damage, not a mere conduction block.

**C. Nerve Sheath (Perineurium) Cut**
Perineurium disruption places the injury at Sunderland IV, a severe fascicular injury.
Spontaneous recovery is poor.

**D. Complete Transection of the Nerve Trunk**
This is **neurotmesis** (Sunderland V), the most severe grade.
It needs surgical repair.

## Mind Capsule

> Neurapraxia = conduction block + intact axon = complete recovery without degeneration.
