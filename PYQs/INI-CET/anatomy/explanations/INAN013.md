# Midline Cleft Lip

**Midline cleft lip** results from a **failure of fusion of the maxillary processes with the medial nasal process (intermaxillary segment)** in the midline, a rare anomaly distinct from the far more common lateral (unilateral or bilateral) cleft, which results from failure of fusion between the maxillary process and the lateral/medial nasal processes at the lateral incisive region.

## Trigger Point

> **Failure of fusion of the maxillary process with the medial nasal process**: the mechanism specific to a midline defect.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Oblique Facial Cleft**
A rare craniofacial cleft running from the lip toward the eye, from failure of fusion of entirely different facial processes.
Not explained by a maxillary-medial nasal process failure alone.

**C. Bilateral Cleft Lip**
Results from failure of fusion of the maxillary processes with the medial nasal process **bilaterally**, at the lateral margins of the philtrum, leaving the central (prolabial) segment free — a different pattern from a true midline defect.
The mechanism described here is specifically midline, not lateral/bilateral.

**D. Cleft Palate**
Results from failure of fusion of the palatine shelves (secondary palate), a separate embryological event from lip fusion.
This question describes a lip anomaly, not a palatal one.

## Mind Capsule

> Midline cleft lip = failure of maxillary process fusion with the medial nasal process in the midline (rare); lateral/bilateral cleft = failure at the lateral margins.
