# Parotid Gland

The **parotid gland** is a **purely serous** salivary gland, its acini composed entirely of serous cells producing a thin, protein-rich secretion — histologically distinct from the sublingual gland (predominantly mucous) and the submandibular gland (mixed sero-mucous).

## Key Finding

> Histological section shows acini that are **predominantly/purely serous**.

## Trigger Point

> **Purely serous acini** on histology: identifies the gland as parotid.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Sublingual Salivary Gland**
Predominantly a **mucous** gland with only scattered serous demilunes.
The opposite acinar composition to what is described.

**B. Pancreas**
Also has serous-type acini (exocrine), but these secrete digestive enzymes into ducts leading to the duodenum, and the gland additionally contains islets of Langerhans, a very different overall architecture from a purely salivary gland.
The question is asking about a salivary-type gland by context, not an entirely different digestive organ.

**D. Liver**
Composed of hepatocytes arranged in hepatic lobules/plates, not acini at all.
An entirely different histological pattern.

## Mind Capsule

> Salivary gland histology: parotid = purely serous; sublingual = purely mucous; submandibular = mixed (serous predominant).
