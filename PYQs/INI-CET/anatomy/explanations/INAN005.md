# Parietal Lobe

The **parietal lobe** contains the primary somatosensory cortex (postcentral gyrus) and the **posterior parietal association cortex**, which merges visual and somatosensory input to build spatial awareness and guide movement (hand-eye coordination). Lesions produce contralateral **neglect** (non-dominant side) and optic ataxia; dominant-side lesions cause **Gerstmann syndrome**.

## Trigger Point

> **Hand-eye coordination, integration of visual and somatosensory information, spatial awareness**: the association functions of the parietal cortex.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Frontal Lobe**
Motor planning, executive function, personality and Broca's speech area.
It initiates movement rather than integrating sensory space.

**C. Occipital Lobe**
Primary visual processing.
It handles vision alone, not the multimodal integration described.

**D. Temporal Lobe**
Auditory processing, language comprehension, memory and emotion (hippocampus, amygdala).
Not the site of spatial sensory integration.

## Mind Capsule

> Spatial awareness + sensory integration = parietal lobe; neglect = non-dominant parietal lesion.
