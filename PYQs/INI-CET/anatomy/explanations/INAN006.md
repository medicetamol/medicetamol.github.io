# External Oblique Muscle

The **inguinal ligament** is the lower free border of the **external oblique aponeurosis**, rolled inwards and stretching between the **anterior superior iliac spine and the pubic tubercle**. It forms the floor of the inguinal canal; the lacunar ligament is its medial expansion.

## Trigger Point

> **Poupart's (inguinal) ligament**: the folded lower edge of the external oblique aponeurosis.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Internal Oblique Muscle**
Its lowest fibres arch over the cord to form the roof and, with transversus, the conjoint tendon; it also gives the cremaster.
It does not form the inguinal ligament.

**C. Transversus Abdominis Muscle**
Forms the deep inguinal ring margin and the conjoint tendon medially.
Not the source of the ligament.

**D. Rectus Abdominis Muscle**
A vertical muscle within the rectus sheath, attached to the pubic crest.
Not related to the inguinal ligament.

## Mind Capsule

> Inguinal ligament = lower border of the external oblique aponeurosis.

## Memory Hook

> **MALT**: **M**uscle (arching fibres of internal oblique and transversus) = roof; **A**poneurosis of external oblique = anterior wall; **L**igament (inguinal, with lacunar) = floor; **T**ransversalis fascia = posterior wall.
