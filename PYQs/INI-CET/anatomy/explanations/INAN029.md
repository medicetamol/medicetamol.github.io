# Hip Abduction

The muscle marked in a posterior view of the gluteal region is taken to be **gluteus medius** (with gluteus minimus). Its main action is **abduction of the hip**, and it stabilises the pelvis in single-leg stance. Paralysis of these muscles gives a Trendelenburg gait.

## Trigger Point

> Posterior view of the hip with a labelled gluteal muscle, and its action is asked.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Internal rotation**
Only the anterior fibres of gluteus medius and minimus contribute, and this is secondary to abduction.

**C. Knee extension**
Not a gluteal action; produced by the quadriceps. Gluteus maximus acts on the knee only indirectly through the iliotibial tract.

**D. Knee flexion**
Produced by the hamstrings, gastrocnemius and popliteus, not by gluteal muscles.

## Mind Capsule

> Gluteus medius and minimus = abductors of the hip (Trendelenburg sign when weak).
