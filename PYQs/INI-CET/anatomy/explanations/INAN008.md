# Levator Veli Palatini

The **levator veli palatini** arises from the petrous part of the temporal bone and the **cartilaginous Eustachian tube**, then passes into the soft palate to **elevate** it during swallowing and speech, sealing off the nasopharynx.

## Trigger Point

> Muscle attaching to the **cartilaginous Eustachian tube** that also **blends into the soft palate**.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Stylopharyngeus**
Arises from the styloid process and inserts into the pharyngeal wall and thyroid cartilage, elevating the pharynx and larynx; supplied by the glossopharyngeal nerve.
Unrelated to the Eustachian tube or the soft palate.

**B. Tensor Veli Palatini**
Also arises partly from the lateral wall of the cartilaginous tube, but hooks around the **pterygoid hamulus** to tense the palate and **open** the tube on swallowing.
It tenses and opens, rather than elevating and blending into, the palate.

**D. Palatopharyngeus**
Forms the posterior pillar of the fauces, running from the soft palate to the pharynx and thyroid cartilage.
It does not arise from the Eustachian tube.

## Mind Capsule

> Levator veli palatini = arises from the tube + elevates the palate; tensor veli palatini = hooks the hamulus + opens the tube.
