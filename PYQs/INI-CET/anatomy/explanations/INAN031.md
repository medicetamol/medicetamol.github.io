# Cystic Artery

The **hepatocystic triangle** is bounded by the **cystic duct**, the **common hepatic duct** and the **inferior surface of the liver (right lobe)**. The **cystic artery** (with the cystic lymph node) lies *inside* the triangle as a content, not as a boundary. Identifying it here is the key step before clipping during cholecystectomy.

## Hepatocystic Triangle

| Boundaries | Contents |
|---|---|
| Cystic duct <br>Common hepatic duct <br>Inferior surface of the right lobe of liver | Cystic artery <br>Cystic lymph node (of Lund) |

## Trigger Point

> "Which of the following is **not** a boundary of the hepatocystic triangle?"

## Why the Others Are True

**A. Common hepatic duct**
It forms the medial boundary of the triangle.

**B. Cystic duct**
It forms the lateral (inferior) boundary of the triangle.

**C. Right lobe of liver**
Its inferior surface forms the superior boundary.

## Mind Capsule

> Hepatocystic triangle = cystic duct + CHD + liver; the cystic artery is a content.
> In the older Calot's triangle, the cystic artery is taken as the upper boundary instead of the liver.
