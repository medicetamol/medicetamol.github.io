# Wider, Shorter and More Vertical Right Bronchus

The **right main bronchus** is **wider, shorter and more vertical** (about 25 degrees from the midline versus about 45 degrees on the left), so it is almost in line with the trachea. Inhaled foreign bodies therefore fall straight into it, and it also supplies a larger lung (three lobes).

## Trigger Point

> Foreign body lodges **more often in the right main bronchus**, so the question asks for the anatomical features that favour this.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Narrower, Longer and More Horizontal; Two Lobes**
This describes the **left** main bronchus, which passes under the aortic arch and over the left atrium.
Its course makes entry of a foreign body less likely.

**C. Wider but More Horizontal; Two Lobes**
Mixes features: the right bronchus is not horizontal, and the right lung has three lobes.
A horizontal course would reduce, not increase, aspiration.

**D. Narrower and More Vertical; Three Lobes**
The right bronchus is wider, not narrower.
Width is a key reason a foreign body can enter.

## Mind Capsule

> Right main bronchus = wider + shorter + more vertical = commoner site for foreign body and aspiration.
