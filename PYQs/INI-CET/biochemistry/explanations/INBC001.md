# Abetalipoproteinemia

In **abetalipoproteinemia** there is a defect of **microsomal triglyceride transfer protein (MTP)**, so apoB-containing lipoproteins (chylomicrons, VLDL, LDL) cannot be assembled. Triglycerides and cholesterol are therefore **very low**, not high, with fat malabsorption, acanthocytes and fat-soluble vitamin deficiency.

## Trigger Point

> High serum triglycerides **EXCEPT**: look for the condition where triglyceride-carrying lipoproteins cannot be made.

## Why the Others Are True

**A. High Fructose Intake**
Fructose bypasses the PFK-1 control step and supplies substrate for lipogenesis.
Hepatic VLDL-triglyceride synthesis rises, causing hypertriglyceridemia.

**B. Diabetes Mellitus**
Insulin deficiency or resistance lowers lipoprotein lipase activity and increases free fatty acid flux to the liver.
This raises VLDL and triglycerides.

**C. Nephrotic Syndrome**
Hypoalbuminemia stimulates hepatic lipoprotein synthesis, and lipoprotein lipase activity is reduced.
Both cholesterol and triglycerides rise.

## Mind Capsule

> Abetalipoproteinemia = no apoB = LOW triglycerides and cholesterol (with acanthocytes and steatorrhoea).
