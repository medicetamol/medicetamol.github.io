# All of the Above

A carbohydrate-rich diet raises insulin, which switches on **de novo lipogenesis**. **PDH** is activated to produce acetyl-CoA, which condenses with oxaloacetate to form **citrate**. Citrate is exported to the cytosol, where **ATP citrate lyase** regenerates acetyl-CoA. **Acetyl-CoA carboxylase** then converts it to **malonyl-CoA**.

## Statement Check

| Item | Verdict |
|---|---|
| 1. Malonyl CoA | **Increased**: product of ACC, rises with lipogenesis |
| 2. Acetyl CoA carboxylase | **Increased**: insulin-activated (dephosphorylation) and citrate-activated |
| 3. PDH | **Increased**: insulin-activated by dephosphorylation |
| 4. Citrate lyase | **Increased**: induced by insulin and high carbohydrate intake |

## Trigger Point

> Obese man on a carbohydrate-rich diet.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. 1, 2, 3**
Leaves out citrate lyase, which is also increased.

**B. 2, 3, 4**
Leaves out malonyl CoA, which accumulates with high ACC activity.

**C. 1, 3, 4**
Leaves out acetyl CoA carboxylase, the rate-limiting enzyme of fatty acid synthesis.

## Mind Capsule

> High carbohydrate + insulin = PDH, citrate lyase, ACC and malonyl-CoA all up (fatty acid synthesis).
