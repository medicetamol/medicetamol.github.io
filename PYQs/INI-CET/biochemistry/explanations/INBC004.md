# Glycogen Synthase

**Glycogen synthase** is active in its **dephosphorylated (a) form** and inactivated by phosphorylation (by PKA, GSK-3 and others). Insulin promotes dephosphorylation through protein phosphatase-1 to favour glycogen synthesis. In contrast, **glycogen phosphorylase is active when phosphorylated**.

## Trigger Point

> Enzyme **active in the dephosphorylated state**: the anabolic glycogen-synthesis enzyme.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Glycogen Phosphorylase**
The rate-limiting enzyme of glycogenolysis; the phosphorylated form (phosphorylase a) is active, driven by glucagon and adrenaline.
It behaves opposite to glycogen synthase.

**C. PFK-2**
The kinase domain of the bifunctional PFK-2/FBPase-2 enzyme is also more active when dephosphorylated in the liver (fed state).
The item is not clean, but glycogen synthase is the textbook example of this rule.

**D. Fructose-2,6-Bisphosphatase**
The phosphatase domain of the same bifunctional enzyme is active when phosphorylated (glucagon, fasting).
It is therefore active in the phosphorylated state.

## Mind Capsule

> Fed state: dephosphorylated glycogen synthase (anabolic) active; fasting: phosphorylated glycogen phosphorylase (catabolic) active.
