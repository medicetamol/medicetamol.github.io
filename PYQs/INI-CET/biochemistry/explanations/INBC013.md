# Increased O2 Consumption, Increased Body Temperature, Decreased ATP Production

An **oxidative phosphorylation uncoupler** (such as 2,4-dinitrophenol) dissipates the proton gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane without driving ATP synthase, so electron transport and **O2 consumption accelerate** (the cell tries to compensate), the energy is released as **heat (hyperthermia)**, and **ATP production falls** despite the high oxygen use.

## Trigger Point

> **Uncoupling agent exposure**: the classic triad is increased O2 use, increased body temperature, and decreased ATP.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Decreased O2 Consumption, Increased Temperature, Increased ATP**
Uncouplers **increase**, not decrease, O2 consumption, and they **reduce**, not increase, ATP yield.
Two of the three directions here are wrong.

**C. Increased O2 Consumption, Decreased Temperature, Increased ATP**
Uncoupling releases the proton-gradient energy as **heat**, raising body temperature, and reduces (not increases) ATP output.
The temperature and ATP directions are both wrong.

**D. Decreased O2 Consumption, Decreased Temperature, Decreased ATP**
While ATP correctly falls, both O2 consumption and temperature should **rise**, not fall, with an uncoupler.
Only one of the three components matches the correct effect.

## Mind Capsule

> Uncouplers: proton gradient dissipated = O2 consumption up, heat production up, ATP synthesis down (classic dinitrophenol effect).
