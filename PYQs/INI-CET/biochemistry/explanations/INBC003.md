# Pyridoxine (Vitamin B6)

The active form, **pyridoxal phosphate (PLP)**, is the coenzyme for **aminotransferases (transaminases such as ALT and AST)**. PLP forms a Schiff base with the amino group of the amino acid, transferring it to an alpha-keto acid. It is also needed for decarboxylation reactions and ALA synthase.

## Trigger Point

> **Coenzyme required for a transamination reaction**: PLP, derived from vitamin B6.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Thiamine (Vitamin B1)**
Its active form, TPP, is the coenzyme for oxidative decarboxylation (PDH, alpha-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase) and transketolase.
It is not used in transamination.

**C. FAD (Riboflavin, Vitamin B2)**
A redox coenzyme for flavoproteins such as succinate dehydrogenase.
It carries electrons, not amino groups.

**D. NAD (Niacin, Vitamin B3)**
A redox coenzyme for dehydrogenases (lactate dehydrogenase, for example).
It transfers hydride, not amino groups.

## Mind Capsule

> Transamination and decarboxylation of amino acids = PLP (vitamin B6).
