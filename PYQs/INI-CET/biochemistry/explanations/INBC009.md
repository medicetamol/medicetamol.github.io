# Both True, Reason Correctly Explains the Assertion

Glycan structures are built by **multiple glycosyltransferases acting non-templated**, in branched configurations with variable linkage points (alpha/beta, multiple carbon positions), unlike the linear, genetically templated assembly of proteins from a fixed set of amino acids. This gives glycans a combinatorially far greater potential structural diversity than proteins, and the reason correctly explains why.

## Trigger Point

> **Assertion and reason both correct, and the reason explains the assertion**: glycans lack a genetic template and branch.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Assertion True, Reason False**
The reason (no genetic template, branching, variable linkages) is a well-established, accurate explanation for glycan diversity, not a false statement.
Both parts are correct here, not just the assertion.

**B. Assertion False, Reason True**
The assertion itself (glycans are more structurally diverse than proteins) is a widely accepted concept in glycobiology, not a false claim.
Both parts hold, not just the reason.

**D. Both True but Reason Does Not Explain**
The reason directly accounts for the assertion: non-templated, branched, variably linked synthesis is precisely what generates the greater combinatorial diversity.
The reason is not just true but causally connects to the assertion.

## Mind Capsule

> Glycans have no genetic template, and their branched, variably linked assembly gives them far greater structural diversity than proteins.
