# Glucose-6-Phosphatase

**Von Gierke disease (GSD type I)** is due to **glucose-6-phosphatase deficiency**, so glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis cannot release free glucose. This causes **fasting hypoglycaemia, hepatomegaly, glycogen accumulation**, ketosis and lactic acidosis.

## Glycogen Storage Diseases

| Type | Enzyme | Key feature |
|---|---|---|
| I (Von Gierke) | Glucose-6-phosphatase | Fasting hypoglycaemia, hepatomegaly |
| II (Pompe) | Lysosomal α-1,4-glucosidase | Cardiomegaly |
| V (McArdle) | Muscle phosphorylase | Exercise intolerance |
| VI (Hers) | Liver phosphorylase | Milder hepatomegaly |
| VII (Tarui) | Phosphofructokinase | Exercise intolerance |

## Trigger Point

> Hepatomegaly, ketosis, glycogen excess in the liver, and no rise in glucose after overnight fasting.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Liver phosphorylase**
Hers disease: milder hepatomegaly with mild hypoglycaemia.

**C. Muscle phosphorylase**
McArdle disease: muscle cramps with exercise.

**D. Phosphofructokinase**
Tarui disease: exercise intolerance with haemolysis.

## Mind Capsule

> Hepatomegaly + fasting hypoglycaemia + glycogen excess = Von Gierke (glucose-6-phosphatase).
