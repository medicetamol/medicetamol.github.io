# Sucrose

**Sucrose** is glucose linked to fructose through an **alpha-1,beta-2 glycosidic bond**, which uses up **both anomeric carbons**. With no free aldehyde or ketone group, it cannot reduce Cu2+ in Benedict's or Fehling's test, so it is a **non-reducing sugar** (trehalose is another).

## Trigger Point

> **NON-reducing sugar, negative Benedict's test**: both anomeric carbons involved in the glycosidic bond.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Maltose**
Glucose-alpha-1,4-glucose; the second glucose keeps a free anomeric carbon.
It is a reducing sugar and gives a positive test.

**C. Lactose**
Galactose-beta-1,4-glucose; the glucose unit has a free anomeric carbon.
It is a reducing sugar and gives a positive test.

**D. Glucose**
A monosaccharide with a free aldehyde group.
It reduces Benedict's reagent, giving a positive test.

## Mind Capsule

> Sucrose (and trehalose) = non-reducing disaccharides, since the anomeric carbons are bonded to each other.
