# pH 6 (Isoelectric Point)

Glycine has two ionisable groups: carboxyl (**pK₁ ≈ 2.34**) and amino (**pK₂ ≈ 9.6**). The **zwitterion** is the predominant form around the **isoelectric point (pI)**, where net charge is zero.

## Working

- pI = (pK₁ + pK₂) / 2
- pI = (2.34 + 9.6) / 2 = 5.97
- **pI ≈ 6**

## Trigger Point

> Glycine titration curve; pH at which it exists predominantly as the zwitterion.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. pH 4.4**
Lies below the pI, so the molecule carries a small net positive charge, and it is not the point of zero net charge.

**C. pH 2**
Close to pK₁, where roughly half of the molecules are cationic and half are zwitterionic.

**D. pH 9**
Close to pK₂ (9.6), where a large fraction of molecules is already in the anionic form.

## Mind Capsule

> Zwitterion predominates at pI = average of the two pKa values flanking it (glycine ≈ 6).
