# Primase

**Primase** is an RNA polymerase that synthesises short **RNA primers** on the single-stranded DNA template, providing the free 3'-OH group that **DNA polymerase III** requires to begin synthesising a new DNA strand during replication.

## Trigger Point

> **Enzyme that synthesises RNA primers** during DNA replication: primase.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Topoisomerase**
Relieves supercoiling ahead of the replication fork by transiently cutting and resealing DNA strands.
It does not synthesise any nucleic acid.

**B. DNA Polymerase II**
Involved mainly in DNA repair in bacteria, not in initiating primers for replication.
A repair enzyme, not the primer-synthesising enzyme.

**C. DNA Polymerase III**
The main replicative polymerase, extending the DNA strand from the primer, but it **cannot initiate synthesis de novo** and depends on primase to provide the starting primer.
It elongates DNA; it does not make the RNA primer itself.

## Mind Capsule

> Primase lays down RNA primers; DNA polymerase III then extends DNA from those primers.
