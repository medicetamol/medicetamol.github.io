# Cyanide - Bitter Almond Smell

**Cyanide** classically has a **bitter almond odour** on the breath (though a genetically determined minority of examiners cannot smell it). It inhibits **cytochrome c oxidase**, blocking the electron transport chain and causing histotoxic (cytotoxic) hypoxia.

## Correct Matches

| Poison | Characteristic feature |
|---|---|
| **Cyanide** | **Bitter almond smell** |
| Carbon monoxide | Cherry-red livor/discolouration |
| Phosphorus | Garlic odour |
| Organophosphate | Excessive secretions (SLUDGE) |

## Trigger Point

> **Bitter almond smell**: the classic clue for cyanide poisoning.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Carbon Monoxide - Cherry Red Discolouration**
This is a true, standard pairing (carboxyhaemoglobin gives cherry-red livor and blood).
It is a genuine association, which makes it a strong distractor but not the keyed answer here.

**C. Phosphorus - Garlic Odour**
Also a true, standard pairing (phosphine/phosphorus gives a garlic-like smell).
Also correct, but not the option selected as the answer.

**D. Organophosphate - Excessive Secretions**
Also a true, standard pairing (cholinergic excess causes SLUDGE symptoms).
Also correct, but not the keyed choice.

## Mind Capsule

> Smell clues: bitter almonds = cyanide; garlic = phosphorus/organophosphate/arsenic; rotten eggs = hydrogen sulfide.
