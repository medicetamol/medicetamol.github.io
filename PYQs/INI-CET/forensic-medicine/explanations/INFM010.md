# Motor Vehicle (Road Traffic) Accident

**"Sparrow-foot" or crow's-foot abrasions**: multiple linear, radiating brush-like abrasions caused as skin is **dragged and scraped** across a rough road surface, typically at the point of impact or drag in a road traffic accident. The pattern reflects the direction of the vehicle's movement.

## Trigger Point

> **Radiating, brush-like ("sparrow-foot") abrasions**: the classic pattern of friction/drag abrasion in RTAs.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Fall From Height Onto a Flat Surface**
Produces more diffuse contusions and lacerations at points of impact.
It lacks the classic radiating, dragged brush pattern.

**C. Firearm Injury**
Produces a punched-in entry wound with abrasion and grease collars, not a radiating brush pattern.
Different mechanism entirely.

**D. Electrocution**
Produces an entry/exit mark that may be a hard, greyish-white, charred or blistered lesion (electric mark).
Not a dragged abrasion pattern.

## Mind Capsule

> Sparrow-foot/crow-foot abrasions = friction/drag injury on a rough road surface = road traffic accident.
