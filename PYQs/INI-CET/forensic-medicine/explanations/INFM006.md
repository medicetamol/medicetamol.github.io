# Dermis

Tattoo pigment is deposited in the **dermis**, taken up by **dermal macrophages and fibroblasts**, which is why tattoos are permanent despite normal epidermal turnover. This dermal location is also why tattoos can still be identified even after the epidermis is lost (for example, in decomposed or skeletonised bodies, if the dermis survives).

## Key Finding

> Skin photograph shows a permanent tattoo mark.

## Trigger Point

> **Permanent** ink pigment marking: permanence implies deposition below the shedding epidermis.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Stratum Corneum of the Epidermis**
This superficial epidermal layer is constantly shed and replaced.
Pigment here would fade with normal turnover, not persist.

**C. Subcutaneous Fat**
Lies deep to the dermis and is not the primary site of tattoo pigment deposition.
Pigment is placed more superficially, in the dermis.

**D. Deep Fascia**
A much deeper fibrous layer, well beyond where tattoo needles reach.
Not the site of pigment deposition.

## Mind Capsule

> Tattoo pigment = dermis (macrophages/fibroblasts) = permanent marking, useful for identification.
