# Hypophosphatemia

**Refeeding syndrome** occurs when carbohydrate refeeding after starvation triggers a surge of **insulin**, driving glucose, phosphate, potassium and magnesium into cells. **Hypophosphatemia** is the most important and characteristic abnormality, causing muscle weakness, respiratory failure, and cardiac dysfunction from ATP depletion.

## Trigger Point

> **Most important and characteristic** electrolyte abnormality of refeeding syndrome.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Hypocalcemia**
Can occur but is not the hallmark biochemical change of refeeding syndrome.
It is a minor player compared with phosphate.

**C. Hypokalemia**
Does occur due to insulin-driven intracellular shift, but phosphate depletion is considered the defining and most dangerous abnormality.
It contributes to, but does not define, the syndrome.

**D. Hyponatremia**
Not a characteristic feature of refeeding syndrome; fluid shifts can occur, but sodium disturbance is not the hallmark.
Not the key abnormality tested.

## Mind Capsule

> Refeeding syndrome = insulin surge = cells pull in phosphate (+K+, Mg2+) = hypophosphatemia is the danger.
