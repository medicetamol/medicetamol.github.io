# Toxoplasma

In an HIV patient with **CD4 count below about 100 to 200 cells/mm3** (here markedly less than 40), a **ring-enhancing brain lesion** is most often **cerebral toxoplasmosis**, from reactivation of latent *Toxoplasma gondii*. It typically presents with multiple lesions at the grey-white junction and basal ganglia; empirical anti-toxoplasma therapy (pyrimethamine-sulfadiazine) is usually tried first.

## Trigger Point

> **CD4 <40 + ring-enhancing brain lesion** on contrast imaging: the classic toxoplasmosis picture.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Lymphoma**
Primary CNS lymphoma (EBV-related) also causes ring-enhancing lesions in advanced HIV, but is typically a single lesion and is considered when there is no response to empirical anti-toxoplasma treatment.
It is an important differential, but toxoplasmosis is far more common and is the first-line clinical diagnosis.

**C. Tuberculosis**
Can cause tuberculomas, which may also ring-enhance, but they are less common than toxoplasmosis as a cause of ring-enhancing lesions in this setting.
Not the most likely diagnosis here.

**D. PML**
Caused by JC virus; lesions are typically **non-enhancing**, white-matter lesions without mass effect.
The described lesion is ring-enhancing, which argues against PML.

## Mind Capsule

> HIV + low CD4 + ring-enhancing brain lesion = toxoplasmosis until proven otherwise.
