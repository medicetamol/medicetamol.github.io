# Greater Than One-Third of the Intrathoracic Diameter

In the **Ann Arbor staging** of Hodgkin lymphoma, the **"X" (bulky disease) modifier** is applied when a mediastinal mass has a maximal width **greater than one-third of the maximum internal transverse thoracic diameter** on a standard chest radiograph (or a nodal mass over 10 cm). Bulky disease influences treatment intensity and radiotherapy decisions.

## Trigger Point

> **Mediastinal mass "bulky" (X modifier)**: the fraction of intrathoracic diameter that defines it.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Greater Than One-Half**
Overstates the threshold; the standard cut-off is one-third, not one-half.
Using this would under-call many truly bulky masses.

**C. Greater Than One-Quarter**
Understates the threshold; one-quarter is too low a bar for the standard definition.
It would over-call bulky disease.

**D. Greater Than Two-Thirds**
A much higher threshold than the accepted definition.
It would miss many genuinely bulky masses.

## Mind Capsule

> Bulky mediastinal mass (X) = width more than one-third of the intrathoracic diameter (or nodal mass over 10 cm).
