# Dilated Cardiomyopathy

In **systemic sclerosis**, progressive **myocardial fibrosis** from small-vessel disease and repeated microvascular injury leads to **dilated cardiomyopathy** with diastolic and later systolic dysfunction. Pericardial involvement also occurs, but myocardial fibrosis leading to DCM is the most characteristic cardiac feature.

## Trigger Point

> **Cardiac abnormality most characteristic of systemic sclerosis**: myocardial fibrosis.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Aortic Regurgitation**
Classically associated with conditions like ankylosing spondylitis or syphilitic aortitis.
Not the characteristic scleroderma lesion.

**B. Mitral Stenosis**
The classic valvular lesion of chronic rheumatic heart disease.
Not related to scleroderma pathology.

**C. Pericarditis/Pericardial Effusion**
Does occur in systemic sclerosis and is common, but myocardial fibrosis causing DCM is considered the more characteristic and prognostically important lesion.
It is a real association but not the keyed "most characteristic" answer.

## Mind Capsule

> Systemic sclerosis + heart = patchy myocardial fibrosis = dilated cardiomyopathy (plus pericardial disease).
