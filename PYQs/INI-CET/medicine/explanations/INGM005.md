# Intravenous Fluids (Isotonic Saline)

In acute symptomatic **hypercalcemia of malignancy**, patients are typically volume-depleted (from vomiting, polyuria). The **first step is aggressive isotonic saline rehydration**, which restores GFR and increases renal calcium excretion. **Bisphosphonates** are then added, since their action takes 2 to 4 days to peak.

## Drug Facts

| Agent | Onset | Key adverse effect |
|---|---|---|
| **IV normal saline** | Hours (first step) | Volume overload if overdone |
| Bisphosphonates (zoledronic acid) | 2 to 4 days | Nephrotoxicity, osteonecrosis of jaw |
| Loop diuretics | Adjunct only, after rehydration | Further volume depletion if used too early |
| Calcitonin | Hours (rapid but short-lived) | Tachyphylaxis |

## Trigger Point

> **Symptomatic hypercalcemia of malignancy**: most appropriate FIRST step.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Bisphosphonates**
Effective but slow-onset (days); they are started after or alongside fluids, not as the very first step.
They do not correct the acute volume depletion.

**C. Loop Diuretics**
Once used early to promote calciuresis, but current practice reserves them for volume overload **after** adequate rehydration, since giving them to a volume-depleted patient worsens hypercalcemia.
Not the correct first step.

**D. Find the Source of Exogenous Calcium**
Irrelevant here, since malignancy-associated hypercalcemia is from PTHrP or bone metastases, not exogenous calcium intake.
Does not address the acute emergency.

## Mind Capsule

> Hypercalcemia of malignancy: fluids first (rehydrate), then bisphosphonates for sustained control.
