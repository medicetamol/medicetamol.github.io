# Sarcopenia

**Frailty** is a state of reduced physiological reserve across multiple systems, and **sarcopenia (loss of muscle mass and strength)** is considered its most direct, characteristic biological substrate, driving weakness, slow gait and reduced reserve (as in the Fried frailty phenotype).

## Trigger Point

> Frailty **most directly characterised/affected** by which factor.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Age**
A risk factor for frailty, but frailty is not present in all elderly people and can occur earlier.
Age alone does not define frailty.

**C. Male Gender**
Frailty is in fact more common in women, though men with frailty tend to have worse outcomes.
Gender is not the defining characteristic.

**D. Gait Alone**
Slow gait speed is one component/marker of frailty (Fried criteria), but it is a downstream sign, not the underlying substrate.
Sarcopenia better explains the weakness and slow gait together.

## Mind Capsule

> Frailty = sarcopenia-driven decline in strength, gait speed, and physiological reserve.
