# Right Coronary Artery

**ST elevation in the inferior leads (II, III, aVF)** indicates an **inferior wall MI**, most often from occlusion of the **right coronary artery (RCA)**, which supplies the inferior wall (and often the AV node, explaining associated heart block). Reciprocal ST depression is usually seen in I and aVL.

## Key Finding

> ECG shows ST-segment elevation in leads II, III and aVF.

## Trigger Point

> **ST elevation in II, III, aVF** localises to the inferior wall, supplied predominantly by the RCA.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. LAD**
Supplies the anterior wall and septum; occlusion causes ST elevation in V1-V4 (anterior leads).
Does not match the inferior lead pattern.

**C. LCx**
Supplies the lateral wall; occlusion causes changes in I, aVL, V5-V6.
Not the inferior distribution shown.

**D. Left Main Coronary Artery**
Occlusion causes widespread, often catastrophic ST changes across multiple territories.
The isolated inferior pattern points to a single-vessel (RCA) lesion.

## Mind Capsule

> II, III, aVF = inferior MI = RCA (usually); V1-V4 = anterior = LAD; I, aVL, V5-V6 = lateral = LCx.
