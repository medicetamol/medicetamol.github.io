# Hearing Impairment

Falls in the elderly are typically due to a combination of **gait and balance disturbance, muscle weakness, and musculoskeletal disease** such as rheumatoid arthritis (joint deformity and instability). **Hearing impairment** affects communication and can indirectly increase risk, but it is the **least directly** linked to the mechanics of losing balance and falling, compared with vestibular or visual impairment.

## Trigger Point

> **Least likely DIRECT cause** of falls: the option furthest from balance/gait mechanics.

## Why the Others Are True

**A. Balance Disturbance**
A core, direct mechanism of postural instability and falling.
It is one of the most important causes.

**B. Rheumatoid Arthritis**
Joint deformity, pain and instability directly impair gait and balance.
A well-recognised direct contributor.

**C. Muscle Weakness**
Sarcopenia and lower-limb weakness directly reduce the ability to correct a stumble.
A major direct mechanism.

## Mind Capsule

> Falls in elderly: gait/balance, weakness and joint disease act directly; sensory deficits like hearing are less direct than vision or vestibular loss.
