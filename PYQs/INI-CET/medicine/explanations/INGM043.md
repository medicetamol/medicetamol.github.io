# Meropenem

A **complicated UTI with hypotension** is **urosepsis** in a high-risk (diabetic) patient. Empirical therapy must be **broad-spectrum**, covering resistant Gram-negatives including ESBL producers, so a **carbapenem (meropenem)** is appropriate.

## Trigger Point

> Diabetic man with complicated UTI and hypotension not responding to IV fluids.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Amoxicillin-clavulanate**
An oral agent with limited spectrum, insufficient for septic shock.

**B. Nitrofurantoin**
Achieves levels only in urine; used for uncomplicated cystitis and not for systemic or upper tract infection.

**D. Ceftriaxone**
Reasonable for many UTIs but unreliable against ESBL producers in a septic, high-risk patient.

## Mind Capsule

> Urosepsis with shock in a high-risk patient = broad-spectrum carbapenem empirically.
