# Pacinian Corpuscle

The **Pacinian corpuscle** is a large, encapsulated mechanoreceptor found deep in the dermis and subcutaneous tissue, built of concentric lamellae ("onion-skin" layers) around a single nerve ending, specialised for detecting **rapid vibration and deep pressure**, with very fast adaptation.

## Key Finding

> Image shows a large, encapsulated receptor with concentric ("onion-skin") lamellae in the deep dermis/subcutis.

## Trigger Point

> **Encapsulated sensory nerve ending with a lamellated, onion-skin appearance**: the classic morphology of the Pacinian corpuscle.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Merkel Disc**
An unencapsulated, disc-shaped receptor located in the epidermis, associated with slowly adapting responses to sustained pressure and texture.
Not encapsulated, and a different location and structure.

**C. Ruffini Ending**
An elongated, spindle-shaped encapsulated receptor in the dermis, responding to skin stretch, with a fibrous (not lamellated) capsule.
Different structural appearance from the layered Pacinian corpuscle.

**D. Meissner Corpuscle**
A small, encapsulated receptor located in the dermal papillae (not deep dermis), specialised for light touch, with a stacked rather than concentric lamellar arrangement.
Smaller, more superficial, and structurally distinct from the Pacinian corpuscle.

## Mind Capsule

> Onion-skin lamellated capsule in the deep dermis = Pacinian corpuscle (vibration/deep pressure, rapidly adapting).
