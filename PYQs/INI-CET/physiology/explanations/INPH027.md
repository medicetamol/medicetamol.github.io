# Respiratory Alkalosis

At high altitude, the low partial pressure of oxygen causes **hypoxia**, which stimulates peripheral chemoreceptors and produces **hyperventilation**. The resulting fall in PaCO₂ causes **respiratory alkalosis**, which is later compensated by renal bicarbonate excretion.

## Trigger Point

> "Which of the following is seen in high altitude?"

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Metabolic acidosis**
Not the primary change at altitude; if present, it is a compensation for chronic respiratory alkalosis, not the initial finding.

**B. Metabolic alkalosis**
Caused by vomiting, diuretics or alkali loading, not by hypoxia.

**C. Respiratory acidosis**
Needs CO₂ retention from hypoventilation, the opposite of the altitude response.

## Mind Capsule

> Altitude = low PO₂ → hyperventilation → low PaCO₂ = respiratory alkalosis.
