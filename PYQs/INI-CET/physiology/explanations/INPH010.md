# Glucose Uptake Into Muscle via GLUT4

**Insulin** promotes translocation of **GLUT4 transporters** to the cell surface in **skeletal muscle and adipose tissue**, allowing rapid glucose uptake. Liver glucose uptake, by contrast, occurs mainly via the **insulin-independent GLUT2** transporter.

## Trigger Point

> **Bolus insulin administration**: the classic insulin-responsive glucose uptake occurs via GLUT4 in muscle.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Glucose Uptake Into the Liver via GLUT4**
The liver uses **GLUT2**, not GLUT4, for glucose transport, and hepatic glucose uptake is largely insulin-independent.
Wrong transporter for the liver.

**C. Glucose Uptake Into Muscle via GLUT2**
Muscle relies on the **insulin-sensitive GLUT4** transporter, not GLUT2.
Wrong transporter for muscle tissue.

**D. Glucose Uptake Into the Liver via GLUT2**
While GLUT2 is indeed the liver's transporter, hepatic glucose uptake through GLUT2 is **not primarily insulin-dependent** in the same acute, insulin-bolus-responsive way as muscle GLUT4 translocation.
Does not reflect the classic acute insulin effect being tested here.

## Mind Capsule

> Insulin -> GLUT4 translocation -> glucose uptake into muscle and fat; liver uses insulin-independent GLUT2.
