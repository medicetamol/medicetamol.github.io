# Dentate Gyrus of the Hippocampus

**Adult neurogenesis** in humans is best documented in the **subgranular zone of the dentate gyrus of the hippocampus** (and, more classically described, the subventricular zone), where new neurons continue to be generated and integrated, playing a role in learning and memory.

## Trigger Point

> **Adult neurogenesis site**: a region of the adult brain where neurons can regenerate and increase in number.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Ventral Part of the Spinal Cord**
Spinal cord neurons do **not** regenerate significantly in adults; damaged neurons here are typically permanently lost.
Not a recognised site of adult neurogenesis.

**C. Substantia Nigra**
Dopaminergic neurons here (lost in Parkinson disease) do **not** regenerate in significant numbers in adults.
Not a site of ongoing adult neurogenesis.

**D. Locus Ceruleus**
A noradrenergic nucleus in the brainstem; it is not classically recognised as a site of adult neurogenesis.
Neurons here do not regenerate appreciably.

## Mind Capsule

> Adult neurogenesis: dentate gyrus (hippocampus) and subventricular zone are the classic sites.
