# RCA Biphasic, LCA Predominantly Diastolic

The **left coronary artery** perfuses predominantly during **diastole**, because the high intramyocardial pressure of the thick left ventricle during systole compresses its intramural vessels. The **right coronary artery**, supplying the thinner-walled right ventricle with lower intramyocardial pressure, receives **appreciable flow in both systole and diastole (biphasic pattern)**.

## Trigger Point

> **How RCA flow differs from LCA flow** across the cardiac cycle: RCA is biphasic, LCA is diastole-predominant.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. RCA Almost Entirely in Diastole, LCA Biphasic**
This reverses the actual pattern; it is the **LCA** that is diastole-predominant due to high LV wall tension in systole.
Not consistent with normal coronary physiology.

**C. Both Perfused Only During Systole**
Coronary flow for both arteries is in fact **higher during diastole** overall, not restricted to systole.
Systole is actually the period of reduced coronary flow, especially for the LCA.

**D. Both Perfused Only During Diastole**
While diastole dominates for the LCA, the **RCA** does receive meaningful systolic flow too (biphasic), so "only diastole" for both is inaccurate.
The RCA's biphasic pattern is missed by this option.

## Mind Capsule

> LCA flow = mainly diastole (thick LV compresses vessels in systole); RCA flow = biphasic (systole and diastole).
