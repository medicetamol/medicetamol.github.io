# Visual (Photoreceptors)

Vertebrate **photoreceptors (rods and cones)** are unique among sensory receptors in that they **hyperpolarize in response to their stimulus (light)**. In the dark, a steady "dark current" of Na+ depolarizes photoreceptors via cGMP-gated channels; light closes these channels, causing hyperpolarization and reduced neurotransmitter release.

## Trigger Point

> **Hyperpolarization on stimulation**: the classic exception among sensory receptors is the photoreceptor.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Olfactory**
Odorant binding **depolarizes** olfactory receptor neurons via a cAMP-gated cation channel.
A typical depolarizing sensory receptor.

**C. Taste**
Taste receptor cells generally **depolarize** on stimulation (with mechanisms varying by taste modality).
Does not show the unique hyperpolarizing pattern of vision.

**D. Tactile**
Mechanoreceptors **depolarize** (generator potential) when mechanically stimulated, triggering action potentials.
A standard depolarizing sensory response.

## Mind Capsule

> Vision is unique: light hyperpolarizes photoreceptors; all other special senses depolarize on stimulation.
