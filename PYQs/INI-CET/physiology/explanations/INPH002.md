# Reduced Renal Perfusion Pressure

**Renin release from the juxtaglomerular apparatus** is stimulated by three main pathways: **reduced renal perfusion pressure** (sensed by baroreceptors in the afferent arteriole), **reduced sodium delivery to the macula densa**, and **increased renal sympathetic activity** (beta-1 stimulation).

## Trigger Point

> **Stimulus for renin secretion**: reduced renal perfusion pressure activates the baroreceptor mechanism.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Elevated Sodium Delivery to the Macula Densa**
It is **reduced**, not elevated, sodium delivery to the macula densa that stimulates renin release.
The direction given here is reversed.

**B. Elevated Renal Perfusion Pressure**
Higher perfusion pressure **suppresses**, not stimulates, renin release via the baroreceptor mechanism.
Opposite of the correct stimulus.

**D. Increased Extracellular Fluid Volume**
Volume expansion **suppresses** the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system through multiple feedback pathways.
This would reduce, not increase, renin secretion.

## Mind Capsule

> Renin release triggers: low perfusion pressure, low macula densa sodium, high renal sympathetic tone.
