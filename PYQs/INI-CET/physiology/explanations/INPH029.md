# Inspiration

**Surfactant** lowers surface tension by lying at the alveolar air-liquid interface. During **inspiration** the alveolar surface area increases, so surfactant molecules spread apart and their concentration per unit area falls, which makes **surface tension rise**. During expiration the area shrinks, surfactant is packed closely and tension falls.

## Trigger Point

> "During which situation does the surface tension of the fluid lining the alveoli increase?"

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Expiration**
Alveolar area decreases, surfactant becomes concentrated and surface tension is lowest. This stabilises small alveoli.

**C. Standing**
A posture, not a change in alveolar surface area; it does not directly alter surface tension.

**D. Supine**
Also a posture; it changes lung volume and ventilation distribution, not the surfactant density per unit area.

## Mind Capsule

> Inspiration = bigger alveolus = diluted surfactant = higher surface tension.
