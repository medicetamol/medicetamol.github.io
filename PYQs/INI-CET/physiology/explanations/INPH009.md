# Thick Ascending Loop of Henle

The **thick ascending limb of the loop of Henle** actively pumps NaCl out of the tubular fluid (via the Na-K-2Cl cotransporter) while remaining **impermeable to water**, establishing the hypertonic medullary interstitium. This is the engine of the **counter-current multiplier**, which is essential for the kidney's ability to concentrate urine.

## Trigger Point

> **Counter-current multiplier of the nephron**: the segment responsible is the thick ascending loop of Henle.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Thin Ascending Loop of Henle**
Also impermeable to water and contributes passively to the medullary gradient, but the **active** NaCl transport that truly "multiplies" the gradient occurs in the **thick** ascending limb.
The thin segment's role is passive, not the active multiplying step.

**C. Collecting Duct**
Functions as the **counter-current exchanger's final target**, where ADH-regulated water reabsorption occurs; it does not generate the medullary gradient itself.
It uses the gradient, rather than creating it.

**D. Vasa Recta**
Acts as a **counter-current exchanger** (passively preserving, not creating, the medullary gradient by matching blood flow to the osmotic gradient), a distinct concept from the multiplier.
Exchanger, not multiplier.

## Mind Capsule

> Thick ascending loop of Henle = active NaCl pump, water-impermeable = the counter-current multiplier; vasa recta = counter-current exchanger.
