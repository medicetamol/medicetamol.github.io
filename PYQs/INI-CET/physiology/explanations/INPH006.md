# SGLT (Sodium-Glucose Cotransporter)

**Oral rehydration therapy (ORT)** for secretory diarrhoea exploits the **SGLT1 sodium-glucose cotransporter** on enterocytes, which remains functional even when cAMP-driven chloride secretion (via CFTR) is pathologically increased (as in cholera). Co-transporting glucose with sodium drives water absorption osmotically, correcting dehydration.

## Trigger Point

> **Oral rehydration in secretory diarrhoea**: the transporter exploited is the sodium-glucose cotransporter.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. CFTR Chloride Channel**
This is the channel **overactivated** by toxins like cholera toxin, **causing** secretory diarrhoea; it is the problem, not the therapeutic target.
It is not what ORT exploits for treatment.

**C. Na+/K+ ATPase Pump**
Maintains the basolateral sodium gradient that powers SGLT1 indirectly, but it is not itself the transporter exploited for oral glucose-based rehydration at the luminal membrane.
The direct clinical mechanism is the apical SGLT.

**D. Aquaporin-2 Water Channel**
Regulated by ADH mainly in the renal collecting duct, not the basis of intestinal glucose-linked sodium and water absorption.
Not relevant to ORT's mechanism.

## Mind Capsule

> ORT works because SGLT1 (sodium-glucose cotransport) still functions in secretory diarrhoea, pulling water in with sodium and glucose.
