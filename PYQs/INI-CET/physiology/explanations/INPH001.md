# Increased Respiratory Drive (Hyperventilation)

**Progesterone** during pregnancy acts on the **respiratory centre in the medulla**, increasing its sensitivity to CO2 and directly stimulating ventilation. This causes **increased tidal volume and minute ventilation (hyperventilation)**, lowering maternal arterial PCO2 (mild respiratory alkalosis), which favours transfer of fetal CO2 to the mother.

## Trigger Point

> **Respiratory effect of progesterone in pregnancy**: increased respiratory drive.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Decreased Tidal Volume**
Progesterone actually **increases**, not decreases, tidal volume as part of the hyperventilatory response of pregnancy.
The opposite change occurs.

**C. Decreased Arterial PO2**
Maternal PO2 is generally maintained or even slightly increased due to hyperventilation, not decreased.
This does not match the known effect of progesterone.

**D. Increased Arterial PCO2**
Hyperventilation **lowers**, not raises, arterial PCO2, producing the mild respiratory alkalosis of pregnancy.
This is the opposite of the actual physiological change.

## Mind Capsule

> Progesterone in pregnancy = raises respiratory drive = hyperventilation = lower maternal PCO2 (mild respiratory alkalosis).
