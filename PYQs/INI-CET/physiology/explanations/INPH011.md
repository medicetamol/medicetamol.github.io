# Decreased LH and Decreased FSH

Exogenous **testosterone therapy** raises circulating androgen levels, which feeds back negatively on the **hypothalamic-pituitary axis**, suppressing **GnRH pulsatility** and thereby lowering both **LH and FSH**. This is also why exogenous testosterone can suppress spermatogenesis (via reduced intratesticular testosterone driven by low LH, plus low FSH).

## Trigger Point

> **Testosterone therapy effect on gonadotropins**: negative feedback lowers both LH and FSH.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Increased LH, Decreased FSH**
Exogenous testosterone **suppresses**, rather than raises, LH through negative feedback.
The LH direction here is incorrect.

**B. Decreased LH, Increased FSH**
FSH is also suppressed by the elevated androgen/oestrogen (aromatised) feedback and by inhibin-independent mechanisms; it does not rise.
The FSH direction here is incorrect.

**C. Increased LH, Increased FSH**
Both gonadotropins fall, not rise, as testosterone feeds back to reduce hypothalamic-pituitary drive.
Both directions are incorrect in this option.

## Mind Capsule

> Exogenous testosterone = negative feedback on the HPG axis = decreased LH and decreased FSH (and can suppress spermatogenesis).
