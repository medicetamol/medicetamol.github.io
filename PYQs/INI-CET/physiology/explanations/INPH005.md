# A (Cation)

The **glomerular filtration barrier carries a net negative charge** (from heparan sulfate proteoglycans in the basement membrane), which **repels anions and favours filtration of cations** of a given molecular radius. So for the same size, a **cationic molecule (curve A)** is filtered most readily, followed by neutral, then anionic molecules filtered least (this is why albumin, an anion, is normally poorly filtered despite its size).

## Key Finding

> Graph shows filterability by molecular radius for three curves A, B, C, with filterability order A > B > C and C marked as anions.

## Trigger Point

> **Based on charge, which is filtered most readily**: the cationic curve, A.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. B (Neutral)**
Neutral molecules are filtered based on size alone, without the charge-based repulsion or attraction, placing them **between** cations and anions.
Filtered less readily than cations of the same size.

**C. C (Anion)**
Anions are **repelled** by the negatively charged basement membrane, making them filtered **least** readily for a given size.
This is the opposite of "filtered most readily."

**D. All Are Filtered Equally**
This ignores the well-established charge-selective property of the glomerular barrier.
Charge clearly affects filterability, as shown by the graph.

## Mind Capsule

> Glomerular barrier is charge-selective: cations filtered best, anions filtered worst, for the same molecular size.
