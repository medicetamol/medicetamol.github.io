# Cadherins, Desmosomes, and Gap Junctions

The **intercalated disc** of cardiac muscle links adjacent cardiomyocytes end to end using three components: **fascia adherens (cadherin-based)** for mechanical anchoring of actin filaments, **desmosomes** for additional mechanical strength, and **gap junctions** for electrical coupling between cells. **Tight junctions** are not a feature of the intercalated disc.

## Trigger Point

> **Composition of the intercalated disc**: cadherins, desmosomes, and gap junctions — but not tight junctions.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Desmosomes, Gap Junctions, and Tight Junctions**
Tight junctions are not part of the intercalated disc; they are a feature of sealing epithelia, not cardiac muscle cell junctions.
Incorrectly substitutes tight junctions for cadherin-based adherens junctions.

**C. Cadherins, Gap Junctions, and Tight Junctions**
Again wrongly includes tight junctions, and omits desmosomes, which are a genuine structural component of the intercalated disc.
Two components are misrepresented here.

**D. All Four (Including Tight Junctions)**
Overinclusive; tight junctions are not a recognised component of the cardiac intercalated disc.
The correct answer excludes tight junctions specifically.

## Mind Capsule

> Intercalated disc = fascia adherens (cadherins) + desmosomes (mechanical) + gap junctions (electrical); no tight junctions.
