# A, C, D and E Only

**Insulin** increases protein synthesis and decreases blood glucose; **epinephrine** increases glycogenolysis; and **glucagon** increases gluconeogenesis — all true, standard hormone actions. **Thyroxine**, however, **increases** (not decreases) the basal metabolic rate, making that specific statement false.

## Statement Check

| Statement | True/False | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| A. Insulin increases protein synthesis | True | An established anabolic action of insulin |
| B. Thyroxine decreases BMR | **False** | Thyroxine **increases** BMR |
| C. Insulin decreases blood glucose | True | Insulin's principal, defining action |
| D. Epinephrine increases glycogenolysis | True | Part of the fight-or-flight glucose-mobilising response |
| E. Glucagon increases gluconeogenesis | True | A key counter-regulatory action of glucagon |

## Trigger Point

> Identify the one **false** statement among the five (thyroxine and BMR) to select the correct combination of true ones.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. A, B and C Only**
Incorrectly includes the false thyroxine statement (B) and omits two genuinely true statements (D and E).
Both the inclusion and the omissions are wrong.

**B. A, C and D Only**
Omits statement E (glucagon and gluconeogenesis), which is also a true, standard hormone action.
Leaves out a correct statement unnecessarily.

**D. All of the Above**
Includes the false thyroxine statement (B), which contradicts thyroxine's true metabolic effect of **raising**, not lowering, BMR.
Overinclusive by one incorrect statement.

## Mind Capsule

> Thyroxine increases BMR (never decreases it); insulin, epinephrine and glucagon act as classically taught.
