# Proteins

The **glomerular filtration barrier** (fenestrated endothelium, basement membrane, podocyte slit diaphragms) normally restricts filtration of large and negatively charged molecules, most importantly **plasma proteins** such as albumin. Damage to this barrier allows proteins to leak into the filtrate, producing **proteinuria**.

## Trigger Point

> **Damage to the glomerular capillary wall**: the substance normally excluded that now appears in urine is protein.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Uric Acid**
A small, freely filtered solute that is normally present in urine regardless of glomerular barrier integrity; its handling is mainly tubular, not barrier-dependent.
Not the classic marker of glomerular barrier damage.

**B. Glucose**
Freely filtered at the glomerulus and normally fully **reabsorbed by the proximal tubule**; glucose in urine (glycosuria) reflects a tubular reabsorption/transport limit (as in diabetes), not glomerular barrier damage.
A tubular, not glomerular, phenomenon.

**C. Ketones**
Small, freely filtered molecules that appear in urine during ketosis due to increased production, unrelated to glomerular barrier integrity.
Their presence reflects a metabolic state, not barrier damage.

## Mind Capsule

> Glomerular barrier damage = proteinuria (loss of normally excluded plasma proteins, especially albumin).
