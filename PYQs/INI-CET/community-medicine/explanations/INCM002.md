# Children Receiving Healthcare <20%

In colour-coded district performance dashboards, **red denotes poor performance**. The colour depends on the direction of the indicator: for **coverage indicators** (higher is better), a very **low** value is poor. So children receiving healthcare below 20% is the red card. The exact cut-offs are programme-specific and are not standardised in textbooks.

## Trigger Point

> **RED card (poor performance)**: which indicator, at the stated criterion, signals poor performance?

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. IMR Less Than 20%**
Infant mortality rate is an outcome indicator where a **low value is desirable**.
A low IMR would signal good performance, not a red card (and IMR is per 1000 live births, not a percentage).

**B. TFR More Than 20%**
Total fertility rate is expressed as children per woman, not as a percentage.
The criterion is not a meaningful red-card threshold.

**C. MMR <20%**
Maternal mortality ratio is an outcome where **lower is better** (per 100,000 live births).
A low value signals good performance.

## Mind Capsule

> Red = poor: a low coverage indicator or a high mortality indicator; low mortality is never red.
