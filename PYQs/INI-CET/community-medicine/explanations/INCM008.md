# Odds Ratio = 6

In a case-control study, the **odds ratio** compares the odds of exposure in cases with the odds of exposure in controls: **OR = (a x d) / (b x c)**.

## Working

| | Exposed | Unexposed |
|---|---|---|
| Cases | a = 60 | b = 20 |
| Controls | c = 40 | d = 80 |

1. Odds of exposure in cases = 60/20 = 3
2. Odds of exposure in controls = 40/80 = 0.5
3. OR = 3 / 0.5 = (60 x 80) / (20 x 40) = 4800 / 800 = **6**

## Trigger Point

> Cases: **60 exposed, 20 unexposed**; controls: **40 exposed, 80 unexposed**.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. 3**
This is only the odds of exposure among cases (60/20).
It ignores the controls.

**C. 1.5**
This is 60/40, the ratio of exposed cases to exposed controls.
It is not a cross-product ratio.

**D. 0.17**
This is the reciprocal (1/6) and comes from inverting the cross-product.
It would imply a protective effect.

## Mind Capsule

> Odds ratio = ad / bc; OR above 1 means exposure is associated with disease.
