# Ecological Study

An **ecological (correlational) study** uses **populations or groups, not individuals, as the unit of analysis**, comparing aggregate exposure with aggregate outcome (here, average alcohol intake versus cirrhosis mortality per country). It is quick and cheap, but prone to the **ecological fallacy**.

## Trigger Point

> **One data point per country**: average intake plotted against mortality rate for 10 countries.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Cohort Study**
Follows exposed and unexposed individuals over time to compare incidence.
Individual-level follow-up is absent here.

**C. Case-Control Study**
Compares past exposure between individuals with the disease (cases) and without (controls).
No individual cases or controls are selected here.

**D. Cross-Sectional Study**
Measures exposure and outcome in individuals at one point in time (prevalence).
The data here are group averages.

## Mind Capsule

> Unit = population (aggregate data) = ecological study; risk = ecological fallacy.
