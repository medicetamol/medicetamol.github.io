# Measles

A **propagated (progressive) epidemic** arises from **person-to-person transmission**: each generation of cases infects the next after roughly one incubation period, so the epidemic curve shows **successive peaks spaced by the incubation period**. Measles, with its high secondary attack rate and short generation interval, is the standard example.

## Trigger Point

> **Multiple peaks separated by the incubation period** = propagated epidemic.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Staphylococcal Food Poisoning**
A **common-source point epidemic** from a preformed enterotoxin, with an incubation of only a few hours.
One sharp peak, with no person-to-person spread.

**C. Typhoid Fever (Common-Source)**
As worded, this is a water or food-borne outbreak from a single contaminated source.
It gives a single peak (or a prolonged plateau if exposure continues), not serial peaks.

**D. Salmonella Food Poisoning**
Also a common-source outbreak from a contaminated meal; cases cluster within one incubation period.
It gives a single peak.

## Mind Capsule

> Propagated = person-to-person = serial peaks one incubation period apart (measles); common source = single peak.
