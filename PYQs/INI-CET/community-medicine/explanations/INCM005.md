# Variation Between Included Studies

**Heterogeneity** in a meta-analysis is the variation in effect estimates across the pooled studies beyond what chance would explain. It is assessed with **Cochran's Q test and the I-squared statistic**, and visually on the **forest plot**. High heterogeneity favours a random-effects model or a search for its source.

## Trigger Point

> **Assessing heterogeneity** (Cochran's Q or I-squared) in a meta-analysis.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Detects Publication Bias Directly**
Publication bias is assessed with a **funnel plot** and tests such as Egger's.
Heterogeneity tests do not detect it directly.

**C. Measures Confounding Within a Single Study**
Confounding is handled by stratification, matching or regression within a study.
Heterogeneity is a between-study concept.

**D. Quantifies the Precision of an Individual Study**
Precision is shown by the confidence interval and weight of each study.
It is not what Q or I-squared measures.

## Mind Capsule

> Heterogeneity = between-study variation (Cochran's Q, I-squared); funnel plot = publication bias.
