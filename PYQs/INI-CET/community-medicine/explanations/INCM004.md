# Population Attributable Risk

**Population attributable risk (PAR)** is the proportion of disease in the **whole population** that is due to an exposure and would be removed if the exposure were eliminated: **PAR = (Ip - Iu) / Ip**, where Ip is the incidence in the total population and Iu in the unexposed. It shows the public health impact of removing a risk factor.

## Trigger Point

> **Proportion of strokes in the population** that would be prevented by removing hypertension.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Relative Risk**
The ratio of incidence in the exposed to the unexposed; it measures strength of association.
It says nothing about the burden in the population.

**B. Attributable Risk**
The excess incidence **among the exposed** that is due to the exposure (Ie - Iu).
It is limited to the exposed group, not the whole population.

**D. Odds Ratio**
The measure of association in case-control studies.
It does not give a population burden.

## Mind Capsule

> Attributable risk = among the exposed; population attributable risk = across the whole population.
