# Polyarteritis Nodosa

**Polyarteritis nodosa (PAN)** is the classic example of a **medium-vessel vasculitis**, causing necrotising inflammation of medium-sized muscular arteries with microaneurysm formation, typically sparing the lungs and glomeruli, and classically associated with **hepatitis B**.

## Classification

| Vessel size | Example vasculitis |
|---|---|
| Small vessel | Leukocytoclastic vasculitis, GPA, MPA |
| **Medium vessel** | **Polyarteritis nodosa, Kawasaki disease** |
| Large vessel | Giant cell arteritis, Takayasu arteritis |

## Trigger Point

> **Classic example of medium-vessel vasculitis**: polyarteritis nodosa.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Leukocytoclastic (Cutaneous Small-Vessel) Vasculitis**
Involves post-capillary venules and small vessels in the skin.
Categorised as small-vessel, not medium-vessel.

**C. Granulomatosis with Polyangiitis**
An ANCA-associated **small-vessel** vasculitis affecting the respiratory tract and kidneys.
Not a medium-vessel disease.

**D. Microscopic Polyangiitis**
Also a **small-vessel**, ANCA-associated vasculitis, typically without granulomas.
Falls in the small-vessel category, unlike PAN.

## Mind Capsule

> Medium-vessel vasculitis = PAN (and Kawasaki); most ANCA-associated vasculitides are small-vessel.
