# Barrett's Esophagus

**Barrett's esophagus** is **metaplasia of the normal stratified squamous epithelium of the lower esophagus into columnar, intestinal-type epithelium with goblet cells**, occurring as an adaptive response to chronic gastroesophageal reflux. It is a premalignant condition that raises the risk of esophageal adenocarcinoma.

## Key Finding

> Lower esophageal mucosa shows columnar (intestinal-type) epithelium with goblet cells replacing squamous epithelium.

## Trigger Point

> **Columnar epithelium with goblet cells replacing squamous epithelium** in the lower esophagus: the defining histology of Barrett's.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Squamous Cell Carcinoma**
Arises from native squamous epithelium (often linked to smoking/alcohol), not from columnar metaplasia.
The finding described is metaplastic, not malignant squamous tissue.

**C. Adenocarcinoma**
Can arise **from** Barrett's mucosa but shows invasive malignant glands, not simple goblet-cell-containing columnar metaplasia.
The description given is of metaplasia, not invasive cancer.

**D. Low-Grade Dysplasia**
A later step that can develop **within** Barrett's mucosa, showing architectural and cytological atypia.
No dysplastic changes are described here, only metaplastic columnar epithelium.

## Mind Capsule

> Squamous to columnar with goblet cells in the esophagus = Barrett's esophagus (a premalignant metaplasia).
