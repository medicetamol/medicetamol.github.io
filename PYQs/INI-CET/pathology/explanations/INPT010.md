# Dense Neutrophilic Infiltration

In myocardial infarction, histology evolves in a predictable sequence. At **1 to 3 days**, **coagulative necrosis** is established and a **dense neutrophilic (acute inflammatory) infiltrate** appears at the margins of the necrotic zone, the hallmark of this interval.

## Classification

| Time after MI | Key histology |
|---|---|
| 0 to 24 hours | Early coagulative necrosis, wavy fibres; may look normal grossly |
| **1 to 3 days** | **Coagulative necrosis with dense neutrophilic infiltrate** |
| 3 to 7 days | Macrophages remove debris; granulation tissue begins |
| 7 to 10 days (and beyond) | Granulation tissue, then collagenous scar |

## Trigger Point

> Myocardium **1 to 3 days after MI**: the characteristic cellular change is a neutrophilic infiltrate.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Coagulative Necrosis with Wavy Fibres Only**
This describes the **earliest** phase (hours), before neutrophils have infiltrated.
The 1 to 3 day window is defined by the added neutrophilic response.

**C. Macrophage Infiltration with Phagocytosis**
Characteristic of the **later phase (about 3 to 7 days)**, once neutrophils have been replaced by macrophages.
Too late for the 1 to 3 day window specified.

**D. Dense Collagenous Scar**
Represents the **healed** infarct (weeks onward).
Far later than the 1 to 3 day time frame.

## Mind Capsule

> MI timeline: hours = coagulative necrosis; 1 to 3 days = neutrophils; days = macrophages; weeks = scar.
