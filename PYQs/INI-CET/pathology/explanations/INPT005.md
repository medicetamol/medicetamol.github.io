# Ineffective (Defective) Erythropoiesis

In **beta-thalassemia major**, deficient beta-globin chains leave excess unpaired **alpha-chains**, which precipitate in erythroid precursors, damaging the cell membrane and causing **intramedullary destruction of red cell precursors (ineffective erythropoiesis)** as well as peripheral haemolysis. This drives massive marrow expansion (bone deformities) and, through **suppressed hepcidin**, secondary iron overload.

## Trigger Point

> Beta-thalassemia major **disease mechanism**: destruction of red cell precursors within the marrow.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Hepcidin is Markedly Increased**
The opposite occurs: ineffective erythropoiesis (via erythroferrone) **suppresses hepcidin**, promoting iron absorption and overload.
Increased hepcidin would restrict, not promote, iron overload.

**C. Hepcidin is Suppressed, Causing Iron Overload**
This is a genuinely true downstream consequence of ineffective erythropoiesis, but the core disease mechanism being asked for is the ineffective erythropoiesis itself.
This option describes a consequence rather than the primary mechanism.

**D. Predominantly Intravascular Hemolysis**
Hemolysis in thalassemia is mainly **extravascular** (in the spleen), in addition to the more prominent intramedullary destruction.
Intravascular hemolysis is not the characteristic mechanism here.

## Mind Capsule

> Beta-thalassemia major = alpha-chain excess = ineffective erythropoiesis (+ hemolysis) = hepcidin suppression = iron overload.
