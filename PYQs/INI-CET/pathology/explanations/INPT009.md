# Subepithelial Electron-Dense "Humps"

**Post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis (PSGN)** follows a sore throat (or skin infection) by 1 to 3 weeks and presents with **cola-coloured urine, periorbital oedema, hypertension and RBC casts** (acute nephritic syndrome). Electron microscopy shows characteristic **subepithelial "hump"-shaped electron-dense deposits** of immune complexes.

## Trigger Point

> **Sore throat two weeks ago + nephritic syndrome**: classic PSGN, with subepithelial humps on EM.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Subendothelial Deposits with Tram-Track Splitting**
This describes **membranoproliferative glomerulonephritis (MPGN)**, which usually has a more indolent, nephrotic-nephritic course, not a sharp post-infectious onset.
The described timeline and presentation fit PSGN, not MPGN.

**C. Diffuse Podocyte Foot Process Effacement, No Deposits**
This is the classic finding of **minimal change disease**, presenting with nephrotic syndrome, not nephritic syndrome with red cell casts.
There is no deposit-free podocyte pattern described here.

**D. Linear Ribbon-Like Deposits Along GBM**
This describes **anti-GBM (Goodpasture) disease**, with linear IgG deposition, not the granular "hump" pattern of PSGN.
Linear deposits are a different immunofluorescence pattern from what is seen here.

## Mind Capsule

> Post-strep nephritic syndrome = subepithelial humps on EM = "starry sky" granular pattern on immunofluorescence.
