# Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

**Immunohistochemistry** uses labelled antibodies to detect a **specific protein/antigen directly within formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tissue sections**, preserving morphology while localising the target (for example, hormone receptors in breast cancer, or specific tumour markers).

## Trigger Point

> **Identify a specific protein in FFPE tissue**: the technique that works directly on fixed tissue sections is IHC.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. FISH**
Detects specific **DNA/RNA sequences** (gene amplification or translocation) using labelled probes, not protein expression directly.
It targets nucleic acids, not proteins.

**C. Western Blot**
Detects proteins after **extraction and electrophoresis** from homogenised tissue or cells, losing all spatial/morphological information.
It cannot be performed directly on an intact FFPE section.

**D. Flow Cytometry**
Analyses proteins/antigens on **cells in suspension**, not on intact fixed tissue sections.
Requires a single-cell suspension, unlike IHC.

## Mind Capsule

> Protein localisation in intact FFPE tissue = immunohistochemistry.
