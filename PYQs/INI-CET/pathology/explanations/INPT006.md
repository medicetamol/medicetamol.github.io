# Iron Deficiency Anemia

**Low Hb, low MCV (microcytic) and low serum ferritin** together define **iron deficiency anemia**: ferritin reflects body iron stores and is the most specific and sensitive marker, falling before anemia even develops.

## Trigger Point

> **Microcytic anemia with low ferritin**: the combination that confirms iron deficiency rather than another microcytic cause.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Megaloblastic Anemia**
Caused by B12 or folate deficiency, giving a **macrocytic (high MCV)** picture, not microcytic.
The MCV here is low, ruling this out.

**C. Aplastic Anemia**
Typically shows **normocytic** anemia with pancytopenia from marrow failure.
Ferritin is usually normal or elevated, not low, and MCV is not characteristically reduced.

**D. Hemolytic Anemia**
Usually **normocytic or mildly macrocytic** (from reticulocytosis), with normal or raised ferritin and raised indirect bilirubin/LDH.
Low ferritin with microcytosis is not the hemolytic pattern.

## Mind Capsule

> Microcytic anemia + low ferritin = iron deficiency anemia (most common cause of microcytic anemia).
