# SCID

**Rejection** is a T-cell-mediated process. In **SCID**, T cells (and usually B cells) are absent or non-functional, so the host cannot recognise or reject a graft. This is also why SCID patients are at risk of graft-versus-host disease.

## Trigger Point

> "In which situation does transplant rejection **not** occur?"

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Bare lymphocyte syndrome**
A defect of MHC expression; some T-cell immunity persists, so rejection can still occur.

**B. Chronic granulomatous disease**
A phagocyte (NADPH oxidase) defect. T-cell function is intact, so graft rejection occurs normally.

**D. DiGeorge syndrome**
Thymic hypoplasia is usually partial, leaving some functional T cells that are capable of rejection.

## Mind Capsule

> Rejection needs functioning T cells; SCID = no T cells = no rejection (but GVHD risk).
