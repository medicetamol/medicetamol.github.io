# Anemia in Chronic Kidney Disease

In CKD, the failing kidney produces too little **erythropoietin (EPO)**, which is the main cause of the anemia. Replacement with **erythropoiesis-stimulating agents** corrects it effectively.

## Trigger Point

> "In which condition is erythropoietin considered beneficial for treatment?"

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Anemia in myelodysplastic syndrome**
ESAs help only a selected group of low-risk patients with low endogenous EPO, so it is not the classic indication.

**C. Nutritional anemia**
The cause is iron, B12 or folate deficiency, and replacing the missing nutrient is the treatment.

**D. Aplastic anemia**
Endogenous EPO levels are already high, and marrow failure means extra EPO does not work.

## Mind Capsule

> EPO deficiency (CKD) = EPO replacement works; EPO already high (aplastic anemia) = no benefit.
