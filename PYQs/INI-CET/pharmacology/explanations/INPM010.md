# Pralidoxime

**Pralidoxime (2-PAM)** is an **oxime that reactivates acetylcholinesterase** by cleaving the phosphate-enzyme bond formed in organophosphate poisoning, provided it is given before "ageing" of the bond occurs. It is used alongside **atropine**, which controls symptoms but does not reverse enzyme inhibition.

## Drug Facts

| Drug | Role in OP poisoning |
|---|---|
| Atropine | Blocks muscarinic effects (symptomatic) |
| **Pralidoxime** | **Reactivates AChE (regenerates the enzyme)** |

## Trigger Point

> **Reverses the inhibited AChE activity**: an oxime that reactivates the enzyme, not just controls symptoms.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Atropine**
A muscarinic antagonist that blocks the effects of excess acetylcholine but does **not** reactivate the enzyme itself.
It treats symptoms, not the underlying enzyme inhibition.

**C. Flumazenil**
A benzodiazepine receptor antagonist, used for benzodiazepine overdose, unrelated to AChE.
Wrong poisoning entirely.

**D. Naloxone**
An opioid receptor antagonist used in opioid overdose.
Not relevant to organophosphate poisoning.

## Mind Capsule

> OP poisoning: atropine treats symptoms; pralidoxime reactivates AChE (give early, before ageing).
