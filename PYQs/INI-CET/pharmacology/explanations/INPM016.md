# First-Order Kinetics

In **first-order kinetics**, the **rate of elimination is proportional to drug concentration**, so a **constant fraction** (not a constant amount) is eliminated per unit time, and the **half-life stays constant** regardless of the starting concentration. Most drugs at therapeutic doses follow first-order kinetics.

## Trigger Point

> **Constant half-life regardless of starting concentration, constant fraction eliminated per unit time**: the definition of first-order kinetics.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Zero-Order Kinetics**
A **constant amount** (not fraction) is eliminated per unit time, and half-life **changes** with concentration (it is not constant), as seen with ethanol, phenytoin, and aspirin at high/saturating doses.
This is the opposite of the pattern described.

**C. Michaelis-Menten (Saturation) Kinetics at Saturating Dose**
At saturating doses, elimination approaches **zero-order** behaviour (a fixed amount per unit time), not a constant half-life.
Inconsistent with the constant half-life described in the question.

**D. Mixed-Order Kinetics**
Describes drugs that behave as first-order at low concentrations and zero-order at high (saturating) concentrations, so it does not have one single constant half-life across all levels.
The question describes one pure, constant pattern (first-order) throughout.

## Mind Capsule

> First-order: constant fraction eliminated, constant half-life; zero-order: constant amount eliminated, variable half-life.
