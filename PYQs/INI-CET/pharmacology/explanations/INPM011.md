# Ticagrelor is a Reversible P2Y12 Inhibitor

**Ticagrelor** is a **direct-acting, reversible P2Y12 receptor antagonist** (unlike clopidogrel and prasugrel, which are irreversible thienopyridines requiring hepatic activation). It is given **twice daily** (not once daily) and, in major trials, showed **similar or lower** rates of major bleeding compared with clopidogrel, though with a higher rate of non-CABG-related minor bleeding and dyspnoea.

## Trigger Point

> **TRUE statement about ticagrelor**: identify the correct pharmacological property.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. It is a Prodrug Requiring Hepatic Activation**
This describes **clopidogrel and prasugrel**, not ticagrelor, which is administered as the active drug itself.
Ticagrelor does not need hepatic bioactivation to work.

**C. It Causes More Cerebral Bleeding Than Clopidogrel**
Major trial data (PLATO) did not show an excess of intracranial/cerebral bleeding with ticagrelor compared with clopidogrel overall.
This overstates its bleeding risk profile.

**D. It is Given Once Daily**
Ticagrelor's reversible binding and shorter half-life necessitate **twice-daily** dosing, unlike clopidogrel or prasugrel.
Once-daily dosing does not match its actual regimen.

## Mind Capsule

> Ticagrelor = direct-acting, reversible P2Y12 blocker, twice daily; clopidogrel/prasugrel = irreversible prodrugs, once daily.
