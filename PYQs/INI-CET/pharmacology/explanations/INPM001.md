# Generalised Tonic-Clonic Seizures

**Lamotrigine** is a broad-spectrum antiepileptic (blocking voltage-gated sodium channels) effective across multiple seizure types, and is a recognised **first-line/adjunctive option for generalised tonic-clonic seizures**. Notably, it can **worsen myoclonic seizures** in some patients, which is a key exception to its broad-spectrum reputation.

## Drug Facts

| Drug | Seizure use | Key adverse effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Lamotrigine** | **GTCS, focal, absence** (can worsen myoclonus) | Stevens-Johnson syndrome, dizziness |

## Trigger Point

> **Broad-spectrum AED, first/adjunctive choice**: for which seizure type is lamotrigine particularly useful.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Myoclonic Seizures**
Lamotrigine can actually **worsen** myoclonic seizures in some patients, making it a poor and even cautioned choice here.
Valproate or levetiracetam are preferred for myoclonic seizures instead.

**C. Absence Seizures**
Lamotrigine has some efficacy in absence seizures, but ethosuximide and valproate remain the classic first-line agents for this seizure type specifically.
It is not the primary recognised choice for absence seizures.

**D. Atonic Seizures**
Valproate and lamotrigine can both be used, but atonic seizures are not the classic, specifically highlighted indication compared with GTCS.
GTCS is the better-established and specifically tested indication.

## Mind Capsule

> Lamotrigine = broad-spectrum, good for GTCS and focal seizures, but can worsen myoclonic seizures.
