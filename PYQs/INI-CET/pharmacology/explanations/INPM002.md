# N-Type Calcium Channel

**Cilnidipine** is a dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker that, in addition to blocking **L-type channels** in vascular smooth muscle, also blocks **N-type calcium channels** on sympathetic nerve terminals. This reduces noradrenaline release, blunting the sympathetic/reflex tachycardia typically seen with other dihydropyridines like amlodipine.

## Trigger Point

> **Cilnidipine, unique among DHP-CCBs**, avoids reflex tachycardia by an additional channel block: N-type.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. T-Type Channels**
Blocked by agents like efonidipine (a dual L/T-type blocker), which reduces both cardiac and renal effects, but this is not cilnidipine's mechanism.
Different channel subtype and different drug.

**B. R-Type Channels**
Not a clinically targeted calcium channel subtype for antihypertensive drugs in standard pharmacology.
Not relevant to cilnidipine's mechanism.

**D. L-Type Channels**
Already blocked by essentially all dihydropyridine CCBs (this is the basic class action); it does not explain cilnidipine's unique lack of reflex tachycardia.
The added, distinguishing action is on N-type channels.

## Mind Capsule

> Cilnidipine = L-type + N-type calcium channel blocker = vasodilation without reflex tachycardia.
