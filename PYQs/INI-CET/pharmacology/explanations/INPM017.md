# Inhibition of Mycolic Acid Synthesis (and Reactive Nitrogen Species)

**Pretomanid**, a nitroimidazole used in the **BPaL/BPaLM regimen** for drug-resistant TB, is a prodrug activated by mycobacterial enzymes to inhibit **mycolic acid biosynthesis** (impairing cell-wall formation) and to generate **reactive nitrogen species**, which are toxic under the anaerobic conditions found in TB lesions.

## Trigger Point

> **Pretomanid, BPaL/BPaLM regimen, mechanism**: cell-wall synthesis inhibition plus reactive nitrogen species generation.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Inhibition of RNA Polymerase Beta-Subunit (rpoB)**
This is the mechanism of **rifampicin**, not pretomanid.
A different drug class and target entirely.

**C. Inhibition of Mycobacterial ATP Synthase**
This is the mechanism of **bedaquiline**, the other novel drug often combined with pretomanid in modern regimens.
Distinct mechanism from pretomanid.

**D. Inhibition of DNA Gyrase (Topoisomerase II)**
This is the mechanism of **fluoroquinolones** (such as moxifloxacin, sometimes used alongside these regimens).
Not the mechanism of pretomanid itself.

## Mind Capsule

> BPaL(M) regimen: Bedaquiline (ATP synthase), Pretomanid (mycolic acid synthesis + RNS), Linezolid (protein synthesis), +/- Moxifloxacin (DNA gyrase).
