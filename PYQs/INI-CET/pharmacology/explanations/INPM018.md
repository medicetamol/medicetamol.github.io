# Aminoglycosides

**Aminoglycosides** show **concentration-dependent killing**: efficacy correlates with the **peak concentration relative to the MIC**, along with a significant **post-antibiotic effect**, which is why they are dosed as a single large daily dose rather than divided doses.

## Trigger Point

> **Concentration-dependent killing**: bactericidal activity that rises with higher peak drug levels.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Beta-Lactams (Penicillins)**
Show **time-dependent killing**, where efficacy depends on the **duration** the concentration stays above the MIC, not the peak level.
A different pharmacodynamic pattern from aminoglycosides.

**C. Glycopeptides (Vancomycin)**
Generally show **time-dependent** killing (often described by the AUC/MIC ratio), not classic concentration-dependence like aminoglycosides.
Different dosing strategy from aminoglycosides.

**D. Macrolides (Erythromycin)**
Typically **time-dependent** with a post-antibiotic effect, but not classified with the concentration-dependent aminoglycoside/fluoroquinolone group.
Different killing pattern from aminoglycosides.

## Mind Capsule

> Concentration-dependent killers: aminoglycosides, fluoroquinolones (dose high, once daily); time-dependent: beta-lactams (keep above MIC).
