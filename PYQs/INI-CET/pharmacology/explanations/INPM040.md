# Ceftriaxone

*Neisseria gonorrhoeae* has widely developed resistance to **penicillins**, tetracyclines and fluoroquinolones. A **third-generation cephalosporin, ceftriaxone**, remains the mainstay of treatment, including for complicated and disseminated infection.

## Trigger Point

> Complicated gonorrhoea caused by penicillin-resistant *N. gonorrhoeae*.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Amoxicillin**
A penicillin; resistant strains by definition will not respond.

**C. Tetracycline**
Resistance is widespread, so it is no longer recommended for gonorrhoea.

**D. Vancomycin**
Active against Gram-positive organisms and has no role in Gram-negative diplococcal infection.

## Mind Capsule

> Gonorrhoea = ceftriaxone (plus coverage for chlamydia per local protocol); penicillins and quinolones are no longer reliable.
