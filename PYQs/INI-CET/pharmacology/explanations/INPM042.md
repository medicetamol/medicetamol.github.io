# Both Have Anti-muscarinic Actions

**Imipramine** (a tricyclic antidepressant) and **diphenhydramine** (a first-generation H₁ blocker) both block **muscarinic receptors**. Together, they have additive anticholinergic effects such as dry mouth, constipation, urinary retention, blurred vision and confusion.

## Trigger Point

> Imipramine given along with diphenhydramine.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. Diphenhydramine inhibits action of imipramine**
No pharmacodynamic antagonism; their anticholinergic effects add up.

**B. Imipramine reduces diphenhydramine elimination**
Not the described interaction, which is a pharmacodynamic one.

**D. Both inhibit norepinephrine reuptake**
Only imipramine does this; diphenhydramine is mainly an H₁ antagonist.

## Mind Capsule

> TCA + first-generation antihistamine = additive anticholinergic load.
