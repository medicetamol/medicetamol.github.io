# Cardiovascular Status (Echocardiography)

**Fenfluramine**, used as adjunctive therapy in **Dravet syndrome**, has structural similarity to older anorectic fenfluramine formulations historically linked to **cardiac valvulopathy and pulmonary hypertension**. Patients on fenfluramine therefore require **baseline and periodic echocardiographic monitoring**.

## Drug Facts

| Drug | Use | Key monitoring/adverse effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Fenfluramine** | Dravet syndrome (seizure control) | **Cardiac valvulopathy, pulmonary hypertension** (needs echo monitoring) |

## Trigger Point

> **Fenfluramine in Dravet syndrome**: the specific monitoring requirement relates to the heart.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Agranulocytosis**
A monitored adverse effect of drugs like clozapine or carbimazole, not the characteristic concern with fenfluramine.
Not the specific, well-known risk here.

**C. Hepatic Failure**
A concern with drugs like valproate or felbamate, not the defining risk profile of fenfluramine.
Not the specifically tested monitoring requirement.

**D. Renal Failure**
Not a recognised characteristic toxicity of fenfluramine.
Cardiac, not renal, monitoring is the key concern.

## Mind Capsule

> Fenfluramine in Dravet syndrome = monitor for cardiac valvulopathy/pulmonary hypertension with echocardiography.
