# Naloxone

**Naloxone** is a competitive **opioid receptor (mu) antagonist** and the specific antidote for acute opioid toxicity, rapidly reversing **respiratory depression, pinpoint (miotic) pupils, and depressed consciousness**. Its short half-life may require repeat dosing or an infusion for long-acting opioids.

## Trigger Point

> **Pinpoint pupils, respiratory depression, reduced consciousness after opioid overdose**: classic triad reversed by naloxone.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Flumazenil**
The specific antagonist for **benzodiazepine** overdose, acting at the GABA-A/benzodiazepine receptor complex.
Not effective for opioid toxicity.

**C. Atropine**
The antidote for **muscarinic (cholinergic) excess**, as in organophosphate poisoning.
Does not reverse opioid receptor effects.

**D. N-Acetylcysteine**
The antidote for **paracetamol (acetaminophen)** overdose, replenishing glutathione.
Unrelated to opioid toxicity.

## Mind Capsule

> Opioid toxicity triad (miosis, respiratory depression, altered consciousness) = naloxone.
