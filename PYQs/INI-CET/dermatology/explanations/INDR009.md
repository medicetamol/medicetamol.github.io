# Skin Biopsy

A **painless black necrotic eschar with surrounding oedema** suggests a **malignant pustule (cutaneous anthrax)**. **Skin biopsy** of an unexplained necrotic lesion gives a definitive diagnosis by histology and culture. Note that a **Gram stain** of the lesion also shows large Gram-positive bacilli quickly, so the preferred test can depend on the textbook followed.

## Trigger Point

> **Painless black necrotic lesion** over the heel with **surrounding oedema**.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. KOH smear**
Used for superficial fungal infections (dermatophytes, candida), not a deep necrotic eschar.

**C. Gram stain**
A rapid presumptive test (large Gram-positive bacilli), but less definitive, and it can be negative after antibiotics.

**D. Tzanck smear**
Shows multinucleated giant cells in herpes and varicella blisters, not necrosis.

## Mind Capsule

> **Painless black eschar + marked oedema** = think **cutaneous anthrax**.
