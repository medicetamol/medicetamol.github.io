# Biopsy and Chest X-ray

An **asymptomatic** annular lesion argues against tinea corporis, which is usually itchy, so a **KOH mount** is not the key test. An annular scaly lesion without symptoms raises the possibility of **granulomatous conditions such as sarcoidosis**, so **biopsy** (to look for granulomas) and **chest X-ray** (for hilar lymphadenopathy) are done.

## Trigger Point

> Asymptomatic ring-shaped scaly lesion on the chest.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**A. 1, 2, 4**
Includes HIV testing and KOH mount, which are not the key tests here, and omits biopsy.

**B. 2, 3**
KOH mount is for itchy fungal lesions, and the chest X-ray is omitted.

**D. 1, 2, 3, 4**
Over-investigates; HIV testing and KOH mount are not indicated for this presentation.

## Mind Capsule

> Asymptomatic annular lesion = think sarcoidosis: biopsy and chest X-ray.
