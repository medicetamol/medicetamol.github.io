# Herpes Simplex Virus

**HSV (usually type 2, increasingly type 1)** causes genital herpes: **painful grouped vesicles on an erythematous base** that erode into shallow ulcers, often with fever, malaise and tender inguinal nodes in the primary episode. Diagnosis is clinical, supported by **PCR** (Tzanck smear shows multinucleated giant cells).

## Trigger Point

> **Painful grouped vesicles** over the genitals with **fever and malaise**.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Haemophilus ducreyi**
Causes chancroid: a painful, soft, ragged ulcer with undermined edges and suppurative buboes.
It starts as a pustule, not grouped vesicles.

**C. Human Papillomavirus**
Causes painless genital warts (condylomata acuminata).
No vesicles, pain or systemic upset.

**D. Treponema pallidum**
Causes primary syphilis: a **painless**, indurated, single chancre.
Painless and non-vesicular.

## Mind Capsule

> Grouped vesicles + pain + fever = HSV; painless indurated ulcer = syphilis.

## Memory Hook

> **Painful vs painless genital ulcer**: painful = HSV and chancroid; painless = syphilis, LGV and donovanosis.
