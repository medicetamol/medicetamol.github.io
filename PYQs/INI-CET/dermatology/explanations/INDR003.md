# Biopsy

An **irregular black pigmented lesion on the heel or sole** is **acral lentiginous melanoma** until proven otherwise. Diagnosis is **histopathological**, and the biopsy (ideally full-thickness excision with a narrow margin) also gives **Breslow thickness**, which decides staging and the width of wide excision.

## Key Finding

> Irregular black pigmented lesion on the heel/sole: the acral melanoma pattern.

## Trigger Point

> **Investigation of choice to establish the diagnosis** of a pigmented acral lesion.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. KOH Mount**
Detects fungal hyphae and spores in scrapings (dermatophytosis, tinea).
It has no role in a pigmented tumour.

**C. Gram Stain**
Identifies bacteria in infective lesions.
A melanoma is not diagnosed by microbiology.

**D. Tzanck Smear**
Shows multinucleated giant cells in herpes infections and acantholytic cells in pemphigus.
It does not diagnose a melanocytic tumour.

## Mind Capsule

> Suspicious pigmented lesion = biopsy (histology and Breslow thickness), never curettage or shave.
