# Neonatal Pemphigus

A transient autoimmune blistering disorder caused by **passive transplacental transfer of maternal IgG** against desmogleins (mainly **Dsg3**). The newborn makes no antibodies; the maternal IgG disrupts keratinocyte adhesion and clears with time.

## Trigger Point

> Mother has an autoimmune blistering disorder **+** neonate blisters in the first days of life.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. SSSS**
Caused by *S. aureus* exfoliative toxin, giving superficial epidermal splitting.
It presents with an infectious picture, and maternal autoimmunity doesn't explain it.

**C. Bullous Pemphigoid**
Autoimmune subepidermal disease with antibodies against BP180/BP230.
A different mechanism, and not a neonatal condition.

**D. Epidermolysis Bullosa**
Genetic defect in structural skin proteins causing mechanical blistering.
Not explained by maternal antibodies.

## Mind Capsule

> Neonate + blisters + mother with pemphigus = neonatal pemphigus (maternal IgG, transient).
