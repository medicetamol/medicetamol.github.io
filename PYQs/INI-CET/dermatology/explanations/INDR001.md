# Neonatal Pemphigus

## Key Concept

**Neonatal pemphigus** is a rare, transient autoimmune blistering disorder caused by **passive transplacental transfer of maternal IgG autoantibodies**, classically directed against desmogleins (especially **Dsg3**).

The newborn does not produce these antibodies. Maternal IgG crosses the placenta and temporarily disrupts keratinocyte adhesion, producing flaccid bullae and erosions. The condition is generally transient, as the maternally derived antibodies are cleared over time.

## Strongest Clue

> Mother has an autoimmune blistering disorder **+** neonate develops blisters within the first days of life.

This strongly points toward **passive transfer of maternal pathogenic antibodies**.

**Therefore → A. Neonatal pemphigus from transplacental transfer of maternal antibodies.**

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. SSSS (Staphylococcal Scalded Skin Syndrome)**
Caused by *Staphylococcus aureus* exfoliative toxins causing superficial epidermal splitting. Typically presents with an infectious picture; maternal autoimmune history would not explain it.

**C. Bullous Pemphigoid (BP)**
BP is an autoimmune subepidermal blistering disease involving antibodies against basement-membrane proteins such as **BP180/BP230** — a distinct mechanism from neonatal pemphigus.

**D. Epidermolysis Bullosa (EB)**
EB is a group of genetic/mechanically induced blistering disorders caused by defects in structural skin proteins. Not explained by maternal autoimmune antibodies.

## Points to Ponder

> **Neonate + blistering + mother with pemphigus/autoimmune blistering disease = Neonatal pemphigus due to transplacental maternal IgG.**
