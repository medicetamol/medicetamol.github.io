# Excisional Biopsy with Histopathology

An irregular black lesion on the heel or sole raises the suspicion of **acral lentiginous melanoma**. Confirmation needs **tissue diagnosis**: a complete (excisional, full-thickness) biopsy with a narrow margin, which allows measurement of **Breslow thickness** for staging. Shave biopsy or curettage should be avoided because they can transect the lesion and miss depth.

## Trigger Point

> **Irregular black pigmented lesion on heel/sole suspicious for acral melanoma**: the confirmatory test is histopathology.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. KOH Mount / Gram Stain**
KOH detects fungi and Gram stain detects bacteria.
Neither identifies a melanocytic tumour.

**C. Tzanck Smear**
Reveals multinucleated giant cells (herpes) or acantholytic cells (pemphigus).
Not useful for a pigmented lesion.

**D. Wood's Lamp Examination**
Uses UV light to show fluorescence in conditions such as tinea capitis, erythrasma and vitiligo.
It cannot confirm melanoma.

## Mind Capsule

> Suspected melanoma = excisional biopsy with histopathology (Breslow thickness decides management).
