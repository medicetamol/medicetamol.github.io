# Oral Antifungals

**Tinea capitis** infects the hair shaft and follicle, where topical drugs do not reach adequate levels, so **systemic (oral) antifungals** are required: **griseofulvin** is the traditional drug and **terbinafine** is an effective alternative (especially for *Trichophyton*). An antifungal shampoo (ketoconazole or selenium sulfide) is an adjunct to reduce spore shedding.

## Key Finding

> Scalp lesion on the clinical photograph consistent with tinea capitis.

## Trigger Point

> **Tinea capitis**, a hair-follicle dermatophyte infection: treatment of choice is systemic therapy.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Antibiotics**
Act against bacteria and have no effect on a dermatophyte infection.
They are used only for secondary bacterial infection.

**C. Methotrexate**
An immunosuppressant used in psoriasis and other inflammatory disease.
It would worsen a fungal infection.

**D. Topical Steroids**
They suppress inflammation and alter the appearance of the lesion (tinea incognito) while allowing the fungus to spread.
They do not treat the cause.

## Mind Capsule

> Tinea capitis (hair-shaft infection) = oral antifungal (griseofulvin or terbinafine); topical alone fails.
