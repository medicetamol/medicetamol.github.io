# Need for First-Trimester Medical Termination of Pregnancy

**Monochorionic diamniotic (MCDA) twins** share a placenta with vascular anastomoses, predisposing to **twin-twin transfusion syndrome, selective growth restriction, and (rarely) congenital anomalies/deformities** from vascular compromise or cord entanglement in monoamniotic cases. **MCDA twinning itself is not an indication for first-trimester MTP**; it is managed with close surveillance.

## Trigger Point

> **NOT a complication** of MCDA twin pregnancy: the option describing a management decision rather than a pregnancy complication.

## Why the Others Are True

**B. Selective Fetal Growth Restriction (sFGR)**
Unequal placental sharing in a monochorionic placenta commonly causes discordant growth between twins.
A genuine, well-recognised complication.

**C. Congenital Deformities**
Vascular events (including from a co-twin demise) can cause ischaemic damage and structural anomalies in the survivor.
A recognised complication of monochorionic pregnancies.

**D. Cord Entanglement**
This is a well-known complication classically of **monoamniotic** twins (sharing one amniotic sac); it can occur when framed within the MCDA/monochorionic spectrum in some exam contexts, but it is not the deliberately excluded option here.
It is listed as a true complication in this question, unlike routine first-trimester MTP.

## Mind Capsule

> Monochorionic twins share circulation = risk of TTTS, sFGR and anomalies; the diagnosis itself is not an indication for termination.
