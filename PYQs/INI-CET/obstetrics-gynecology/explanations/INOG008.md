# Androgen Insensitivity Syndrome

**Complete androgen insensitivity syndrome (46,XY)** presents as a **phenotypic female** with primary amenorrhoea, an **absent uterus** (Mullerian structures regress due to AMH), **normal or well-developed breasts** (from peripheral aromatization of androgens to oestrogen), and **scanty or absent pubic/axillary hair** (androgen receptors are non-functional).

## Trigger Point

> **Absent uterus + normal breasts + sparse pubic/axillary hair**: the classic triad of complete AIS.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. MRKH Syndrome**
Also causes primary amenorrhoea with an absent uterus, but the karyotype is **46,XX**, ovaries are normal, and pubic/axillary hair is **normal** (since androgen action is intact).
The hair pattern here does not fit MRKH.

**C. Turner Syndrome (45,XO)**
Presents with a **uterus present** (though often hypoplastic), streak ovaries, short stature and other somatic features.
The uterus is typically present, unlike in this case.

**D. Kallmann Syndrome**
Hypogonadotropic hypogonadism with anosmia; the uterus is present but underdeveloped due to lack of oestrogen, and breast development is usually poor (not well-developed).
Breast development does not fit, since oestrogen is deficient here.

## Mind Capsule

> Absent uterus + normal breasts + no pubic/axillary hair = complete androgen insensitivity syndrome (46,XY).
