# Low-Dose Aspirin from 12 Weeks

In women at **high risk of pre-eclampsia** (such as a past history of it), **low-dose aspirin (75 to 150 mg) started from around 12 weeks of gestation** (and continued until delivery or 36 weeks) reduces the risk of recurrent pre-eclampsia by improving trophoblastic invasion and placental perfusion.

## Trigger Point

> **Best method to prevent pre-eclampsia** in a high-risk woman: low-dose aspirin from early pregnancy.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. Aspirin Combined with LMWH**
Adding LMWH has **not** been shown to add benefit over aspirin alone for pre-eclampsia prevention in most trials, and it is reserved for specific thrombophilia indications.
It is not the standard recommended regimen.

**C. Strict Salt Restriction**
Not shown to prevent pre-eclampsia and is not part of current recommendations.
Excessive restriction can even be counterproductive.

**D. Calcium Supplementation**
Beneficial mainly in populations with **low dietary calcium intake**, and evidence is less robust than for aspirin in high-risk women generally.
Not the single best answer compared with aspirin.

## Mind Capsule

> High-risk of pre-eclampsia = low-dose aspirin from ~12 weeks, continued through pregnancy.
