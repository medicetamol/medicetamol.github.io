# Asherman Syndrome - Intrauterine Adhesions

**Asherman syndrome** follows vigorous uterine curettage (often post-partum or post-abortion), causing **intrauterine adhesions/endometrial scarring**, presenting as secondary amenorrhoea or hypomenorrhoea, typically with **normal hormone levels** since the ovary and hypothalamic-pituitary axis are intact.

## Correct Matches

| Cause | Correct mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Asherman syndrome** | **Intrauterine adhesions after curettage** |
| PCOD | Hyperandrogenism with LH/FSH imbalance (usually raised LH, not hypogonadotropic) |
| Sheehan syndrome | Postpartum pituitary necrosis (hypopituitarism), not primary ovarian failure |
| Premature ovarian failure | Primary ovarian failure with raised (not normal) FSH, not outflow obstruction |

## Trigger Point

> **Endometrial scarring after curettage** causing amenorrhoea: the classic description of Asherman syndrome.

## Why Not the Other Options?

**B. PCOD - Hypogonadotropic Hypogonadism with Low LH**
PCOD is characterised by an **elevated LH:FSH ratio**, not hypogonadotropic hypogonadism.
This mechanism is mismatched.

**C. Sheehan Syndrome - Primary Ovarian Failure**
Sheehan syndrome is **postpartum pituitary infarction** causing panhypopituitarism, a central, not ovarian, cause.
The mechanism described is wrong.

**D. POF - Outflow Tract Obstruction with Normal FSH**
Premature ovarian failure is an **ovarian** cause with **raised FSH** due to loss of negative feedback, not an outflow obstruction.
Both the mechanism and FSH level are incorrect.

## Mind Capsule

> Amenorrhoea after curettage = Asherman syndrome (intrauterine adhesions, normal hormones).
